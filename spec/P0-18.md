## 1. What this delivers

Expand it: the saved draft of a floor (or of all floors stacked, with a "Show up to" cut) as floors and walls with doors cut through, orbited with the mouse. In OA-6a, "All floors" draws floors, plates and walls; items and connectors arrive in OA-6b. The cross-cutting criteria (AC-18 to AC-22) apply to what each sub-slice ships.

**Needs:**
- OA-6a: OA-4a-1 merged (the editor's view model with the dock slot, the saved-document stream of X1, the finish tokens of P0-11 AC-77).
- OA-6b: OA-4a-2 (the catalog read, items and connectors in the editor), OA-4b-1 (the Stores panel's list of stores, which the labels read, X1) and OA-3 (the seeded malls for the manual checks).
- Neither needs FF-3′ or OA-4c.

**What is built (DC-2 and DC-17 say why):**
- A pure Dart **scene builder**: the saved document, the catalog and the view choices in; a plain **scene description** out (world-space numbers, resolved colours as 32-bit integers, no Dart objects). It is where nearly all of the logic sits, and it is unit-testable on the VM.
- One small hand-written JavaScript **adapter** (`app/web/preview3d/`), the only code that calls three.js. It builds the three.js scene from the description, owns the camera, the draw loop and the label layer (through three.js's own label add-on, AC-26), and holds no document logic and no colour.
- A Dart **port** (an interface) with two implementations: a web one (a platform view and `dart:js_interop`, E-23) and a stub for tests and for the Android and iOS builds, so nothing web-only is compiled into the shopper app.
- The dock widget, its view model, its constants file, its ARB strings, its tokens.
- The vendored three.js files in `app/web/vendor/three/` with their pin: the main module, and the one label add-on E-25 now covers (AC-26).

## 2. Terms

- **"The saved draft"** is the venue map draft as the server last stored it, as the editor knows it: the draft loaded at open, then the document of each save the server answered 200 (DC-1). **"The editor"** is P0-11 r3's. **"The dock"** is the preview's bar and drawing area. **"The scene description"**, **"the adapter"** and **"the port"** are as §1 says.
- **"A level"**, **"a unit"**, **"an item"**, **"a connector"** are the document's (OA-1). **"The stack"** is all levels of the saved draft. **"The cut"** is AC-3's "Show up to" choice.
- **"The caps"** are the `VenueMapConstants` generated from `venue-map-schema.json`, as OA-3 AC-23 raises them to twice the measured malls. This spec writes no cap's number (they move).
- **"The palette"** is the table of resolved colours the Dart side sends the adapter: a finish key or a role name in, a 32-bit ARGB integer out.
- **"A label"** is a short text drawn over the picture beside what it names: a floor's name, or a place's name (AC-23). **"The label layer"** is the add-on's layer of such labels. **"The stores list"** is the venue's stores as the editor's Stores panel holds them (OA-4b-1, X1).
- **"The tokens"** are the colours in `app/lib/core/theme/gabay_tokens.dart` and `Theme.of(context).colorScheme` (rule 6).

## 3. Acceptance criteria

### 3.1 Before code, the dock, the modes

- AC-1 [OA-6a]: **The facts this spec marks RECALLED are checked first.** Before any code that uses three.js or the interop, the plan's step 0 checks each item of §8's table against the pinned package and the pinned Flutter and Dart SDKs (doc fetch or source read), and records the result in the slice report. A claim that proves false stops the slice and comes back here as a revision; it is not worked around. Verify: review (the report's step-0 section, item by item).
- AC-2 [OA-6a]: **The dock.**
  - It sits under the 2D canvas, in the slot P0-11 r3 §1 reserves. It is **collapsed on every editor open**: only its bar shows, with the title "3D preview", an expand button, the status text of AC-4 and no drawing surface.
  - Expanded, it has a default height and a drag handle on its top edge. Dragging the handle, or focusing it and pressing Up or Down (a step) and Home or End (the minimum or maximum), resizes it within a minimum and a maximum. The 2D canvas keeps at least its own minimum height. The height lasts while the editor stays open; it is saved nowhere (DC-5).
  - Resizing changes the drawing's size only. It rebuilds no scene and keeps the camera (AC-5, AC-12).
  - **Nothing of three.js is fetched while the dock has never been expanded.** The first expansion starts the load of AC-15 and AC-16; later collapses and expansions never reload it (DC-10).
  - On a narrow window (the breakpoint of P0-11 AC-135) the bar opens the dock as a sheet over the editor instead of under the canvas (DC-14).

  Every height, minimum and maximum is a named constant in the preview's one constants file; the plan sets the values (DC-4). Verify: widget test (collapsed at open; toggle; drag and keys clamp; the 2D canvas's minimum; the port's load called once, on first expansion only; the sheet on a narrow window); manual case (a real drag in a browser).
- AC-3 [OA-6a]: **Two modes, and the cut.**
  - **"This floor"** (the default, on each editor open) draws the level the editor has active, and follows it: choosing another floor tab in the editor changes the preview with it, and asks nothing. If the active level is not in the saved draft yet (added since the last save), the dock says "This floor is not saved yet" and draws nothing until the save lands.
  - **"All floors"** draws every level of the saved draft at its own `elevation` in the venue frame. A **"Show up to"** list, highest elevation first and named as the editor's floor tabs are (short name, full name as the accessible name), defaults to the highest level. Choosing a level hides every level whose `elevation` is above it, and keeps the levels at equal elevation (DC-9). The list is not shown in "This floor".
  - The mode and the cut last while the editor stays open. Changing either re-fits the camera (AC-12).

  Verify: unit test (the level selection for each mode and cut, on fixtures of two buildings with levels at equal elevations, negative elevations and an outdoor level); widget test (the controls; the follow; the not-saved state); manual case.
- AC-4 [OA-6a]: **The saved draft only (re-plan Q10).**
  - The preview draws the saved draft (§2). It never draws an unsaved edit: not while dragging or typing, not between a change and its save, not after an undo before the save lands, not after a failed save.
  - The bar's status text says, in words and never by colour alone, "Saved version N". While the editor's save state is dirty, saving, error or conflict, it adds "Unsaved changes are not shown". In conflict it also says that someone else changed the map and that Reload shows theirs.
  - When a save is answered 200, the saved draft becomes that document and the preview updates (AC-5). A Reload (after a conflict), a Restore, a Clear and an Import each replace the saved draft when their own save or load lands; a Clear leaves no draft.
  - With **no draft** (a venue with no map, or after a Clear) the dock says there is nothing to show yet and creates no drawing surface.
  - The preview reads the saved draft and its version through the editor's view model (X1) and makes no request of its own (AC-20).
  - The picture is the saved draft; the **names on the labels** (AC-23) are the exception that proves the rule: a store's name is not in the draft, it is a server row saved at once and apart from the draft (P0-11 AC-69, AC-113), so a label shows the name the server holds, never a half-typed form value.

  Verify: widget test with a fake editor view model (an edit leaves the scene unchanged; a 200 updates it; a failed save keeps the old; the conflict text; the draft shown at open; no draft); unit test (the status text for each save state); manual case (two tabs).
- AC-5 [OA-6a]: **A new saved draft does not cost the admin their view.**
  - A new saved draft rebuilds the scene. One rebuild runs at a time; a newer saved draft that lands during a rebuild replaces any waiting one, so the last save always wins and at most one waits.
  - The camera, the mode, the cut, the dock's height and the selection highlight are kept. The old scene stays on screen until the new one is ready, so there is no blank frame, and the scene is swapped whole, never partly.
  - A collapsed dock keeps the latest saved draft and builds only when expanded.
  - Opening another venue in the editor resets everything (AC-13).

  Verify: widget test with a fake port (rebuild counts; latest wins; no build while collapsed; the camera, mode and cut kept); unit test (the builder is a pure function: the same inputs give an equal description).

### 3.2 What is drawn

- AC-6 [OA-6a]: **One frame.** A single projection module maps the document's frame to the scene's, in metres with no scaling: world X is `x`, world Z is `z`, and world Y is the level's `elevation` plus the height above its floor. Plan "up" (−z) is the far side in the default view. A `rot` of 0 faces up the plan (−z) and a positive `rot` turns clockwise seen from above (office §3). The site's and a building's `mapUp` are **not** applied: the preview is in the venue's frame, as the 2D editor is (P0-11 AC-60). The Dart side resolves everything to world coordinates and to a yaw in the adapter's convention, so the adapter does no document logic. Verify: unit test (a round trip of the four axis directions; a box's facing for `rot` 0, 90, 180 and 270; elevation −20 and 300; a 9 × 9 m unit measures 9 × 9 in world units), unit test in Node (a box yawed by the builder's value, built with the pinned three.js, has its facing edge where the convention says: this pins three.js's rotation sign, which is RECALLED, §8).
- AC-7 [OA-6a]: **Floors (the look is Q3 (a), ruled: document-faithful).**
  - Each drawn level has a thin slab at its `elevation`, coloured by its `floor.finish`. The slab's outline is the footprint of the level's building when the building has one, the site boundary for the outdoor level, and otherwise the level's rectangle (DC-6). Ceilings are never drawn.
  - Each unit is a thin plate over its polygon, a little above the slab, coloured by its own `floor.finish` or the level's. A walkable and a non-walkable unit look alike; the walls tell them apart. No tint by category (Q3 (a)).
  - Concave polygons are drawn. A polygon that cannot be drawn (fewer than three distinct points, a self-crossing ring, a number that is not finite) is skipped and counted, never an error and never a hang.
  - Two slabs at one elevation with the same outline are drawn once; levels that overlap in plan at the same elevation do not flicker (the plan names the tie-break).
  - The count of things that could not be drawn is shown in the bar's status ("N shapes could not be drawn").

  Verify: unit test (the outline rule; the finish resolution; the skip rules; the count), Node test (a concave unit, a bow-tie, a repeated point, a ring with `NaN`), manual case (the look, against the 2D editor).
- AC-8 [OA-6a]: **Walls and openings.**
  - Each wall is a box from `a` to `b`, `t` thick, as high as its level's `height`, standing on the slab. Its kind picks its palette entry: `solid`, `partition`, `glass` (translucent by a named opacity). An unknown kind is drawn as `solid`.
  - Each opening cuts a gap in its wall: its span is `at` ± `w`/2 along the wall's length, clipped to the wall's ends. A door's gap runs from the floor to the door height (the smaller of a named constant and the level's height). A window's runs from a named sill to a named head, clipped to the level's height. The wall above and below a gap stays. Overlapping gaps merge. An opening whose wall is not in the saved draft is skipped and counted.
  - Corners are not mitred (DC-8).

  Verify: unit test (splitting a wall at one, two and overlapping openings; clipping at the ends; a door taller than its level; each kind; the skip), Node test (the meshes of one wall with and without a gap), manual case.
- AC-9 [OA-6b]: **Items, as catalog primitives (office §5's design note).**
  - Each item stands on its floor at (`x`, `z`) with yaw `rot`. Its size is the item's own `w`, `d` and `h`, else its type's catalog default. A round type is a cylinder of diameter `w` (`d` follows `w`, OA-1 §2.5.4); anything else is a box. Its finish key picks its palette entry.
  - A type the catalog does not hold, or whose size cannot be resolved, is a plain box of a named fallback size. A type that is **inactive** but present in the catalog is drawn by its own row (§5 item 15). A wall-mounted type still stands on the floor (DC-7).
  - Items are drawn **instanced**: draw calls depend on the primitive kinds and the levels, never on how many items there are (AC-17).

  Verify: unit test (size resolution: own, type, fallback; round; unknown type; an inactive type held by the catalog fixture and one the catalog does not hold), Node test (5,000 items give the same number of meshes as 50), manual case.
- AC-10 [OA-6b]: **Connectors.**
  - An **elevator** is a box (`w` × `d`) over its footprint from the lowest shown level it serves to the top of the highest. **Stairs**, an **escalator** and a **ramp** are an inclined solid (`len` along its facing, `w` across) rising between consecutive shown levels it serves, in order of elevation; a connector serving N shown levels gives N − 1 solids (an escalator serves exactly two). Each kind has its palette entry.
  - In "This floor" a connector is its footprint on that level: for an elevator a block as high as the level, for the others a flat plate.
  - Under the cut, a connector is drawn only between the levels that are shown.
  - `walkable`, `twoWay`, `preferred`, `evacuationOnly`, `cars`, `shape` and `review` do not change the drawing (DC-8). An unknown kind is not drawn and is counted.

  Verify: unit test (each kind; stairs over three levels; an elevator over three; the cut; This floor), Node test, manual case.
- AC-11 [OA-6b]: **Selection is shown, never made (preview only, office §8.1).**
  - The editor's selected walls, units, items and connectors that also exist in the saved draft, under the same id and kind, are drawn in the highlight colour. A selected element that is not in the saved draft yet, and every other kind of selection, is ignored.
  - The highlight follows the selection without a scene rebuild, and adds no mesh per selected element.
  - Nothing in the dock can be picked, dragged or edited. A pointer in the drawing moves the camera only.

  Verify: widget test (the port receives only the ids found in the saved draft); unit test (the filter); Node test (a recolour changes no mesh count); manual case.

### 3.3 Camera, drawing, colours

- AC-12 [OA-6a]: **Camera and keys (own controls, DC-3).**
  - **Mouse:** a left drag orbits around the target, a right or middle drag pans, the wheel zooms. The wheel never scrolls the page, and the browser's right-click menu is not shown over the drawing.
  - **The first view** looks toward plan-up from the south, tilted, with the target at the centre of the drawn geometry and everything in view. Buttons: Reset view, Top view (straight down, plan-up at the top of the dock, as in 2D), Zoom in, Zoom out, Rotate left, Rotate right.
  - **Limits:** the pitch may go from straight down to just above straight up (a view from below is allowed; plates are drawn on both faces). The distance has a minimum and a maximum taken from the bounds, and the near and far planes are derived from the bounds, so a 320 m tall stack (elevation −20 to 300, plus height) and a site 1,300 m wide both fit. The numbers are named constants (DC-4).
  - **Re-fit:** a mode change, a cut change and a new venue re-fit. In "This floor", changing floor moves the target's height with the level and keeps angle and distance.
  - **Keyboard:** the drawing and the controls are reachable with Tab, with a visible focus. With focus in the dock: the arrow keys orbit, `+` and `−` zoom, `0` resets. The dock lists its own keys. **While the dock has focus, the editor's shortcuts (tool keys, Delete, Backspace, `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+D`, the arrow-key nudge) do nothing**, the rule P0-11 AC-87 states for a text field (X1); a Delete pressed in the preview never deletes the selected element.
  - **No animation:** the view jumps; no easing exists, so reduced motion needs nothing. Touch gestures are not supported (PRD §3: a desktop browser).

  Verify: unit test in Node (fit, orbit, pan and zoom deltas, the pitch and distance limits, near and far for the tallest and the widest cases); widget test (the buttons; the keys; the editor's shortcuts silent while the dock has focus); manual case (a real mouse: orbit, pan, wheel without a page scroll, no context menu).
- AC-13 [OA-6a]: **Drawing loop and lifecycle.**
  - **On demand:** a frame is drawn only when the scene, the camera, the palette, the highlight or the size changed. Nothing is drawn while idle, while the dock is collapsed, or while the browser tab is hidden. A zero-sized surface draws nothing and raises no error.
  - **Size:** the surface follows the dock; the device pixel ratio is read again on a resize and capped by a named constant. The light is fixed (an ambient and one directional light); no shadows, no post-processing.
  - **Disposal:** leaving the editor (another module, another venue, another tenant in the switcher) disposes every geometry, material, texture and the renderer, loses the graphics context explicitly, **removes the label layer and every label element**, and drops the scene description and the store names it held. After that, no part of the previous venue's scene or names is held in Dart or in the adapter (tenant boundary). A failed mount disposes what it made.
  - **The label pass** (AC-24) runs inside the same on-demand frame, not in a loop of its own.

  Verify: unit test in Node with a fake renderer (frames only when dirty; none after collapse, hide or dispose; every created resource and every label element disposed); widget test (the port is told to pause on collapse and to dispose on a route change and a tenant switch); manual case (open and close the dock 20 times: no "too many contexts" warning; an idle expanded dock shows no GPU work in the browser's tools).
- AC-14 [OA-6a]: **Finishes are keys; colours come from the tokens (rule 6; E-25).**
  - The document holds finish keys only. The Dart side maps each `FloorFinish` and `ItemFinish` key to the **same token P0-11 r3 AC-77 gives the 2D editor**, so a finish looks the same in both. The other colours the preview needs (the background, `solid`, `partition` and `glass` walls, each connector kind, the highlight, the slab edge if one is drawn, and the **labels'** text and backing colours, AC-25) are **new tokens**, light and dark, in `gabay_tokens.dart` or `colorScheme`, named in the plan. The labels' colours are sent as CSS colour strings made in Dart from the tokens, so the adapter only assigns them.
  - Each is resolved to a 32-bit ARGB integer for the current brightness and sent in the palette. **The adapter holds no colour literal and no default colour**: a palette that lacks an entry it needs is refused as a bad scene (AC-16), never drawn grey. A finish key with no token falls back to the `default` key's colour; a test fails when any current key lacks its token.
  - A theme change sends a new palette and **no geometry**; the scene recolours and does not rebuild.
  - Numbers that are not colours (the glass opacity, light intensity) are named constants.
  - `colour-literals` scans Dart under `app/lib/` only, so a second check covers the adapter: a scan test fails on a hex colour, a `0x` six-digit number, `rgb(`, `hsl(` or a CSS colour name in `app/web/preview3d/`. `app/web/vendor/` is exempt.
  - Colour management: a token must show on screen as itself (three.js's own colour handling is RECALLED, §8); the plan's step 0 settles how the integer reaches three.js, and a manual pixel check confirms it.

  Verify: unit test (every key of both sets, and every new token, resolves in both themes; the fallback; the palette is complete), tools or Node test (the adapter scan), widget test (a theme switch sends a palette and no scene), `npm run verify` (`colour-literals`), manual case (a wall's colour on screen equals its token).

### 3.4 Self-hosting, failure, performance

- AC-15 [OA-6a]: **Self-hosted, pinned, admin only (E-25).**
  - three.js 0.186.1 is served by the admin build itself, from `app/web/vendor/three/` (the plan fixes the file names after step 0; DC-13), copied byte for byte from the pinned package with its licence file, and loaded relative to the page's base href. The folder holds the main module and **the one add-on of AC-26 (the label renderer), and no other file**. **No CDN, no outside host, no URL with a scheme and a host for any preview file.**
  - One manifest holds the pin: the version and the SHA-256 of each vendored file (the add-on included) and of the adapter. A tools test fails when a file's hash differs, a file is missing or an extra file sits in the folder, or when any HTML, JavaScript or Dart file under `app/` names an outside host for the preview. The vendored folder is excluded from ESLint and Prettier so that a format pass cannot change it (EC-30).
  - Blueprint Part 1's pins table gains the row (three.js 0.186.1, MIT). The adapter and the vendored files are requested with one version query, so an old cached file never pairs with a new one (EC-3).
  - It is **never in the shopper app**: the code that uses the interop sits behind a conditional import and its stub, in the admin feature's folder; the Android and iOS builds compile without it; a scan test fails if any file outside the admin feature, other than the port's interface, imports it.

  Verify: tools test (hashes; no outside host; no extra file), unit test (the scan), `flutter analyze` and the stub build, review (Blueprint, E-25's text), manual case (the browser's network panel shows only same-origin requests).
- AC-16 [OA-6a]: **When it cannot start or stops, the dock says so and the editor carries on.**
  - **The module cannot load** (the file is missing or blocked, the network is off, the adapter and three.js disagree): the dock says "The 3D preview could not load" with a reason class, and **Try again**. Each press tries the load once more, by a new version query where the browser would otherwise reuse the failure (RECALLED, §8).
  - **The browser cannot draw** (no WebGL 2, RECALLED): "This browser cannot draw the 3D preview". **The graphics context is lost:** one automatic attempt to restore, then the same message with Try again.
  - **The scene is refused** (an incomplete palette, a description the adapter cannot read, an unexpected exception): "The 3D preview could not draw this map", with the count of the cause where there is one.
  - **The label add-on alone cannot load** (OA-6b): the picture draws without labels and the bar says "Labels could not load", with Try again; the rest is unaffected (DC-23, AC-26).
  - In every case the text description of AC-18 stays, and **the editor is untouched**: tools, autosave, undo and publish work as before. No message holds raw error text, a stack trace or any document content (`formatApiError`'s rule, though no API is involved); a log line names the class only.

  Verify: widget test through the fake port (each failure: the message, Try again, a retry that succeeds; the fake editor still saves); unit test in Node (a rejected palette; a bad description); manual case (rename the vendored file; turn hardware graphics off; force a context loss from the browser's tools).
- AC-17 [OA-6b]: **Performance at the caps (Q1 (a), ruled: measure, record, set no target).** None is stated in the PRD or the intent (§7); the owner ruled to **measure and not gate**, as OA-3 AC-32 does. The structure test below still applies.
  - **Structure, which is testable:** the number of draw calls depends on the levels drawn, the material groups and the item primitive kinds, and **never on the number of walls, openings, units or items.** The builder merges walls and plates per level and palette entry.
  - **At the caps:** a synthetic document with every per-level cap at its maximum on every level the caps allow (read from `VenueMapConstants`, never as literals), with 64-point polygons, builds, loads into the adapter and draws without error, and its draw-call count equals that of a document with a tenth of the elements.

Writing the numbers in the spec is not done (they come from the measurement).
  - The scene builder runs when a save lands, not per frame, and yields to the event loop between levels so the editor stays responsive (DC-11).

  Verify: unit test (the synthetic document at the caps), Node test (the draw-call equality), manual case (measured on the three malls, recorded).

### 3.6 Labels (Q2 (b), ruled 2026-10-14; added after the ruling, so numbered last)

The owner ruled that the preview carries store, unit and floor names, and widened E-25 to cover three.js 0.186.1's own add-on files (the same package and version, self-hosted, the admin page only, expiry 2027-04-02). The lightest add-on is the DOM label renderer, `CSS2DRenderer` (RECALLED until step 0, R-14); it needs no font file. These four criteria are OA-6b's.

- AC-23 [OA-6b]: **Which labels exist, and what they say.**
  - **Floor labels:** one per drawn level, in both modes and under the cut: the level's `name`, at the north-west corner of the bounding box of its slab's outline, at the slab's height.
  - **Unit labels:** only in "This floor" (DC-19). One per **non-walkable** unit that has a name to show (DC-20): the name of the unit's **active store** from the stores list (`Occupant.Name`), else the unit's own `name`; a unit with neither has no label. A walkable unit is not labelled, so the "Door channel" units OA-3 adds and the corridors stay clear. An inactive store gives no label; the unit's own name is used if it has one.
  - **The text is the mall's own words, verbatim:** not translated, not changed in case, not shortened except by the ellipsis of AC-25; a store's spoken name is never shown (PRD §6.23). It is set as **text**, never as markup: a name such as `<img onerror=…>` shows as those characters and runs nothing.
  - **The point:** a unit's label sits at a point inside its polygon (the centroid when that lies inside it, else a point the plan names that does), a named height above its plate. A pure Dart function, tested on concave polygons.
  - **Saved, not typed:** a store's label needs its unit in the saved draft, so a store on a unit not saved yet, or whose unit was deleted (key status "No place"), has no label; the name is the one the server holds, as the stores list last loaded or saved it (AC-4). A store that is added, renamed, deactivated or moved changes the labels at once, **without rebuilding the geometry**.
  - Each label carries a **priority**: floor labels first, then unit labels by the unit's area, largest first, ties by id, so which survives a collision is stable (AC-24).

  Verify: unit test (the label set for each mode and cut; store name over unit name; an inactive store; walkable units excluded; a store whose unit is not in the saved draft; a label point inside concave, L-shaped and 64-point polygons; a 200-code-point name with an emoji, verbatim; the priority order), unit test in Node (a name with markup becomes text only), widget test (a stores-list change updates labels and builds no scene), manual case (Aurora's store names against the Stores panel).
- AC-24 [OA-6b]: **When labels hide.** All in the adapter's label pass, in screen space, once per drawn frame (AC-13), deterministic for a given camera:
  - **Mode and cut:** unit labels exist only in "This floor"; a floor label exists only for a level that is shown.
  - **Collisions:** a label whose box overlaps the box of a label already kept is hidden; labels are taken in priority order, so a floor label beats a place label, and a larger room beats a smaller. A label's box is measured once, when it is made, and cached.
  - **Distance, as size:** a unit label is hidden while its room's on-screen size is smaller than the label's, by a named factor, so zooming out thins the names and zooming in brings them back. Floor labels are not hidden by size.
  - **Budget:** at most a named number of labels are visible at once.
  - **Out of view:** a label behind the camera or outside the drawing is hidden.
  - **Not drawn at all:** while the dock is collapsed, the tab is hidden, or the label layer failed to load (AC-26).

  All the numbers are named constants (DC-4). Verify: unit test in Node with a fake projection (each rule; the same camera gives the same visible set twice; priority order decides a collision; the budget; behind the camera), manual case (zoom out and in on Aurora; labels thin and return; none is ever left over a hidden room).
- AC-25 [OA-6b]: **Label size, look and accessibility.**
  - **Layer:** the labels are DOM text in the add-on's layer over the picture, with pointer events off: they never block the camera or the dock's resize handle, are not selectable, and have no hover.
  - **Size:** the label's text size is a named base multiplied by the app's text scale, which the Dart side passes with the scene options, and clamped between a named minimum and maximum; at ×1.4 it grows and nothing overflows the dock (the layer clips). A long name ends in an ellipsis at a named maximum width, in the label only; the full name is in the text description.
  - **Contrast:** a label's text and its backing are two tokens, light and dark, whose contrast is at least 4.5:1, PRD §6.23's figure for map text, applied to the admin page by DESIGN CHOICE (the admin has no figure of its own). A floor label differs from a place label by weight as well as by colour, so a label's kind is never told by colour alone.
  - **Screen readers:** the layer and every label are hidden from the accessibility tree and out of the tab order, so a reader is not given hundreds of labels. **The text description of AC-18 lists the same names once**, built from the same label data and independent of which labels the collision pass hid; so the names are neither duplicated for a reader nor lost.
  - **Rule 6:** the label colours are CSS colour strings made in Dart from the tokens and sent in the palette (AC-14); the adapter assigns them and holds no colour literal (the scan of AC-14 covers the label code).

  Verify: unit test (the contrast of each pair, in both themes; the text-scale clamp; the palette holds the label entries), unit test in Node (every label and the layer are `aria-hidden`, have pointer events off and are not focusable; the colour scan), widget test (the text scale reaches the adapter; the description lists each labelled name once and its counts equal the label set's), manual case (a screen-reader pass: the names come only from the description; ×1.4).
- AC-26 [OA-6b]: **The label add-on: self-hosted, pinned, and its failure.**
  - The add-on is `CSS2DRenderer` from three.js 0.186.1's own package (the path is RECALLED, R-14), vendored byte for byte beside the main module under AC-15's folder, manifest and tools test, its hash in the manifest. **No other add-on, no font file and no second package** is added: the tools test fails on any file the manifest does not list.
  - The add-on names the module `three` by a bare import (RECALLED, R-15). How the page resolves it (the expected way is an import map in `app/web/index.html` that points `three` at the vendored file) is settled at step 0 and recorded; the vendored bytes are never edited to fix it, and nothing is loaded from a CDN. The version query of AC-15 covers it.
  - The add-on loads with the main module on the dock's first expansion (DC-10). **If only the add-on fails to load,** the picture still draws without labels, the bar says "Labels could not load" with Try again, and the rest works (AC-16, DC-23).
  - E-25's row is amended to the owner's widening (AC-22).

  Verify: tools test (the hash, no extra file, no outside host), widget test with the fake port (a labels-only failure: the message, Try again, the scene unaffected), review (E-25's row, Blueprint Part 1), manual case (block only the add-on file).

## 4. Edge cases

Each is covered by the criterion named, or has a one-line reason.

**Offline or stale package**
- EC-1: The three.js file or the adapter cannot be fetched (offline, blocked, missing, an old cached build): the load-failure message with Try again; the editor is unaffected (AC-16).
- EC-2: The network drops after the scene is built: the preview fetches nothing after its load, so it keeps working (AC-20).
- EC-3: An old cached adapter next to a new three.js file or add-on, or the reverse: all are requested with one version query, and a pair that disagrees fails at mount with the load-failure message (AC-15, AC-16; DC-13).
- EC-4: A stale package or a published layout: **not applicable**. The preview reads neither a package nor a published version, only the saved draft (AC-4).

**Empty and maximum data**
- EC-5: No draft, or a cleared one: "nothing to show yet", no drawing surface (AC-4).
- EC-6: A draft with levels and no elements: slabs only; a stack of empty levels draws (AC-7).
- EC-7: The caps, 64-point polygons, and the largest seeds (Aurora, Bayview Grand's 19 levels, Skyline Spire's −9 to 66 m): AC-17.
- EC-8: Elevations at the range ends (−20 and 300 m): the camera's fit and its near and far planes (AC-12).
- EC-9: Two levels at one elevation, in one building or in two buildings (Meridian Twin Malls has three, each level's rectangle being the site's, OA-3 DC-9): both draw; an identical outline draws once; no flicker (AC-7).
- EC-10: A very small level (the schema's 2 m minimum side) beside a site 1,300 m wide: the camera fits the drawn geometry, not the site (AC-12).
- EC-11: A document at the 4 MiB limit: the description's size is bounded by the caps, which bound the document (AC-17).

**Tenant boundary**
- EC-12: Leaving the editor, opening another venue or choosing another tenant in the switcher disposes the scene; nothing of the previous venue remains (AC-13).
- EC-13: The preview makes no request, so no tenant predicate applies to it; another tenant's venue gives "Not found" and no dock (AC-20).

**Each role**
- EC-14: VIEWER, VENUE_EDITOR, MALL_ADMIN and SUPERADMIN all see the dock; none can change anything through it. A SUPERADMIN with no tenant never reaches the editor (400, P0-11) (AC-20).

**Permission denied**
- EC-15: A 403 on the editor's load or save belongs to the editor. With no loaded draft there is no dock; a 403 on a save does not blank the dock, which keeps the last saved draft and says unsaved changes are not shown (AC-4).

**Concurrent edits**
- EC-16: A second tab saves first: this tab's save gets a 409, the editor goes to conflict, and the preview keeps its saved version and says someone else changed the map; after Reload it shows theirs (AC-4).
- EC-17: Undo, redo and drags are unsaved changes: the preview does not move (AC-4).
- EC-18: Two saves close together: the last wins; one rebuild runs, at most one waits (AC-5).

**Publish in progress**
- EC-19: A publish is `BUILDING`: the preview shows the draft, not the version being built, and a save made meanwhile appears in it, as in the editor; the banner is the editor's (AC-4).
- EC-20: A Restore, Clear or Import replaces the draft: the new saved draft appears when its save lands; a Clear empties the dock and re-fits (AC-4, AC-5).

**Invalid or odd input**
- EC-21: A polygon that cannot be triangulated, a wall whose ends coincide, an opening outside its wall, a number that is not finite: skipped, counted, never thrown (AC-7, AC-8).
- EC-22: An unknown wall kind is drawn `solid`; an unknown connector kind is not drawn and is counted; an unknown finish key takes the `default` colour; an unknown item type is a plain box (AC-8, AC-10, AC-14, AC-9).
- EC-23: A saved draft of an unknown spec version or one that cannot be read: the editor handles it; the preview shows "could not draw this map" with no content (AC-16).

**Platform and interaction**
- EC-24: Focus in the dock: Delete, `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+D` and the arrow nudge do not reach the editor (AC-12).
- EC-25: A drag that leaves the dock mid-gesture ends cleanly; the wheel never scrolls the page; the context menu is suppressed (AC-12).
- EC-26: The dock at its minimum height, or the window at 320 px, or a zero-sized surface: nothing breaks, nothing is drawn at zero size (AC-2, AC-13, AC-18).
- EC-27: The tab is hidden or the dock collapsed: no frames (AC-13).
- EC-28: No WebGL 2, or a lost context: the message and Try again (AC-16).
- EC-29: The theme changes while open: the palette is re-sent and the geometry is not rebuilt (AC-14).
- EC-30: A format pass or an editor that rewrites the vendored file: the hash test fails; the folder is excluded from the formatters (AC-15).
- EC-31: Browser zoom, or the window moves to a monitor with another pixel ratio: read again at the next resize, capped (AC-13).
- EC-32: A touch-only device: not supported (PRD §3); mouse and keyboard only (AC-12).
- EC-33: Reduced motion: no animation exists (AC-12).
- EC-34: The penthouse or an owner export in a screenshot or report: never; this covers its store and room names on labels too (AC-21).

**Labels**
- EC-35: A store name of 200 characters, with an emoji, in a script other than Latin, or with markup in it: shown verbatim as text, ellipsis in the label, the whole name in the description; no markup runs (AC-23, AC-25).
- EC-36: A unit with an inactive store only: the unit's own name is used if it has one, else no label; a unit with two stores cannot occur for active ones (one active store per unit, P0-11 AC-110), and the active one is shown (AC-23).
- EC-37: A store renamed or added in the Stores panel but not yet saved: the label keeps the server's name until the save lands (AC-4, AC-23).
- EC-38: The stores list is not loaded, or its load failed (the Stores panel's own error): unit and floor labels still show; the preview raises no error of its own (AC-23, X1).
- EC-39: A store whose unit is not in the saved draft (new unit unsaved, or the unit deleted): no label (AC-23).
- EC-40: Many labels on one spot, or hundreds on Aurora's busiest level: collisions, size and the budget decide, the same way for the same camera (AC-24).
- EC-41: The label add-on alone fails to load: picture without labels, the message, Try again (AC-26).
- EC-42: Moving the camera below a floor, or looking from the side in a stack: floor labels stay at their corners; unit labels exist only in "This floor", where the DOM layer's lack of depth cannot show a name through a slab (AC-23, DC-19).

## 9. Design choices

Each is a DESIGN CHOICE, not a requirement, with an alternative that would also satisfy what is stated. They are listed for sign-off, not asked (L174).

- **DC-1 The saved draft comes from the editor's view model** (the draft loaded, then the document of each save answered 200), tagged with its `draftVersion`. P0-11 r3's hook exposes the *current* document; the preview needs the saved one beside it (X1). The sent document equals the stored copy up to the server's canonical form, which the builder tolerates (it fills every default itself). *Alternative:* the preview re-reads `GET …/map` after each save: always the stored copy, but one more request per save and a second reader of the draft.
- **DC-2 Dart builds a scene description; one JavaScript adapter owns every three.js call.** Almost all logic stays in testable Dart, and three.js's surface is one file. **After the Q2 (b) ruling** the description also carries the label data (text, world point, priority, room size), the adapter owns the label layer and the collision pass, and the Dart side owns which names exist (AC-23): the choice stands, and the add-on is called from the adapter alone. *Alternatives:* Dart calls three.js directly through `dart:js_interop` bindings (a binding for every class used, harder to test); or an iframe with a static page that three.js owns and `postMessage` (clean isolation, but not the platform-view embedding Q10 ruled).
- **DC-3 Our own camera controls in the adapter** (orbit, pan, zoom, limits). **Changed by the Q2 (b) ruling:** E-25 now covers the package's add-on files, so vendoring three.js's orbit-controls add-on is allowed, but the owner asked for the lightest add-on, and the label renderer is the one this spec needs. Our own controls keep the vendored set to that one add-on (a second add-on is a second file to pin, and a second bare `three` import). *Alternative:* vendor the orbit-controls add-on too, which saves writing the controls and adds a file to the manifest.
- **DC-4 Preview numbers are named constants in one Dart constants file** (dock heights, slab and plate thickness, door and window heights, fallback item size, glass opacity, pixel-ratio cap, camera limits), values set in the plan and recorded in the report, each with a note that it is a typical figure and not a source. They are not settings: no mall tunes them. *Alternative:* `GlobalSetting` rows, which would make a rendering detail an option list.
- **DC-5 The dock opens collapsed; its height, mode and cut last for the editor session only.** The collapsed default follows P0-11's hook. *Alternative:* remember them across sessions in `shared_preferences`.
- **DC-6 A level's slab follows its building's footprint** (the site boundary for the outdoor level, the rectangle when there is none). Every seeded level's rectangle is the site's (OA-3 DC-9), so a rectangle slab would be a giant plate, and two buildings at one elevation would coincide. *Alternative:* always the rectangle.
- **DC-7 Items, including wall-mounted types, stand on the floor.** The document has no mounting height. *Alternative:* a named mount height for `mount: wall` types.
- **DC-8 Simple shapes for walls and connectors.** Walls are not mitred; an elevator is a box and the other connectors one inclined solid per pair of levels, with no steps, cars or shape. *Alternative:* detailed models, which office §5's design note leaves out on purpose.
- **DC-9 "Show up to" is how a stack is read.** Opaque slabs hide what lies beneath, so a stack needs a way in. *Alternatives:* per-level checkboxes; or translucent slabs (mush at 16 levels).
- **DC-10 The three.js file loads on the first expansion.** A failure then stays inside the dock, and the editor opens as fast as without it. *Alternative:* load with the editor.
- **DC-11 Draw on demand; build when a save lands, yielding between levels.** An idle dock costs no GPU. *Alternative:* a continuous animation loop, simpler but busy while idle.
- **DC-12 The look is document-faithful** (floors, walls with height, items; no tints by category), differing from the shopper's 2.5D map as the owner accepted (re-plan §1.1); ruled Q3 (a). Names are now labels (Q2 (b), DC-19 to DC-24). *Alternative (not taken):* tint non-walkable rooms by category.
- **DC-13 A manifest of hashes and a tools test pin the vendored files; the adapter and three.js share one version query.** *Alternative:* Subresource Integrity through an import map (support RECALLED, not relied on).
- **DC-14 On a narrow window the dock opens as a sheet,** as P0-11 AC-135 moves the panels. *Alternative:* hide the dock below a width.
- **DC-15 Every role that can open the editor sees the dock,** VIEWER included: it changes nothing. *Alternative:* hide it from VIEWER.
- **DC-16 The status counts what could not be drawn** ("N shapes could not be drawn") rather than hiding it. *Alternative:* silent skips.
- **DC-17 A port with a web implementation and a stub,** the web one behind a conditional import, so the shopper app and the VM tests compile without the interop (R-8). *Alternative:* one class that checks `kIsWeb` at runtime, which still compiles the web-only imports everywhere.
- **DC-18 Two sub-slices (adopted after the labels).** At 22 criteria this was one slice; with the four label criteria it is 26, over L174's "about 20 a stage". **OA-6a** (AC-1 to AC-8, AC-12 to AC-16: the dock, one floor with walls, camera, self-hosting, failure) has 13 of its own and meets the 5 cross-cutting criteria (AC-18 to AC-22), so 18. **OA-6b** (AC-9 to AC-11, AC-17, AC-23 to AC-26: items, connectors, selection, the stack at the caps, labels) has 8 and meets the same 5, so 13. Each ends in something visible. They share one adapter, one scene description and one set of tokens, and cost a second review and changelog entry. *Alternative:* one slice of 26.
- **DC-19 Unit labels only in "This floor"; floor labels in both modes.** The add-on's labels have no depth test (R-14), so in a stack a store's name would show through the slabs above it. The owner's own list names mode and cut as reasons a label hides. *Alternative:* in a stack, unit labels for the top shown level only.
- **DC-20 Only non-walkable units are labelled.** Stores, restrooms and other rooms get names; corridors do not, and neither do OA-3's "Door channel" units, which would otherwise print hundreds of the same words. *Alternative:* label every unit with a name and exclude door channels by a rule on their name or an element flag, which OA-3 does not provide.
- **DC-21 The label layer is the three.js add-on's DOM renderer.** It needs no font file, takes colour and size from CSS set by us, and is the lightest add-on (the owner's instruction). *Alternatives:* our own DOM overlay from projected points (no add-on, but we own the layer's code and its tests); or labels drawn as text sprites inside WebGL (depth-correct, but needs a font or glyph atlas and more memory).
- **DC-22 Priority is the room's area, then its id; a budget and a size rule thin the labels.** Stable between frames, so labels do not flicker. *Alternative:* priority by distance to the camera, which changes with every movement.
- **DC-23 A label failure never stops the picture.** The add-on's load failure shows "Labels could not load" and the picture draws without labels. *Alternative:* treat it as a load failure of the whole preview (AC-16), simpler and worse for the admin.
- **DC-24 Store names come from the editor's Stores panel list, not from a request of the preview's own.** The panel (OA-4b-1) already holds, loads and refreshes the list, so the preview keeps AC-20's "no API" and has one reader of stores. It makes the labels depend on OA-4b-1. *Alternative:* the preview calls the stores list route itself, which adds a second reader, a request and a ledger row.

## 11. Questions, as ruled

The product owner (Genesis Perez) ruled all three on 2026-10-14 in the P0 sitting, and approved the intent and this spec with them. Each ruling is applied where it is named. The options offered stay below as the record. No question is open.

**Q1 [spec-writer] What speed must the preview reach, if any (AC-17)? Ruled 2026-10-14 by the product owner: (a).** Applied in AC-17, §7 and EC-7. No target for first draw, frame rate or memory is stated in the PRD, the intent or the re-plan.
- **(a) (Ruled.) Measure on the three malls, record the numbers, set no target.** The numbers go in the report for sign-off, as OA-3 does for the seed (OA-3 AC-32; P0-02 ruling C5). Structure that keeps cost flat as elements grow is tested (draw calls never depend on the number of elements).
- (b) You state targets now, and AC-17 is gated on them.
- (c) Limit what is drawn: "All floors" refused above a number of levels.

**Q2 [spec-writer] Should the 3D picture carry names (store and floor labels)? Ruled 2026-10-14 by the product owner: (b), not the recommendation.** Applied in AC-23 to AC-26, AC-14, AC-15, AC-16, AC-18, AC-20, DC-2, DC-3, DC-19 to DC-24, the §8 table (R-14 to R-17), §12 and the intent. The owner **widened E-25** to cover three.js 0.186.1's own add-on files (the same package and version, self-hosted, the admin page only, expiry 2027-04-02) and told the spec to use the lightest add-on, its DOM label renderer, which needs no font file (RECALLED until step 0).
- (a) The recommendation, not taken: no labels in this slice; the text description and the 2D editor carry the names.
- **(b) (Ruled.) Store names (from the `Occupant` rows) and unit names, and floor names, as labels in the picture.**

**Q3 [spec-writer] How should rooms look in 3D (AC-7, DC-12)? Ruled 2026-10-14 by the product owner: (a).** Applied in AC-7, DC-12 and §12.
- **(a) (Ruled.) Document-faithful.** Floors in their finish colours, walls at their real height with doors cut through, items as simple shapes; a store is a room with walls around it, as in the editor. It follows office §8.1 and re-plan Q10 ("from catalog primitives").
- (b) Also tint non-walkable rooms (stores) by their category.

## 1. What this spec covers

The shopper's ways of finding a destination, on an Android emulator or a phone connected to this machine (E-20, localhost only). Coders for FF-5 (A25): engine-coder (Opus 5.5) for the sweep, the FTS5 query and P0-03 r3's FF-5 part; flutter-coder (Sonnet 5.5) for the screens.

1. **One walking-distance sweep** (engine-coder): one Dijkstra with a binary heap over the whole graph from the RG3-snapped origin, cached in the routing worker, feeding every distance shown (L103; Blueprint §4.7).
2. **Search** (engine-coder for the FTS5 query and P0-03 r3's index additions, flutter-coder for the screens): as-you-type search of the package's FTS5 index from a search bar on the Map tab, closest first, unreachable last (PRD §6.5).
3. **The "nearest" chips** on the Map tab (L85, L99): each lists the 3 nearest open places of its kind.
4. **The Places tab**, replacing FF-SK's placeholder: the floor listing grouped by category, with the facilities and the connectors that stop there (L85).
5. **Favourites and the last 8 destinations**, on the phone only (L99, L88).
6. **Voice search** through `speech_to_text` (E-10): recognition on the phone first and the L116 question; "nearest ⟨type⟩" straight to the route; search by phrase; "the first one" (A13). Replies are shown on screen and announced to a screen reader in FF-5; they are spoken from FF-7 by P0-14 r2 (A13, A14).
7. **The search service and the voice input as provided interfaces** that P0-08 r2's "Where are you?" sheet calls in FF-6 (A9).

"Describe where you are" is not in this spec: P0-08 r2 (FF-6) builds the sheet and the description rules on top of item 7, recorded in FF-5's L-row (A9, ruling Q10).

## 3. Acceptance criteria

### A. The walking-distance sweep (engine-coder)

- **AC-1:** Given the open package, an origin and the routing options, the worker computes a metre cost to every reachable node with one Dijkstra (binary heap) over the whole graph, starting from the origin's RG3 snap (P0-06 AC-8, AC-9) and honouring one-way edges (L79 RG1, L86). Each node's cost equals a reference single-target Dijkstra's within 0.001 m (P0-06 DC-3). Verify: unit (P0-06's shared vectors, plus sweep vectors added to the same file, AC-32 of P0-06).
- **AC-2:** A place's distance is its nearest door's (Blueprint §4.7). An occupant uses its unit's doors. A nested section uses its own doors, or its parent's where it has none (P0-03 AC-13). An amenity, transit point or entrance uses its own node. A lift or stairs uses its stop on the level asked about. A named parking area has no door: the graph nodes that lie inside or on its outline count as its doors, so its distance is to the nearest of them, and a route to it ends where the shopper first enters the area; an area with no graph node inside or on its outline has no distance ("No way there", AC-7). P0-06 r4 names this destination kind as its AC-74 [r4 · FF-5]: a parking area's entry points are the graph nodes inside or on its outline, the route ends where the shopper first enters the area, and that cost is its distance (R1, Q18 option 1); the sweep uses the same entry points, so AC-3's agreement holds. Verify: unit (a two-door store whose far door is nearer by the graph; a doorless section; a parking area entered from two sides; a parking area with no graph node inside).
- **AC-3:** **Agreement with routing (invariant 6).** Under the same options, every place's sweep distance equals the metre cost of P0-06's least-cost way to it, within 0.001 m, before the stairs rule is applied; lists show this figure even where the stairs rule makes the route lead with another way (B7). This holds for passages too (L92, P0-06 AC-21, AC-24): a passage counts towards a place it serves, or towards every place when the origin is in a place it serves, and never as a shortcut otherwise while shortcuts are off. A closed passage makes the places behind it unreachable (E11). Verify: unit (on every shared vector and on the Aurora package, the sweep distance of each place equals P0-06's lead cost with the stairs rule off).
- **AC-4:** The sweep takes the same options object as routing: step-free (P0-07 r2's filter, AC-16), shortcuts (default off, PRD §6.4), and the avoid set once P0-06 r4 (FF-6) adds it. Changing any of them recomputes. Verify: unit.
- **AC-5:** The result is cached in the routing worker, keyed by the package version, the origin node (§6 DC-1), step-free, shortcuts, the avoid set and the overlay version (Blueprint §4.7). Every list reads the one cache. A second read with the same key does not run the sweep again. Verify: unit (sweep count across several list reads).
- **AC-6:** A result carries the package version it was computed on. A result from another version is dropped and the sweep runs again (as P0-06 AC-33). A newer request replaces a pending one (P0-06 DC-6). Verify: unit.
- **AC-7:** With an origin, a place with no way to it has no distance. It is listed after every reachable place with the line "No way there" (the demo's), and is never offered by a chip. Verify: unit and widget.
- **AC-8:** The sweep runs off the UI isolate and makes no network request. No origin, distance or query leaves the phone (invariant 4, G3). Verify: unit (a failing HTTP client is never called; the sweep code runs only in the worker).

### B. Search (FTS5)

- **AC-9:** Typing searches the package's FTS5 index over: stores, amenities with their type labels and synonyms, transit points, nested sections (PRD §6.5), venue entrances (by `Opening.Name`, else "Entrance, ⟨level⟩") and connector stops and ends ("Lift A · Level 2") (P0-03 r3, A10). Until P3-02, evacuation-only connectors and the refuge and assembly amenity types are not in the index, so search never lists them (P0-03 r3 as ruled, R28, R29). Landmarks (`Anchor` rows) are in the index for P0-08 but never appear in this spec's lists (B6). In Aurora, "cr" and "comfort room" each list restrooms (PRD §6.5; P0-03 AC-43's example), and "lift" lists each lift stop. Verify: integration (Aurora's package) and unit (fixture package, a landmark named like a store: only the store is listed).
- **AC-10:** Every word typed must match, each as a prefix of a word in the place's indexed text or of its level's words (AC-55), case-insensitive and ignoring accents both ways (A10), so results narrow as the shopper types (§6 DC-2). Verify: unit ("comf" finds restrooms; "bay grand" needs both words; "nino" finds "Niño" and "niño" finds "Nino").
- **AC-11:** Typed text never reaches FTS5 as query syntax. Quotes, `*`, `-`, `^`, `:`, parentheses and the words `AND`, `OR`, `NOT` and `NEAR` are matched as text or ignored, and never raise an error. The query is bound as a parameter, never interpolated (rule 3's phone-side form). Verify: unit (each character and word, alone and mixed).
- **AC-12:** With an origin, results are listed closest first by AC-1's distances, unreachable last, with ties broken by name (§6 DC-3). With no origin, they are listed by name (B1, AC-58). In wheelchair mode, restroom rows follow AC-56. Verify: unit.
- **AC-13:** Each result row shows the place's name, its kind (category, facility type, "Entrance" or the connector type), its level (with the building where the venue has more than one), the mall-set status for a store, and, with an origin, "About N m to walk" or "No way there" (no distance line with no origin, B1). An amenity without a name shows its type's label (§6 DC-4). A nested section also names the place it is inside. Verify: widget.
- **AC-14:** Nested sections are found by name ("Shoes" inside the department store, PRD §6.5). Verify: unit (fixture) and widget.
- **AC-15:** A store whose status is not routable is listed in its distance order (by name with no origin) with its status, dimmed. Picking it opens the place sheet with Go disabled and the reason (P0-04 AC-44; PRD §6.5). Verify: widget.
- **AC-16:** **The Map tab's search bar** (Blueprint §4.4) uses the shared search field (rule 7; no bare `TextField`), with a clear button and the microphone (F). Tapping it opens the results over the map:
  - with nothing typed: favourites, then recents, then "Nearby", the 40 closest places (demo `_Results`); with no origin, the third section is §6 DC-6's;
  - with a query: up to 40 matches, closest first (demo), by name with no origin;
  - "Nothing found" when nothing matches (demo).

  Verify: widget.
- **AC-17:** Picking a result closes the results, shows the place's level, moves the camera to it and opens P0-04's place sheet, which gains "About N m to walk" (or "No way there"; neither with no origin) and the favourite star (E). Picking a connector stop shows that connector on the map at that level, as AC-29. Picking never ends an active trip (B4, AC-57). Verify: widget.
- **AC-18:** An empty or spaces-only query shows the nothing-typed view (AC-16). Verify: widget.

### C. The "nearest" chips (L85, L99; PRD §6.5)

- **AC-19:** Under the search bar, a row of chips in the demo's order: Restroom, ATM, Food, Lift, Stairs, Exit, EV charging, Parking (PRD §6.5; demo `nearby`). The My car chip is not shown until P1-02 brings "I parked here" (B3). Each chip reads the data named by one closed table in the constants file (A26), with PRD §6.5 as its authority (B2):
  - Restroom → amenity types `CR` and `CR_PWD`;
  - ATM → amenity type `ATM`;
  - Food → occupant category `DINING`;
  - Lift → connectors whose cost model is `ELEVATOR`;
  - Stairs → connectors whose cost model is `STAIRS`;
  - Exit → venue entrances (`Opening.IsVenueEntrance`);
  - EV charging → amenity type `EV_CHARGING`;
  - Parking → named parking areas (units of IMDF category `parking` with a name), with no free-space figure (B3, L88); each one's distance is the cost to the nearest graph node inside or on its outline, where the route ends (AC-2, R1; P0-06 AC-74).

  A mall's own new type or category is found by search, never by a chip (B2). The row scrolls sideways when it does not fit. Verify: widget; unit (the table's mapping on a fixture with a tenant-added amenity type).
- **AC-20:** With an origin, tapping a chip opens "Nearest ⟨type⟩" ("By walking distance from you") with up to 3 of the nearest open places of that kind, the first marked "closest", each with its level and metres; the map shows the first one's level and moves to it. "None found" when there is none. Tapping the same chip again, or ✕, closes it (demo `_nearBody`, `act('near')`). With no origin, the chip opens a sheet saying "Set where you are to see what's nearest" (B1, AC-58). In wheelchair mode, the Restroom chip follows AC-56. Verify: widget and unit.
- **AC-21:** "Open" means an occupant whose status is routable (`IsRoutable`). Amenities, entrances, connectors and parking areas count as open until live status exists (P1-01). Verify: unit.
- **AC-22:** The Lift and Stairs chips list the lifts or staircases that stop on the origin's level, nearest first, each with the floors it serves ("Goes to L1, L3"; PRD §6.5, L99; demo `connPlace`, `placeSub`). Verify: unit and widget.
- **AC-23:** Tapping a row in "Nearest ⟨type⟩" starts the route to that place (demo `pickgo`) through P0-06, and records it as a recent (AC-34). During an active trip, AC-57 applies. Verify: widget (fake router).

### D. The Places tab (L85; PRD §6.5)

On a venue with more than one building, each level of every building is its own entry, labelled "⟨building⟩ · ⟨level⟩"; buildings come in published order with the main one first (as E5's wing shortcuts), levels top to bottom within each, and the outdoor ground is its own entry (B9). The filter scrolls when it does not fit. Verify: widget (one building; two buildings sharing a level name).
- **AC-26:** "All" with nothing typed: favourites, then recents, then "Nearby": the 60 closest places of every kind except facilities and connectors, with the line "Nearest 60 of N places, closest first." (demo `_places`). With no origin, the third section is §6 DC-6's. Verify: widget.
- **AC-27:** "All" with a query: matches on every level, facilities and connector stops included, closest first (by name with no origin), up to 80, with "N matches, closest first." or "The closest 80 of N matches." (demo); with no origin, "N matches, by name." or "The first 80 of N matches, by name." (§6 DC-6). Verify: widget.
- **AC-28:** One level: everything on it, grouped. Stores are grouped by their category, facilities by their type and transit points by their type, then "Entrances and exits", each group closest first (by name with no origin, B1); then "To other floors": the lifts, escalators, stairs and ramps that stop there, each with the floors it goes to (demo `_floorDirectory`, `_connRow`). - **AC-29:** Tapping a place opens it on the Map tab (AC-17). Tapping a connector row shows that connector on the map at that level (demo). Verify: widget.
- **AC-30:** The tab has four states (FEATURE_PIPELINE §5 item 5):

  - empty: "Nothing on this floor" for an empty level, or no places at all;
  - error: the package cannot be read (P0-06 AC-31's "routing unavailable" case);
  - data.

  Verify: widget.
- **AC-31:** Returning to the Places tab after the origin, an option or the package changed shows the new distances and order (item 7). Verify: widget.

### E. Favourites and the last 8 destinations (L99; PRD §6.5)

- **AC-32:** The place sheet has a star that adds the place to the favourites or removes it, with its state named for a screen reader ("Add to favourites" / "Remove from favourites", the demo's). Verify: widget.
- **AC-33:** Favourites are kept in the order they were added (demo). No cap is stated. Verify: unit.
- **AC-34:** Recents hold the destination of each route started (Go, a "Nearest" row, voice), newest first, no repeats, the last 8 (PRD §6.5). A connector and the car are not recorded (demo `startRoute`). Verify: unit.
- **AC-35:** Favourites, then recents not already in the favourites, top an empty search (AC-16) and the Places tab's "All" view (AC-26) (PRD §6.5; demo `starred`, `recent`). Verify: widget.
- **AC-36:** Both lists are saved on the phone per mall and restored after a restart (demo `session.dart`); they are never sent; a broken, out-of-range or foreign saved value falls back to an empty list (PRD §6.4, L88). Verify: unit (corrupted values; a failing HTTP client).
- **AC-37:** A saved favourite or recent whose place is not in the open package stays saved on the phone and is hidden while absent; it shows again if a later version restores the place (same mall, same place id) (B8). The saved recents stay the last 8 started, hidden ones included, so fewer than 8 may show. Verify: unit (v1 with the place, v2 without, v3 with it again).

### F. Voice search (E-10, L112, L116; A12, A13, B5)

- **AC-38:** A microphone button in the Map tab's search bar (PRD §6.5; "Where are you?" is P0-08's). One tap listens for one phrase, only after the tap; listening stops after the phrase, on a second tap, or when the app leaves the foreground (invariant 5). Nothing is recorded or kept. Verify: widget (fake recogniser) and unit (lifecycle fake).
- **AC-39:** The first voice search asks for the microphone, with a line in place saying why. A refusal leaves every other feature working, and the button then explains that the microphone is off (PRD §6.23, Apple 5.1.1(iv)). Verify: widget (fake permission).
- **AC-40:** Recognition on the phone is asked for first (`onDevice`). Only where that fails does the tap ask "Your phone sends this to Google/Apple to understand it. Use it?" with Yes and Type instead (L116). No audio is handed to an off-phone recogniser before Yes. Yes listens with the phone's own online recogniser; Type instead puts the cursor in the search field. ## 6. Design choices (not requirements; each with an alternative)

DC-1 to DC-5 were accepted on 2026-10-12 (A7). DC-6 and DC-7, new in r2, were accepted on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R2, R3). DC-8 names the fallback setting the owner ruled on 2026-10-12 (from Spike 2); the name alone is the design choice.

- **DC-1 The origin node.** The cache key's origin is the end of the snapped edge nearer the snapped point (Blueprint §4.7's "origin node"); distances are measured from the snapped point itself. *Alternative:* key on the exact snapped point and recompute on every move; exact, but every live-dot update would sweep again (FF-9).
- **DC-2 Prefix matching per word, all words required.** FTS5 matches word prefixes, so "mart" finds "Martin's" but not "Walmart". *Alternative:* substring matching as the demo (`contains`), through a trigram index; this means a larger package and a P0-03 change.
- **DC-3 Ties by name.** *Alternative:* ties by level then name.
- **DC-4 An unnamed amenity shows its type's label** ("Restroom"), told apart by its level and distance. *Alternative:* leave unnamed amenities out of search, which would hide most restrooms in the seeds.
- **DC-5 The cache holds one entry**, the latest key. *Alternative:* keep the last few keys, so turning step-free off and on again needs no new sweep; better if AC-52's timings show the sweep is slow.
- **DC-6 The nothing-typed view with no origin. Accepted (R2).** B1 rules "lists sort by name". With no origin, the third section of the empty search (AC-16) and the Places "All" view (AC-26) is "All places", the first 40 or 60 by name, with the line "First 60 of N places, by name." *Alternative:* no third section until there is an origin, only favourites, recents and the line "Set where you are to see what's nearby"; better if an alphabetical list of a 400-place mall is judged noise.
- **DC-7 Accessible-first in mixed lists. Accepted (R3).** A11 says "accessible restrooms before other restrooms; other rows keep their distance order". In search results and the Places lists, the positions the restroom rows hold are kept and filled in P0-07's order, so no other row moves. *Alternative:* lift every restroom row to the top of the list in P0-07's order; better if a wheelchair user's "cr" search should read like the chip.
- **DC-8 The fallback setting's name** (AC-59): `routing.sweepMinIntervalS`, a `routing.*` key so that it travels in the package with the routing settings the sweep already reads. The setting, its default of 0 (off) and its fallback role are the owner's ruling of 2026-10-12; only the name is a DESIGN CHOICE. *Alternative:* a non-routing key such as `nearest.minIntervalS` sent in the public mall list (A6), which would let a mall change it without a republish; better if the limit is expected to be tuned often in the field.

## 12. Questions raised in r2 (ruled 2026-10-12)

Two gaps that r1 did not raise and the L165 rulings did not settle. Both were ruled by the product owner on 2026-10-12 (`plan/FF-5-11-residual.md`, APPROVED, every item as recommended); the options are kept for the record.

**Q18 [spec-writer] Where a route to a named parking area ends, and so its distance.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R1): option 1.** → AC-2, AC-19, EC-52; P0-06's next revision names the destination kind. B3 rules that the Parking chip lists the 3 nearest named parking areas, and the intent puts "About N m to walk" on parking zones (L103). A parking area is a walkable unit with no door (the seeds store the demo's zones as named units of IMDF category `parking` with no opening), and Blueprint §4.7's "a place's distance is its nearest door's" and P0-06 r2 AC-3's door nodes do not cover it. Holds: AC-2's parking line and AC-19's Parking chip.
1. *(Recommended)* The area's walkway nodes that lie inside or on its outline count as its doors: the distance is to the nearest of them, and a route ends where the shopper first enters the area. P0-06's next revision names this destination kind; one with no node inside has no distance ("No way there"). It matches "you are in the parking area" and reuses P0-06's multi-door search (P0-06 DC-1).
2. One point: the area's centre, snapped to its walkways (RG3), as the single destination. Simpler to show on the map, but the figure includes walking across the area.
3. Build the Parking chip with P1-02, which brings the Parking tab and its zone rules; FF-5 shows Restroom to EV charging only.

**Q19 [spec-writer] From FF-7, which word lists a spoken phrase is read against.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R4, shared with P0-08 Q15): option 1.** Every shipped language's lists are read, so Taglish works; the Filipino lists are drafted in FF-6 and reviewed with FF-7's Tagalog, and only approved lines merge. → §2, AC-41, AC-42, AC-54, EC-49, §9. A12 puts the "nearest" words and ordinals in ARB per language, and Q5's ruling makes recognition follow the Language setting. Nothing says whether a shopper with Language set to English who says "pinakamalapit na CR" or "yung una" is understood (L112's "everyday Taglish" concerns Gabay's speech). FF-5 is not held (English lists only); this holds EC-49's FF-7 half and P0-14 r2's tests of it.
1. *(Recommended)* Every language's lists, whatever the Language setting: a Taglish speaker mixes both, and the words do not collide ("una" is not English). Needs the app to read the Tagalog ARB strings while showing English (RECALLED, §9).
2. Only the lists of the Language setting; a shopper who speaks Tagalog sets Language to Filipino.

## 2. Definitions

- **Cost** *[r5]*: the routing weight, in **seconds** (L170 Q4, reversing L86 and L91; office §11; invariant 15 as the Blueprint rewrites it). Until r4 it was the *metre cost*, and r4's criteria that say "metre cost" now read "cost".
  - **Where it comes from:** every edge's cost is computed at publish and stored in the package by P0-03 r4 (FF-3′): a walkway edge, a store-crossing edge, and a connector edge, with whatever the mall tunes on its `ConnectorType` row and whatever the `preferred` mark does. **This spec does not define, restate or recompute any of it.** The router reads each edge's cost in seconds as the package gives it, and nothing else about how it was formed (the product owner's ruling of 2026-10-14).
  - What this spec does require of the costs (a requirement on P0-03 r4): every cost is a finite number of seconds that is 0 or more (EC-3, DC-4); and it is stored to at least the resolution of DC-3's tolerance (0.001 s).
- **Real metres:** the walked length, without weights. Legs show real metres (L92: "the steps show the real metres"). *[r5]* A way's real metres are the sum of its edges' real lengths as the package stores them (P0-03 r4; re-plan Q4 (a)). Each edge's real length is stored beside its cost, never derived from it.
- **Route figure** *[r5]*: the metres shown on a way are its **real metres**, and its minutes come from its **cost in seconds** ("About N min", AC-10; L170 Q4). *Until r4:* the metres shown were the metre cost, as in the reference (`demo_app` `sheet.dart`, `routes[i].total`), and the minutes were metre cost ÷ 1.2 m/s (PRD §6.6, L86).
- **Leg:** a contiguous part of a route of one kind:
  - walk;
  - walk through a store (with the store);
  - ride (with the connector, up or down, the from level and the to level).
- **Shortcut edge:** a store-crossing edge of a store the mall marks `Unit.PassThrough` (L29, L92). *[r5]* The mark is now the unit's `passThrough` in the venue map document (OA-1 §2.5; re-plan §1.2).
- **Passage:** a walk-through store that publish marks as the only way to one or more places (L92, Blueprint §4.1). If a place can be reached only through either of two stores, both are passages (P0-03 ruling D10). The package lists the places each passage serves.
- **Behind a passage:** a place is behind a passage when every way to it uses one of its passages.
- **No-stairs search:** the same search with every connector whose `CostModel` is `STAIRS` excluded.
- **Way:** one of the routes offered, either the lead way or, when one qualifies, the other way. From r4, also each way around (§4.11).
- **Active way:** the way the shopper is following on an active trip: the lead way, or the way they tapped or chose (AC-15, AC-53).
- **Venue frame:** the one planar coordinate frame, in metres, shared by every building and level of a venue (ruling E10). *[r5]* It has the office orientation (L170 Q5; office §3; OA-1 §2): `x` runs across the plan and `z` runs down it, the origin is the top-left corner, and no coordinate is negative. Positions in this spec are written (x, z).
- **Two-way escalator** *[r5]*: an escalator whose `twoWay` flag is set. It runs both ways. This is a Gabay-only exception to office §4.2 (N6; OA-1 §2.6). Any other escalator is one-way, from its `levels[0]` stop to its `levels[1]` stop. Stairs, ramps and lifts are always two-way (OA-1 §2.6).
- **Nearest reachable point** *[r5]*: where a way ends when no door of its destination can be reached (AC-76; office §11; L170 Q2, Q6).
- **Ends short** *[r5]*: said of a way that ends at the nearest reachable point instead of at a door of its destination (AC-76).
- **Planar run** of an edge: the straight-line distance in the venue frame between its two end nodes.
- **Level span** of an edge: the absolute difference between the ordinals of its end nodes' levels. An edge with a level span above 0 is *level-changing*, whatever its kind: a connector, or a walkway between two buildings' levels with different ordinals (D6).
- **Estimate (RG2):** A*'s lower bound on the remaining cost (AC-36; in seconds from r5).
- **Route origin (r4):** the point a route starts from. FF-4 and FF-5: the Start-here point (AC-35). From FF-6: the `PositionSource`'s route origin, which P0-08 decides (ruling A8: hand-set, approximate, live-confident, live-unsure but not grey, and guided are origins; grey is not; a held hand-set position beats a live-unsure one).
- **Unsnapped position (r4):** the position off route, browsing and arrival read. For a live position it is the matcher's estimate, before every display step (ruling F3; P0-13 AC-24, L106). For every other state it is the position itself.
- **Progress (r4):** the shopper's real metres along the active way from its start (AC-42).
- **Direction (r4):** one thing the shopper does along a way, landmark first (AC-39, AC-40).
- **Stretch ahead (r4):** what "Find another way" avoids (AC-50).
- **Trip avoidance (r4):** the stretches the shopper has chosen to go around during this trip (AC-56).
- **Deviation (r4):** a wrong turn confirmed by the off-route rule (AC-57).
- **Parking area (r4, residual sheet R1):** a named walkable unit of IMDF category `parking` (P0-05 AC-19; ruling B3). It has no door. Its **entry points** are the walkway graph's nodes on its level that lie inside its outline or on it, and they count as its doors (AC-74).

## 3. What the engine reads from the package (requirements on P0-03)

P0-06 does not fix the package's binary layout, because Spike 2 may change P0-03's format choices (intent P0-03, notes; ruling D1). *[r5]* The package is built at publish from the **published venue map document**, plus the `Occupant`, `Beacon` and settings rows (re-plan §1.2, "Shopper package"; P0-03 r4, FF-3′). The walkway graph is still generated at publish (L32: 1.5 m spacing, 16 neighbours), now with office §11's 0.24 m body margin around blockers and door channels cut through it (L170 Q6). That is P0-03 r4's work; the engine only reads the result. The engine reads, through one package-reader interface:

1. **The graph:** a directed graph in CSR form. Each edge carries its cost (*[r5]* in seconds, §2), its real length and its kind:
   - walkway;
   - store crossing, with the store's unit;
   - connector, with the connector, its `CostModel`, its from level and its to level.
   A one-way connector has an edge only From → To (L86). A lift has an edge between every pair of stops it serves (Blueprint §3.4, `NavConnectorStop`). *[r5]* In the document's terms (OA-1 §2.6): a one-way escalator has an edge only from its `levels[0]` stop to its `levels[1]` stop. A two-way escalator (N6), stairs and ramps have edges both ways. A lift has an edge each way between every pair of the levels it serves. A connector with `walkable: false` is scenery and has no edge (office §11).
2. **Per node:** its level and its planar position in metres, in the venue's one frame (ruling E10; P0-01 and P0-03 hold to it; *[r5]* in the office orientation, §2).
3. **Per level:** ordinal, building, `IsOutdoor`, name and short name. *[r5]* These come from the document's level: `ordinal`, `building`, `outdoor`, `name`, `shortName` (OA-1 §2.4).
4. **Per place:** its door nodes. **Per store:** `PassThrough`, its status's `IsRoutable` (a snapshot at publish; live status is P1-01), its name and its category icon. **From r4:** each place's door points, level, name, kind (occupant, amenity, transit point, anchor, venue entrance, connector stop or end, and *[r4 · FF-5]* parking area) and category or type code, used for landmarks (AC-41), similar places and "Get help" (AC-54). For a parking area, the unit's category, name and outline and the graph's nodes give its entry points (AC-74). All of these are already in P0-03's allow-list (§3.7), so R1 needs no package change.
5. **The passages:** each passage store and the places it serves.
6. **Connector names** (and `SpokenName`, read by P0-14). Ruling A9 adds them to the package (P0-03 r2). From r4 the connector's stops or ends per level are read too, for a ride's levels passed (AC-40).
7. **The R\*Trees for RG3** (Blueprint §4.2): one over walkway edges and one over wall segments, per level. Ruling A9 adds the wall-segment R\*Tree to the package (P0-03 r2).
8. **The routing settings:** the venue tenant's values of every `routing.*` key, resolved at publish (the tenant's override, else the platform default) and fixed with the version (ruling A3; P0-03 AC-72 publishes "every `routing.*` setting"). r2 used `routing.stairsSaveM`; P0-07 adds `routing.stepFreeMinWidthM` (FF-5); r4 adds the keys of AC-70. *[r5 · FF-4]* `routing.stairsSaveS` replaces `routing.stairsSaveM` (AC-18). Any `routing.*` key that P0-03 r4 uses to form edge costs is applied at publish and is never read by the engine from this list. `routing.*` never travels in the mall list. Everything else the phone needs travels only in the list (A3, A6; P0-04).
9. **`PackageFormatVersion`.** *[r5]* It stays 1, because no reader exists yet (re-plan §1.2; R31).
10. *[r5 · FF-4]* **String ids.** Every id that the engine reads, takes in a query or returns in a result is the document's string id, matching `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,40}$` and unique within the document (office §3; OA-1 §2). This covers buildings, levels, places, units, connectors and, from the relational rows, the unit key a store is keyed on. Graph node indices stay integers internal to the CSR, and DC-2's tie-break uses them. A store's `Occupant` row is keyed on its unit's id (re-plan §1.2, "Stores").

## 4. Acceptance criteria

### 4.1 The engine

AC-1: Given a package, an origin and a destination place, the engine returns the way with the least cost (L86, invariant 15). *[r5 · FF-4]* The cost is the **cost in seconds** (§2; L170 Q4), the sum of the edge costs the package holds. Its cost equals a plain Dijkstra's on the same graph within 0.001 s (§7 DC-3). *Until r4:* the least metre cost, within 0.001 m. Where no door of the destination can be reached, AC-76 applies. Verify: unit (shared vectors, each also solved by a reference Dijkstra in the test).

AC-2: The search runs in a worker isolate that loads a package's graph once and serves every later query on that package. The UI isolate never runs a search, and reaches the engine only through asynchronous messages. Verify: unit (the service test counts graph loads across several queries and asserts the search code is called only in the worker).

AC-3: A destination with several doors is reached at whichever door gives the least cost (*[r5]* seconds) (Blueprint §4.7, "a place's distance is its nearest door's"). Verify: unit (vector: two doors, the far door cheaper by graph).

AC-74 *[r4 · FF-5]*: **A named parking area as a destination (residual sheet R1, the ruling on P0-05 Q18; ruling B3).** A parking area (§2) has no door, so its entry points count as its doors: the walkway graph's nodes on its level that lie inside its outline or on it. A route to it is AC-3's search to that set of points (§7 DC-1), so it ends where the shopper first enters the area, and its metre cost is the area's distance (P0-05 AC-2, through P0-05's sweep). *[r5 · FF-5]* Its cost, in seconds, equals the sweep's cost to that entry point. The distance P0-05 shows is the sweep's real metres, which is P0-05's to revise. From FF-6, its directions end with an arrival at that point (AC-39, AC-40), and a detected arrival is within `routing.arriveM` of any entry point (AC-67). A parking area with no entry point gives no route, with the reason "unreachable from here" (AC-31). *[r5 · FF-5]* This stays so under AC-76: with no entry point there is no goal node to be near (§7 DC-40). A parking area whose entry points exist but cannot be reached follows AC-76. It is built in FF-5, because P0-05's Parking chip and its distances (B3; P0-05 AC-2, AC-19) are FF-5's. Verify: unit (a vector with a parking area reached from two sides: the route ends at the first entry point reached, and a point on the outline counts; its cost equals the distance P0-05's sweep gives; an area with no node inside or on its outline gives "unreachable from here").

AC-4: A one-way escalator is never travelled To → From (L86). Where the only short way runs against it, the engine returns the longer legal way. Where there is no legal way, it returns "no route". *[r5 · FF-4]* Under the document, To → From means `levels[1]` → `levels[0]` (OA-1 §2.6). **A two-way escalator (`twoWay`, N6) is travelled both ways**, each at its published cost (L170 N6, a Gabay-only exception to office §4.2). Where no legal way reaches the destination, AC-76 applies: a way to the nearest reachable point, or "unreachable from here". Verify: unit (three vectors: the longer legal way; no legal way, which gives AC-76's result; a two-way escalator ridden down).

AC-36: At each package load, the worker derives the estimate's two coefficients from the package's own edges (ruling A7, amending L79 RG2's wording; RG2's shape is kept: a planar term plus a per-level term). *[r5 · FF-4]* **Every edge's cost is in seconds and is read from the package as it is** (§2), so the coefficients are in seconds per metre and seconds per level, and nothing about how P0-03 r4 formed a cost (a mall's tuning, a `preferred` mark, a walking rate) is known to, or needed by, this derivation:
- `a = min over edges with planar run > 0 of (cost ÷ planar run)`, and `a = 0` when no edge has a planar run. **The r2 to r4 cap, `min(1, …)`, is removed** (§7 DC-10): the 1 meant one metre of cost per metre walked, which has no meaning in seconds;
- `b = max(0, min over level-changing edges of ((cost − a × planar run) ÷ level span))`, and `b = 0` when no edge changes level;
- the estimate at node n is the minimum over the destination's door nodes d of `a × planar distance(n, d) + b × |ordinal(n) − ordinal(d)|`.

Every edge then satisfies `cost ≥ a × planar run + b × level span` (this needs every cost to be 0 or more, §2). By the triangle inequality in the one venue frame (E10) and on ordinals, two properties follow:
- The estimate is 0 at every door node.
- For every edge u → v, `estimate(u) ≤ cost(u → v) + estimate(v)`. The estimate is consistent, so it never exceeds the true remaining cost, whatever the package's costs are (a connector tuned low, a connector with a lowered cost, a cost of 0), and A* never reopens a node. This is the heuristic trap's answer for a lowered edge: the lowered edge lowers `b`, and the estimate follows it down instead of overestimating (V-RG2-7).

When the package's costs drive both coefficients to 0, the estimate is 0 and A* behaves as Dijkstra: still least-cost, only slower (visible in AC-34's times). The same estimate serves AC-76's search for an unreachable goal: no goal node is reached, so the search settles every reachable node, as Dijkstra does. Verify: unit (`heuristic_test`):

### 4.7 The latency target [r3 · FF-4]

AC-37: The routing latency target is provisional (L117 ruling 4) and fixed by rule (the owner's ruling on Q10, 2026-10-12): **the target is Spike 2's measured 95th-percentile A\* time on the realme (M5) × 1.5**, rounded up to one decimal of a millisecond (§7 DC-15, ruled 2026-10-12 by residual sheet R37). The rounding is done on the exact decimal product, never on a binary float, so a product already at one decimal is kept as it is (EC-46). It is a setting:
- the platform `GlobalSetting` `perf.routeP95TargetMs` (§7 DC-13, accepted by R43), listed in `functions/src/config/settings.js` `DEFAULTS` before use (invariant 16), with the default 0, meaning "no target yet". The setting holds text (`GlobalSetting.SettingValue VARCHAR(400)`), so a value with one decimal fits, and it is read as a decimal number;
- seeded with the computed value in FF-4, by Gabay's first settings seeder (`db/seeds/lib/settings.js`), from Spike 2's report (`phase-reports/feature-first/Spike-2.md`), which is committed without it (FF-4 plan 1.0 §14 (c), ruled 2026-10-12);
- not written into this spec. Filling it in needs no further ruling and no revision of this spec (the owner's ruling), only the seed row, with PRD §6.6 and §7 and Blueprint OQ6 updated to cite it (§9 item 12);
- not a `routing.*` key, so it is not in the package (P0-03 AC-72), and not in the public mall list's allow-list (P0-04 AC-60): no phone reads it. AC-34's comparison reads it.

**What the target covers (the owner's ruling of 2026-10-12 on Spike 2's results; `phase-reports/feature-first/Spike-2.md` §5.1).** It is the A\* search itself, in the worker isolate, on a graph of about 20,000 nodes, the scope Spike 2 measured. It judges two option sets, each with no step-free and no avoidance: Spike 2's (store shortcuts allowed) and the shopper's default (shortcuts off, PRD §6.4) (FF-4 plan 1.0 §14 (a), ruled 2026-10-12). It applies to every route the shopper asks for, the first after the package opens included, because the worker is warmed (AC-75). The following are recorded in AC-34's run but not judged against the target: the RG3 origin snap, building the route's points and directions, the isolate message round trip, and the step-free and avoid option sets.

Verify: unit (the `DEFAULTS` entry and its 0 default; a one-decimal value is read as that decimal); seed test (once the report is merged, the platform row exists with a positive value); review (dod-reviewer checks the seeded value equals the report's M5 p95 × 1.5, rounded up to one decimal of a millisecond).

AC-75 *[r4 · FF-4]*: **The router is warmed when a package opens** (the owner's ruling of 2026-10-12 on Spike 2's results: the first A\* after a fresh start took about four times the warm p95, Spike-2.md §4.5). Each time the worker loads a package (at launch, on a mall pick and after every swap, AC-33), it runs one hidden route between two nodes of that package before it reports itself ready. The hidden route:
- uses no position, origin or destination of the shopper (invariant 4);
- is never shown, raises no route event (AC-44) and is not counted among AC-34's timed searches;
- never delays a shopper's query by more than its own run: a query that arrives meanwhile is served as soon as it ends (DC-6 still applies between the shopper's own queries).

The hidden route runs from the package's first node to its last node, with the shopper's default options (shortcuts off), and its result is discarded (FF-4 plan 1.0 §14 (b), ruled 2026-10-12). Verify: unit (on a package load the worker runs exactly one search, from the first node to the last with default options, before the first query, and its result is discarded with no event; again after a swap); integration (AC-34's Drive APK run also records the first route after a fresh start, which is compared with AC-37's target like the rest).

### 4.9 Directions, progress and heading [r4 · FF-6]

AC-39: For every way in a result (the lead, the other way and each way around), the worker derives its directions in order along the way, by one pure function, from the way's points and legs and the package (§7 DC-16). There is one direction for each of these:
- **a turn:** a bend between two walk segments on one level, each at least 3 m long (§7 DC-17). It is a turn at 30° or more (PRD §6.6). It is classed slight from 30° to under 50°, plain from 50° to under 120°, and sharp from 120° (DC-17). A slight bend counts only at a fork: another walkway leaves the bend point (not the way back, the way on, a store crossing or a connector) and bends at most 10° more than the way does (PRD §6.6 "about as straight"; DC-17). A corridor that merely curves gets no direction (PRD §6.6). Left and right are as the shopper walks (L113);
- **a ride:** consecutive connector legs on one connector are one ride, however many levels it spans (DC-17, the reference's rule);
- **a store crossing:** "Walk through ⟨store⟩" (AC-25);
- **going outside or coming in:** where the way moves between an indoor level and an outdoor one (`IsOutdoor`);
- **a bridge:** a walk edge whose ends lie on levels of two different buildings (D6);
- **arrival:** always the last.

Verify: unit (direction vectors on fixture packages: a straight walk; a 90° turn; a 40° bend at a fork and the same bend on a curve; a 130° turn; a 2 m jog; a lift over three levels; an escalator; a store crossing; going outside; a bridge; the arrival side).

AC-40: Each direction carries:
- its kind, from a closed set (§9 item 14), and for a turn its hand and class;
- its point along the way, in real metres from the way's start (P0-14 §4; P0-15 §3 item 1): the bend point, the foot of a ride, the door into a store, the threshold, or the destination's door;
- its approach: the real metres walked since the previous direction's point, counted from the top or end of a ride, not its foot (the reference's rule);
- its level, and whether its approach is outdoors;
- its landmark and the landmark's side (AC-41), where there is one;
- the places passed on its approach, in order, each with its point along the way and its side (AC-41; P0-14 §4 "checkpoints along its approach");
- for a ride: the connector, up or down, the from and to levels, and the levels between them in order, each marked with whether the connector stops there (P0-14 AC-21's express escalator and lift);
- for a store crossing: the store; for arrival: the destination and the side its door is on.

Verify: unit.

AC-41: **Landmarks and sides.** A direction's landmark is the place whose door point is nearest the direction's point, within 12 m, on the same level (§7 DC-18). The candidates are open occupants (`IsRoutable`), amenities, transit points and anchors (named landmarks, decision sheet G10). The destination, venue entrances and connector stops are never a landmark (DC-18). A place is passed on an approach when the way runs within 5 m of its door point (DC-18). Sides are left or right, relative to the direction of travel along the way at that point (L113), never the screen, so they do not change when the map turns. Where nothing qualifies, the direction has no landmark, and the card gives the metres alone (PRD §6.6). Verify: unit (one vector, the map rotated to several angles, the sides unchanged; a tie broken as DC-18 says).

AC-42: **Progress, the next direction and the heading.** For the active way, the route ViewModel exposes:
- **progress:** the real metres along the way of the unsnapped position's projection onto it, computed by one function (§7 DC-19). It takes the nearest point of the way on the position's level, searched within a radius around the previous progress and advancing at most a maximum step per update. P0-13 AC-24 supplies the radius and step, `dot.progressRadiusM` (5 m) and `dot.progressMaxStepM` (10 m), from FF-9; until then the same values are named constants. A guided position gives the guided source's own metres (P0-15). A hand-set or approximate position gives 0, because each new one works the route out again from itself (P0-08 AC-23);
- **the next direction:** the first whose point lies more than 0.01 m beyond progress (the reference's rule);
- **the metres to it**, which count down as progress grows (PRD §6.6);
- **the heading of the way's walk segment at any progress point**, connector segments skipped (Blueprint §4.4), read by P0-04 r3's turning map and by P0-14 for sides.

Verify: unit (fake position streams; a ride; a guided track; a hand-set position).

The notice stays until 3 consecutive updates fall within the limit, a new route replaces this one for another reason, or the shopper dismisses it (§7 DC-30). Verify: unit; widget.

AC-60: **Falling progress** (L80; Blueprint §4.2): when progress along the active way falls for 3 consecutive updates by more than `routing.progressFallM`, the same notice and event as AC-59's items 2 and 3 follow, but the route is not worked out again (§7 DC-28). The same states as AC-58 are excluded. The value of `routing.progressFallM`, and whether the limit is per update or in total over the 3 updates, are proposed by FF-6's plan as a provisional setting with its basis (the reference or the spike where one exists, else a reasoned figure), and the product owner approves them with that plan, as F6 ruled for P0-13 (Q12, ruled 2026-10-12, residual sheet R38). Spike 1 or FF-9's on-site run may retune it through the seed. Verify: unit (fake streams at the plan's approved value and reading: 3 updates falling by just more than the limit give the notice and no re-route; by exactly the limit, none).

AC-61: The route never changes on its own otherwise (L110). It is not switched when a better way appears, on a venue alert (P1-01), or when a live dot takes over from a hand-set position (P0-13 EC-38: progress continues from the live position). A route changes only by a confirmed deviation (AC-59), "Go this way" (AC-53), a new position set by the shopper (P0-08 AC-23), an option change (AC-26) or a new destination. Verify: unit (a better way appearing mid-trip changes nothing; a takeover keeps the way).

### 4.13 Browsing (L110) [r4 · FF-6; proven again in FF-9]

AC-62: The shopper is browsing while a live position is inside a store that the active way neither crosses nor ends at (P0-13 AC-1's store), and has moved less than `routing.browseMoveM` (Blueprint §4.2) over a time window. AC-63: While browsing, no deviation is counted and there is no wrong-way notice, sound, vibration or re-route (L110). The route stays drawn. P0-14 says nothing and gives no buzz (P0-14 AC-26). Verify: unit.

AC-64: After `routing.browseAskS` (120 s; Blueprint DC23) of browsing, a small card at the bottom of the screen asks "Still going to ⟨destination⟩?" with Continue and Pause. ## 5. Scale targets

- **Graph size:** up to about 20,000 nodes per venue, for venues up to about 100,000 m² (PRD §7, "Scale").
- **Mall-scale proof:** the invented Aurora-sized seed, about 400 places on a cross-shaped galleria of five levels with two basements and a car park. It is published through P0-03 "so download, refresh and routing are proven at mall scale before the pilot" (L102; FEATURE_PIPELINE P0-02).
- **Routing latency [r3]:** a provisional target, by the owner's rule on Q10: **Spike 2's M5 p95 on the realme × 1.5, rounded up to one decimal of a millisecond** (R37), held in `perf.routeP95TargetMs` (AC-37). Its scope is the A\* search itself on about 20,000 nodes, judged under two option sets, Spike 2's (store shortcuts allowed) and the shopper's default (shortcuts off) (FF-4 plan 1.0 §14 (a)), the first route included once the router is warmed (AC-75). The origin snap, the points and directions, the isolate round trip and the step-free and avoid sets are recorded, not judged (AC-37). AC-34 compares Aurora's and the 20,000-node graph's p95 with it; a miss goes to the product owner, who decides whether it blocks (A5 of 2026-10-12). Spike 2's report (`phase-reports/feature-first/Spike-2.md`) exists on 2026-10-12 and is committed without the setting row. The number is filled in by the seed row in FF-4 (`db/seeds/lib/settings.js`), never in this spec (AC-37, EC-45; FF-4 plan 1.0 §14 (c)). The earlier "p95 ≤ 100 ms" was dropped (L64) and is not used.
- **Graph load time and memory:** not stated anywhere. Spike 2 measures graph load (M2, M4); no target.
- **Map frame times:** P0-04's, not this spec's. FF-4 records them on the realme as provisional figures (ruling A6).
- **r4's work per position update (off route, browsing, arrival, progress) and the time to derive directions, to find the ways around and to work a route out again:** not stated anywhere. Measured on the realme with Aurora and recorded, with no pass or fail (A5 of 2026-10-12: "where nothing states a number, measure and record only"). Working a route out again is a routing query, so AC-37's target applies to its search.
- **Provisional numbers used (L117 ruling 4):**
  - *[r5]* The connector costs are P0-03 r4's and the `ConnectorType` rows' (L170 Q4), not this spec's. The L91 metre costs that r2 to r4 listed here are retired.
  - `routing.stairsSaveS` = 120 s (L170 Q4; until r4 `routing.stairsSaveM` = 144, the demo's value, L99). It is a setting, not a measurement.
  - `routing.arriveM` 2.0 and `routing.arriveS` 2, the spike's starting point (PRD §6.6).

## 7. Design choices (not requirements; each with an alternative)

- **DC-1 Multi-door destinations:** one search to the set of the place's door nodes. *Alternative:* one search per door, keeping the cheapest; better if places have very few doors and a per-door estimate is much tighter.
- **DC-2 Tie-break:** among equal costs, the lower node index wins. *Alternative:* fewer edges wins; better if the shopper-visible "fewer turns" mattered for equal metres.
- **DC-3 Tolerance:** *[r5 · FF-4]* costs, in seconds, compare within **0.001 s** (until r4: 0.001 m, the database's `DECIMAL(9,3)` resolution). AC-36's consistency check and AC-11's "equal" use the same tolerance. It is far below anything a shopper could notice, and it holds only if P0-03 r4 stores each edge's cost to at least that resolution (a requirement on P0-03 r4, §2). *Alternative:* integer milliseconds per edge in the package, the same tolerance by construction; better if Spike 2's format favours integer edges for size. Or 0.01 s: looser, better if costs are stored as float32 and rounding noise exceeds 0.001 s on long routes.
- **DC-4 Load check:** the worker refuses a graph with a negative or non-finite cost or a broken CSR, rather than routing on it. *Alternative:* trust P0-03's validation only; acceptable if the package's SHA-256 is the only corruption risk.
- **DC-5 Middle edge:** edge ⌊n/2⌋ (counting from 0) of the lead way's n edges. *Alternative:* the lead way's longest edge; better if the middle edge often sits in a corridor with no parallel.
- **DC-6 Superseding queries:** a newer query replaces a pending one. *Alternative:* queue every query; better if results had to be audited in order (they do not).
- **DC-7 Vector file:** JSON in the repository, read by the Dart tests and by any later server router. *Alternative:* Dart-only fixtures, which fail invariant 6's "shared".
- **DC-8 The leg list:** in FF-4 the sheet lists legs: walk N m, "Walk through ⟨store⟩ (N m)", "Take ⟨connector⟩ up/down to ⟨level⟩". Approved with r1 Q2, option 1. From FF-6 the direction list replaces it (DC-21). *Alternative:* no step list until landmark-first directions; L92's store-crossing step would then not be shown in FF-4.
- **DC-9 "Levels apart" counts level ordinals** (AC-36), the per-level shape A7 keeps. *Alternative:* the levels' height difference in metres (*[r5]* the document level's `elevation`, OA-1 §2.4; until r4 `ElevationM`, FF-1 ruling B6), with b per metre climbed. That keeps the level term across buildings whose ordinals do not line up (V-RG2-6), and is better if such venues prove slow in AC-34's times. Both are never above the true cost.
- **DC-10 *[r5 · FF-4]* `a` is not capped.** Until r4, `a` was capped at 1 (FF-4 plan 1.0 §6.2, `min(1, …)`): a walkway's metre cost was its length, never below its straight-line run, so the cap only mattered in a venue with no walkway run. In seconds the 1 has no meaning, and a cap at 1 would be a number this spec would have to justify from a walking rate it does not state. Without it, `a` is exactly the package's smallest cost per metre of run, tight and still consistent (AC-36); with no edge having a run, `a = 0`. FF-4's plan 1.0 §6.2 names the cap and is revised to 1.1. *Alternative:* keep a cap at a named constant; equally correct, never tighter, and it needs a value and a reason.
- **DC-11 A missing or invalid `routing.*` value in the package uses its platform default** (*[r5]* 120 for `routing.stairsSaveS`; until r4 144 for `routing.stairsSaveM`; for the other-way margin of AC-13 the default P0-03 r4 sets; AC-70's defaults for r4's keys), the same rule P0-04 applies to its positive-number keys in the mall list (P0-04 AC-60, as narrowed by residual sheet R22). *Alternative:* treat it as an unreadable package (EC-3, routing unavailable); stricter, better if a missing setting should block routing as a corrupt graph does.
- **DC-12 A new Start-here origin during a shown route works the route out again from it**, as AC-26 does for an option. From FF-6, P0-08 AC-23 makes the same rule for every new position, which replaces this choice's Start-here case. *Alternative:* ignore Start here until ✕; simpler, but it slows testing the tool's snap. The tool is internal only (A1).
- **DC-13 [r3] Accepted (R43): the target's key is `perf.routeP95TargetMs`, a platform `GlobalSetting` that no phone reads.** The product owner accepted the name on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R43). "A setting" is the owner's ruling; the key name and where it lives are this choice. A `routing.*` name was not used because P0-03 copies every `routing.*` key into the package (AC-72), where it would serve no one. *Alternative:* a named constant in the timing harness, beside the Spike 2 report; simpler, but it is not a setting, which the ruling asks for.
- **DC-14 [r3] The closed-passage reasons list the closed stores in the order the package lists the place's passages, joined "A and B" or "A, B and C"; "way out" covers a trip from a place behind closed passages; when both ends are behind closed passages, the origin's reason is given.** The ruling worded the one-store "out" case and the two-store "in" case; the two-store "out" case and these orders follow the same mirror. *Alternative:* the generic "unreachable from here" for every case the ruling did not word; it says less about why.
- **DC-15 [r3] Ruled (R37): the target is rounded up to one decimal of a millisecond.** The product owner ruled this on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R37), replacing r3's whole-millisecond rounding. It is a mechanical rule, so filling it in needs no judgement, and the setting holds text, so a decimal fits (AC-37). *Not taken:* the next whole millisecond. It is equally mechanical, but at the measured scale it is about a quarter looser (the sheet's reason).
- **DC-16 [r4] Directions are derived in the worker with the route and returned in the result** (AC-27), because the fork test needs the graph's adjacency. *Alternative:* derive them in the UI isolate from the result's points; possible only if the result also carried each bend point's other walkways.
- **DC-17 [r4] Turn thresholds as named constants:** a 3 m minimum segment, slight below 50°, sharp from 120°, a fork within 10° of the way's bend, and one ride per connector, the reference's values (`directions.dart`; read, not copied). PRD §6.6 states only 30° and 30–50°. *Alternative:* `routing.*` settings; better if malls' corridors call for tuning.
- **DC-18 [r4] Landmarks within 12 m of the turn, passing within 5 m of a door,** the reference's values as named constants. Closed stores, the destination, entrances and connector stops are not landmarks; on a tie the place with the lower id wins (*[r5]* ids are strings: code-unit order, DC-39). *Alternative:* prefer anchors (named landmarks) over stores at equal reach; better if malls name many landmarks.
- **DC-19 [r4] One progress function, shared with P0-13's route snap** (P0-13 AC-24), so the drawn dot and the countdown never disagree. *Alternative:* a separate projection in P0-06; simpler now, but two answers to "where on the route am I".
- **DC-20 [r4] Card wording:** "Turn left / right", "Bear left / right" for slight, "Turn sharp left / right", "Take ⟨connector⟩ up / down to ⟨level⟩", "Walk through ⟨store⟩", "Go outside" / "Go inside", "Cross to ⟨building⟩", "Arrive at ⟨place⟩"; "in N m" from 3 m, "now" below, the reference's threshold. The words are English ARB text, translated by P0-14. *Alternative:* the metres always shown, never "now".
- **DC-21 [r4] The direction list replaces the leg list in the steps state.** *Alternative:* show both; longer, with each ride and store crossing listed twice.
- **DC-22 [r4] The landmark label comes third, after "via" and "goes outside",** keeping r2's approved order and naming the first differing place along the way. Blueprint §4.2 lists the landmark before "goes outside". *Alternative:* the Blueprint's order; better if shoppers find a landmark more telling than going outdoors.
- **DC-23 [r4] Avoid is strict over Prefer stairs; the say-so names stairs first; the other way under the say-so uses the same relaxed options**, as the reference does. *Alternative:* no other way under the say-so; simpler, but it hides a second choice when one exists.
- **DC-24 [r4] Ways around:** an 80% edge-set overlap drops a way, at most 8 rounds, "Same length" within 0.5 m; the stretch skips the edge the shopper is on and the destination's door edge. The reference's values and rules. Blueprint §4.2 gives "too much" without a figure. *Alternative:* overlap measured in metres; better if short edges skew the count.
- **DC-25 [r4] A similar place or "Get help" starts its route without asking "End your route to ⟨place⟩?"**, because the shopper chose it inside the trip's own sheet. Ruling B4's question governs routes started from Places, the chips and search (P0-05). *Alternative:* ask as B4 does; one more tap, and consistent everywhere.
- **DC-26 [r4] The information desk is the amenity type `INFO`** (the seeded platform type, `db/seeds/lib/lookups.js`), named in the constants file with PRD §6.6 as its authority; "Get help" is hidden when none is reachable. *Alternative:* a per-venue setting naming the help point; better if malls use other types for help.
- **DC-27 [r4] The trip avoidance is a union and is dropped for a search that would otherwise find no way.** *Alternative:* keep it strictly and show no route; truer to "keeps the avoidance", but it can strand a shopper who wandered behind the avoided stretch.
- **DC-28 [r4] Falling progress shows the notice without working the route out again,** because L110 lets the route change only after a confirmed wrong turn. *Alternative:* re-route too; better if falling progress is in practice always a wrong turn.
- **DC-29 [r4] Off-route distance is measured to the way on the position's own level; another level counts as beyond; nothing is counted while riding.** *Alternative:* a 3D distance; the level would then matter less than a few metres of floor.
- **DC-30 [r4] The wrong-way notice stays until 3 updates fall within the limit, a new route replaces it, or the shopper dismisses it.** *Alternative:* a fixed time; simpler, but it may vanish before the shopper looks.
- **DC-31 [r4] The browsing card is asked once per store visit; "towards the way" is a falling distance over 3 updates.** *Alternative:* ask again every `routing.browseAskS`; more reminders, more interruption.
- **DC-32 [r4] Pause on the browsing card calls P0-14's Pause through an interface faked in FF-6.** *Alternative:* the card shows Continue only until FF-7; less work in FF-6, but not L110's card.
- **DC-33 [r4] Accepted (R43): key names `routing.avoidAheadM`, `routing.arriveM`, `routing.arriveS` and `routing.progressFallM`.** The product owner accepted them as named on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R43). The browsing window's key is named by FF-6's plan (R38). The Blueprint names the others. All are `routing.*`, so they travel in the package (A3) and work offline. *Alternative:* other names; or non-`routing.*` names sent in the mall list, which would make guidance depend on a list read.
- **DC-34 [r4] "I've arrived" is accepted wherever the position is.** *Alternative:* only within some distance of the destination; it would refuse a shopper whose hand-set position is stale.
- **DC-35 [r4] The browsing time and the arrival dwell count foreground time only** (invariant 5). *Alternative:* wall-clock time; the card could then appear at once on return.
- **DC-36 *[r5 · FF-4]* "Nearest reachable point" is the nearest by straight line on the goal's level (AC-76).** Office §11 says only "the reachable cell nearest the goal"; it does not define nearness. The rule is: among the nodes the search can reach that lie on a level holding a door of the destination, the one with the least straight-line distance in the venue frame to the nearest such door; ties go to the lower cost from the origin, then the lower node index. Same-level only, so a shopper is never sent to a floor that merely lies under the goal. No reachable node on the goal's level gives "unreachable from here". *Alternative 1:* nearest by straight-line distance over all levels, ranking a node on the floor above or below the goal; it can end a way on the wrong floor. *Alternative 2:* the reachable node with the least walking cost to the goal, by a reverse search from the doors; it is truer to walking, but it is undefined when the graph does not connect the goal at all (the main case), and needs a second search.
- **DC-37 *[r5 · FF-4]* The wording for a way that ends short (AC-76).** A line above the ways, "There's no way into ⟨place⟩ from here. This way takes you as close as it can."; for an end node equal to the origin, "You're as close to ⟨place⟩ as the map can take you."; from FF-6, the last direction "This is as close as you can get to ⟨place⟩" and the arrival line equal to the origin form. The shopper-facing sentences are this spec's DESIGN CHOICE, because no source words them; they are English ARB text, translated by P0-14 (A4). No other way is offered for a way that ends short, because its comparison with the lead (AC-13) has no meaning. *Alternative:* a single neutral line "Closest point we can reach"; shorter, but it hides why the way stops. *Alternative for the other way:* offer one anyway when it also ends short at a different node; more choice, more screens, no source asks for it.
- **DC-38 *[r5 · FF-4]* Minutes are the cost in seconds ÷ 60, rounded up to whole minutes (Simple) or shown to one decimal (Technical); under 60 s it reads "< 1 min".** This keeps the reference's formatter rules (AC-10) and changes only the input. "About" prefixes Simple only, as L170 Q4 words the shopper's view ("About N min"). *Alternative:* round to the nearest minute; closer to the figure, but "About 0 min" for a 29 s walk.
- **DC-39 *[r5 · FF-4]* Where r2 to r4 broke a tie on "the lower id" (DC-18's landmarks), ids are strings, so it is the lower in code-unit order** (a byte comparison of the id text, case-sensitive). Graph tie-breaks stay on the integer node index (DC-2). *Alternative:* the element's position in the document's array, which the validator never reorders (OA-1 AC-12); it needs the package to carry that position, and P0-03 r4 would have to add it.
- **DC-40 *[r5 · FF-5]* A parking area with no entry point gives no route and does not fall under AC-76** (AC-74): AC-76 needs a goal node to be near, and the area has none. *Alternative:* use the area's outline's nearest walkway node as the goal; it routes somewhere, but it would invent an entry the venue never drew.
- **DC-41 *[r5 · FF-4]* The stairs rule compares two ways only when both reach the destination** (AC-16, AC-17). A no-stairs way that ends short never leads over a stairs way that reaches the goal; with no no-stairs way reaching it, the stairs way leads (AC-17). *Alternative:* let the no-stairs way lead anyway when the stairs way is much costlier; it would send the shopper to the wrong place to save seconds.

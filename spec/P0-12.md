## 1. What this delivers

A mall admin opens a level of a venue in the admin page and sees two things:
- **The estimate (L95):** a rule-of-thumb beacon count and cost for that level. It shows even before the level is drawn.
- **Three plans (L40):** minimum proximity, recommended tracking and budget-capped. Each shows the beacon positions, mount heights, count, estimated cost, coverage and GDOP heat-maps, and the estimate beside it as a sanity check.

The admin chooses one plan. The chosen plan's new positions are written to the beacon registry (`Beacon` rows) for installation (L40). Every count is labelled a budgeting figure until a walk survey of the installed floor confirms it (L96).

**The beacon list (A20).** A plain list of a level's beacons, where the admin activates a beacon once it is installed, records its "Installed on" date, corrects its position, and deactivates a beacon activated by mistake (R20). Without it nothing could activate a planned beacon (A20's gap).

The planner assumes no buyer: D8, who owns and pays for the beacons, is held until after the app is built (L114). Cost is shown as a figure, with no owner attached.

## 3. Acceptance criteria

Terms used below:
- **Level model:** the draft rows of one `Level`, as stored now. It is not a published version (DC-2). It contains:
  - its units, openings, floor objects, connector ends and lift stops;
  - its `NavCorrection` rows of kind `BLOCK_AREA`;
  - its `CeilingHeightM`, `WidthM` and `DepthM`;
  - its active `Beacon` rows (the existing beacons, H6).
- **Walkable area:** the level's walkable area exactly as P0-03 AC-24 derives it, from the same code (DC-3).
- **Wall:** as P0-03 defines it (ruled D4). Each edge of a non-walkable unit's outline is a wall, except where one of that unit's openings sits on it.
- **Cell:** a square of the level's `GridCellM` over the walkable area (DC-4).
- **Hears:** the RF model's RSSI for a beacon at a cell is at or above the heard threshold (AC-13).
- **Connector landing:** a `NavConnector` end (From or To) or a `NavConnectorStop` on the level. Evacuation-only connectors are included (DC-11).
- **Entrance:** an `Opening` on the level with `IsVenueEntrance = TRUE`.
- **Existing beacon:** an active `Beacon` row on the level (H6).
- **Corridor unit:** a walkable unit of IMDF category `walkway` or `footbridge` (H12). Every other walkable unit is an **open-area unit**.

### 3.1 The estimate (L95, L96)

- AC-1: For one level, the estimate is N = max(`planner.minPerFloor`, ⌈A × k / s²⌉ + E). The inputs are:
  - s, the spacing for the accuracy tier: Room 15 m, Standard 10 m, High 6.5 m;
  - k, the wall factor: Open 1.0, Partitioned 1.3, Heavy 1.8;
  - E, the decision points;
  - the minimum, 4.

  These are L95's values. Each one is a `planner.*` setting with that value as its default (C8; H11). Worked examples:
  - L94's penthouse figures (A = 75.95 m², Standard, Partitioned, E = 4) give ⌈0.98735⌉ + 4 = 5;
  - A = 10 m² with E = 0 gives 4.

  Verify: unit (a table of cases including these two).
- AC-2: **The area A (L95, L96).**
  - While the level has no walkable unit, A is `WidthM × DepthM`.
  - Once it has one, A is the area of the walkable area. Corridors are counted by area like any walkable unit (L96 ruling 1).

  The response names the source used: "level size" or "drawn walkable area". Verify: unit and API.
- AC-3: **The decision points E (L95; H2).** E is the sum of:
  - the level's entrances;
  - its connector landings;
  - its corridor junctions: each place on the drawn level where the outlines of three or more walkable units touch or overlap. One such place counts once, however many units meet there (DC-17). No graph is read and no publish is needed (H2).

  The response gives each of the three counts. Verify: unit (fixtures: a T of three corridor units is 1; a cross of four is 1; two separate T's are 2; two units that meet are 0).
- AC-4: **Exact arithmetic.** A is rounded to 0.01 m², and the formula is computed with `decimal.js`. Floating-point error cannot round an exact quotient up: A = 100, Standard, Open gives 1 + E, never 2 + E. Verify: unit.
- AC-5: **Cost (H4; A23; N8).**
  - The estimate's cost is N × the unit price, computed with `decimal.js` and returned as a decimal string with four places (rule 1; invariant 11). Cost is units × unit price only: no install or battery cost (H4).
  - The unit price is the one the admin enters in the planner's inputs. It is prefilled from the chosen `BeaconModel.UnitPrice` when that is set (A23). The seed's MOKO H2 has no price and gets none (N8: the admin types a price per plan).
  - With no unit price, the count is shown and the cost reads "No price set". The count is never withheld.

  Verify: unit and API.
- AC-6: **The tier and the wall type** are chosen by the admin for each estimate. They default to Standard and to the wall type that `rf.wallLossDb` falls in (A18): under 2 dB Open, up to 6 dB Partitioned, above that Heavy. With the spike's 3 dB (AC-13), the default is Partitioned, as in L94's reference build (DC-16). Verify: unit and widget.
- AC-7: **No write.** Computing the estimate writes no row. When plans are generated, the estimate's inputs and result are saved in each plan's `InputsJson` (L95: "its inputs live in `BeaconPlan.InputsJson`"). Verify: integration (row counts before and after an estimate).
- AC-8: **Where it shows (L95).** The estimate shows on the level's planner screen:
  - before the level is drawn (a level with no units);
  - beside each plan, for the inputs that plan was generated with.

  Every count shown, the estimate's and each plan's, carries the line "Budgeting figure: confirm with a walk survey of the installed floor before ordering" (L96 ruling 3). Verify: widget.

### 3.2 Generating plans (L40 steps 1 to 8; L45)

- AC-9: A generate request for an indoor level of the request's tenant writes, in one transaction:
  - one `BeaconPlan` row each of `PlanKind` `MIN_PROXIMITY` and `RECOMMENDED`, and one of `BUDGET_CAP` when a unit price and a budget cap are given (H5, R16), each with `Status = 'PROPOSED'`;
  - their `BeaconPlanItem` rows.

  With no unit price, no `BUDGET_CAP` row is written, and the budget plan's card says "Enter a unit price to plan to a budget" (H5). With a price but no cap, no `BUDGET_CAP` row is written, and the card says "Enter a budget cap to plan to a budget"; the other two plans still generate (R16). The request waits until the plans are written (DC-1). A failure part-way writes nothing. Verify: integration (including an injected fault after the second plan, a run with no price that writes two plans, and a run with a price but no cap that writes two plans).
- AC-10: **Inputs, recorded (L95).** Each plan's `InputsJson` holds what produced it:
  - the tier, the wall type and the estimate (AC-1 to AC-5);
  - the beacon model's id, code and label, and the unit price used (or none);
  - the k values, the `rf.*` values and every `planner.*` value, as resolved for the tenant;
  - the existing beacons taken as fixed inputs: their ids, positions and mount heights (H6);
  - the cell size and the random seed (DC-5);
  - the level model's fingerprint (DC-7).

  Money in `InputsJson` is a decimal string, never a JSON number (rule 1). Verify: integration.
- AC-11: **Required beacons first (PRD §6.20: "Connector landings and entrances always get beacons"; L40 step 5).** Every plan, of all three kinds, holds:
  - at least one beacon at or beside each connector landing, with `PlacementReason = 'CONNECTOR'`;
  - one beacon just inside each entrance, with `PlacementReason = 'ENTRANCE'`;
  - one beacon just outside each entrance, with `PlacementReason = 'ENTRANCE'` (L40's inside/outside pair; H8). It is an item of the building level's plan, recorded on the building level and mounted on the outer face beside the door. If that spot falls outside the venue boundary, the beacon is left out and the plan warns "The outside beacon at ⟨entrance⟩ needs the venue boundary drawn around the entrance (map editor)" (R18; no migration).

  An existing beacon near the required spot counts as that required beacon, and no item is added for it (DC-18, accepted R17). How many go at a landing is DC-11. Verify: unit (including an entrance on the building's edge whose outside spot falls beyond the derived rectangle: no outside item, the warning named).
- AC-12: **Mount height (L45; H3).** Each item's `MountHeightM` follows these rules:
  - When `CeilingHeightM` is below `planner.ceilingMountBelowM` (default 3, L45), the beacon is ceiling-mounted and `MountHeightM` = `CeilingHeightM`.
  - Otherwise `MountHeightM` is the lower of `CeilingHeightM` and `planner.maxMountM` (default 4, from L40's "3 to 4 meters").
  - With `CeilingHeightM` NULL, generating is refused with 409 "Set this level's ceiling height first", and nothing is written. The ceiling height is a level field (FF-1, B6). The estimate still shows.

  Verify: unit (ceilings of 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4 and 6 m give 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4 and 4) and API (NULL).
- AC-13: **The RF model (L40 step 4; A18).** The modelled RSSI at a cell is log-distance path loss from the RSSI at 1 m, less:
  - a loss for each wall crossed (walls as P0-03 D4);
  - the `SignalPenaltyDb` of each floor object crossed whose type has one (`ObjectType`, L40). A type with NULL adds nothing;
  - a body margin.

  The distance and the RSSI at 1 m follow DC-22. The numbers come from the shared `rf.*` group (A18). Their defaults are the spike's model values (gabay_spike `lib/venue_editor/editor_constants.dart` lines 75–88, the same values as `assets/venue/mezz.json` lines 13–24; numbers only, L34):

  | Meaning (key as P0-13 r2 names it) | Default | Note |
  |---|---|---|
  | RSSI at 1 m (`rf.rssiAt1mDbm`) | −55 dBm | The H2's measured power (C6) |
  | Path-loss exponent (`rf.pathLossExponent`) | 3.0 | |
  | Wall loss (`rf.wallLossDb`) | 3.0 dB | Also sets AC-6's default wall type |
  | Body margin (`rf.bodyMarginDb`) | 5.0 dB | L40's "about 5 dB" |
  | Heard threshold (`rf.heardThresholdDbm`) | −95 dBm | |
  | Phone height (P0-13's phone-height setting, F6) | 1.1 m | DC-22 |

  The seeded object types already carry the spike's per-kind losses: Pillar 20 dB, Shaft 20 dB, Furniture 1 dB (`db/seeds/lib/lookups.js` lines 54–56). With the defaults, a beacon at phone height is heard to 14.7 m in free space and to 11.7 m through one wall: L94's ranges (A18). The planner uses no per-phone offset (A18's offsets are the live engine's). The two dBm figures are stored and read as the real negative dBm the beacon datasheets use (−55, −95), under the validity rule P0-13 states for each `rf.*` key (R22; P0-13 AC-46). Verify: unit (fixtures: free space at 14.6 m heard and at 14.8 m not; one wall at 11.6 m heard and at 11.8 m not; one pillar).
- AC-14: **Candidate positions (L40 step 3).** Candidates lie on the faces where a beacon can be mounted (DC-10):
  - wall segments, sampled at `planner.candidateSpacingM`;
  - the faces of floor objects with `BlocksWalk`;
  - the outer edge of the walkable area.

  When the beacon is ceiling-mounted (AC-12), candidates also lie over the walkable area at that spacing. No candidate lies within `planner.liftClearM` of a lift's position (L40: "avoid … elevator shafts"). Every item lies inside `Venue.BoundaryGeoJson`, so publish's boundary check (P0-03 AC-16) never refuses a beacon the planner placed. Verify: unit.
- AC-15: **Recommended tracking (L40 steps 6 and 7; H12).** After the required beacons:
  - greedy k-coverage adds beacons until each cell hears at least k, counting the existing beacons (H6). k is `planner.kCorridor` (3) in cells of corridor units and `planner.kOpen` (4) in cells of open-area units. A cell inside both kinds is open area (DC-21);
  - then geometry refinement lowers the 95th-percentile GDOP and rejects collinear triples (DC-13).

  Where no available candidate can reach k at a cell, the plan is still produced. That cell is shown short, and `CoveragePct` is below 100. Verify: unit.
- AC-16: **Minimum proximity (H1).** The required beacons, then greedy cover adds the fewest beacons it finds until each walkable cell hears at least one, counting the existing beacons. Verify: unit.
- AC-17: **Budget-capped (H5).**
  - The count cap is ⌊`BudgetCap` ÷ unit price⌋, computed with `decimal.js`. It caps new beacons only: existing beacons cost nothing (H6).
  - The plan takes the required beacons, then adds greedily by the largest coverage gain until the cap, then refines as AC-15.
  - `BeaconPlan.BudgetCap` stores the cap the admin entered.
  - When the required beacons fit the cap, `EstimatedCost` ≤ `BudgetCap`.
  - When they do not, the plan holds the required beacons anyway, adds nothing more, and is marked "Over budget: the required beacons alone cost ₱X" (H5). `EstimatedCost` is then above `BudgetCap`, and the mark is derived from that, with no new column.
  - With no unit price, or with a price but no budget cap, the plan is not generated (AC-9; H5, R16).

  Verify: unit (a cap that fits, a cap below the required beacons' cost, a cap equal to it).
- AC-18: **Deterministic (DC-5).** The same level model, inputs and settings always give the same items in the same order, with the same figures. Verify: unit (generate twice and compare).
- AC-19: **Density check (L40 step 7; H10).** Each plan lists, as a warning, each walkable unit with fewer than one beacon per `planner.densityWarnM2` (default 100 m², L40) of its area, counting existing beacons. The warning never changes the plan, and there is no fourth plan (H10). Verify: unit.
- AC-20: **Nothing to plan.** A level with no walkable area cannot be planned. A generate request is refused with 409 "Draw this level's walkways first, then generate plans", and nothing is written. The estimate still shows, from the level size (AC-2). Verify: API.
- AC-53: **Outdoor levels (H8; PRD §6.9: GPS outdoors).** An outdoor level (`IsOutdoor`) gets no coverage plan and no estimate. Its planner screen says "Outdoor levels get no beacon plan: phones use GPS outdoors". A generate request for it is refused with 409 and that message, and nothing is written. Verify: API (Aurora's outdoor ground level) and widget.
- AC-54: **Existing beacons are fixed inputs (H6).**
  - Every plan starts from the level's existing beacons. They count toward coverage, GDOP, k and density, and they are never moved or repeated.
  - Plan items are the new beacons only. Each plan shows "N new + M existing".
  - An inactive beacon is not an existing beacon, whether or not it has an "Installed on" date (EC-39). One that has an "Installed on" date is kept on a new choice (AC-30; this reading of H6 confirmed, R19).

  Verify: unit (a fixture with two existing beacons: they appear in the heat-maps, are not items and are named in `InputsJson`).

### 3.3 Plan figures and heat-maps (L40; PRD §6.20)

- AC-21: Each `BeaconPlan` row has:
  - `BeaconCount` = its number of items, the new beacons (H6);
  - `EstimatedCost` = count × unit price (`DECIMAL(18,4)`, `decimal.js`), or NULL when no price is set (A23);
  - `P95Gdop` = the 95th percentile of the cells' GDOP over the cells that have a fix (DC-12), with the existing beacons included;
  - `CoveragePct` = the share of cells that meet the plan's target: at least one beacon heard for minimum proximity, and k for the other two (DC-12), with the existing beacons included.

  Verify: integration (the figures are recomputed from the items and the existing beacons in the test).
- AC-22: **Coverage heat-map.** Each cell is coloured by the number of beacons it hears, in L94's bands: 0–1, 2, 3 or more. A legend names the bands. Verify: unit (cell counts) and widget.
- AC-23: **GDOP heat-map (H9).**
  - Each cell that hears at least three beacons is coloured by its GDOP: good up to `planner.gdopGoodMax` (default 2), fair above that up to `planner.gdopFairMax` (default 5), poor above that. Both are provisional settings, replaced after Spike 1 (H9).
  - A cell with fewer than three is shown as "No fix".
  - A legend names the bands.

  Verify: unit (GDOP of exactly 2 is good, exactly 5 is fair) and widget.
- AC-24: **Reading a plan.** A read route returns a plan with its items and both heat-maps for the level. The heat-maps are computed from the plan's items and the existing beacons over the current level model (DC-6).
  - If the level model's fingerprint differs from the one in `InputsJson`, the response says `stale`.
  - The screen then shows "This level has changed since these plans were made. Generate them again." It keeps the stored figures, labelled "when generated", and draws no heat-maps.

  Verify: API and widget.

### 3.4 Keeping plans and choosing one (L40: "The admin picks one")

- AC-25: **The level's plans.** The planner screen lists, for the level:
  - the latest generated set;
  - the `SELECTED` plan, if any, when it is not in that set.

  Each shows its kind, "N new + M existing", cost, coverage, P95 GDOP, when it was generated and by whom. Verify: API and widget.
- AC-26: **A new set supersedes the old one (DC-8).** Generating sets every earlier `PROPOSED` plan of that level to `SUPERSEDED`, in the same transaction. A `SELECTED` plan stays `SELECTED`. Verify: integration.
- AC-27: **Choosing (DC-9).** Choosing a `PROPOSED` plan does the following in one transaction:
  - sets it to `SELECTED`;
  - sets the other plans of its set to `SUPERSEDED`;
  - sets any earlier `SELECTED` plan of the level to `SUPERSEDED`;
  - writes the registry rows (§3.5).

  At most one plan per level is ever `SELECTED`, including when two choices race. Verify: integration (two concurrent choices on one level: one succeeds and one gets 409).
- AC-28: **Refused choices.** Each of these answers 409 with its message and changes nothing:
  - choosing a `SUPERSEDED` plan: "These plans have been replaced. Generate them again.";
  - choosing the plan that is already `SELECTED`: "This plan is already chosen.";
  - choosing a stale plan (AC-24): "This level has changed since these plans were made. Generate them again." (DC-15).

  Verify: API.
- AC-29: **Plans are history.** `BeaconPlanItem` rows are never updated or deleted (the schema gives them `CreatedBy` only). `BeaconPlan` rows change only their `Status` and audit columns, and nothing deletes a plan. Verify: integration.

### 3.5 Feeding the beacon registry (L40; H6, A19, A20)

- AC-30: **One `Beacon` row per new item (H6; A20).** Choosing a plan writes one `Beacon` row per item, with these values:
  - `TenantId`, `VenueId` and `LevelId` from the plan;
  - `X`, `Y` and `MountHeightM` from the item;
  - `ProximityUuid` = the tenant's `BeaconProximityUuid` (invariant 9);
  - `Major` and `Minor` per AC-31;
  - `IdentityScheme = 'IBEACON_STATIC'` (D9, L114);
  - `BeaconModelId` = the plan's model;
  - `BeaconPlanItemId` = the item;
  - `MeasuredPower`, `InstalledOn` and `BatteryServicedOn` NULL;
  - `IsActive = FALSE` until the admin activates it in the beacon list (A20; AC-56).

  Existing beacons are not touched (H6). In the same transaction, every `Beacon` row on the level that came from an earlier plan (`BeaconPlanItemId` set), is inactive and has no `InstalledOn` is deleted: a previous choice's beacons not yet installed are removed (H6). Verify: integration (choose plan A, activate one of its beacons with a date, choose plan B: A's other beacons are gone, the activated one stays and was a fixed input of B).
- AC-31: **The per-floor identifier range (D9, L96, L114; A19).** Every beacon on a level shares one `Major`, so a phone can tell which floor a signal comes from:
  - the level's `Major` is the one its existing and remaining beacons use;
  - else it is the lowest `Major` from 1 that no other level of the tenant's namespace uses (DC-20);
  - `Minor`s are the lowest free numbers from 1 under that `Major` (DC-20; the seed numbers its beacons from 1).

  No two active or inactive beacons share (`ProximityUuid`, `Major`, `Minor`) (`UQ_Beacon_Identity`). The seed's per-venue Majors (penthouse 1, MEZZ 2, L160 C1) already meet this, because each has one level, and they are not re-allocated (A19). Publish's refusal of two levels sharing a `Major` is P0-03 r3's, built in this slice (A19). The live engine does not read floors from `Major` (A19). Verify: integration (a fixture with Major 1 in use on one level, and a second level that gets Major 2).
- AC-32: **No namespace, no beacons (H7).** If the tenant has no `BeaconProximityUuid` (Demo Malls, L119), choosing is refused with 409 "This tenant has no beacon namespace yet. A SuperAdmin assigns one." Nothing changes. Generating and reading plans still work. Verify: API (on Aurora).
- AC-33: **Identifiers exhausted.** Choosing is refused with 409 naming which ran out, and nothing changes, when:
  - the level's `Major` has no free `Minor` left for the plan's items (1 to 65,535 per `Major`, DC-20);
  - the namespace has no free `Major`;
  - the level's beacons already use more than one `Major`: "This level's beacons use Majors ⟨list⟩. One Major per level is needed." (DC-20).

  Verify: unit (allocator with injected used sets) and API (the two-Major case).
- AC-34: **The registry and publish (A20).** The new rows are listed in the level's beacon list (§3.9). Inactive rows stay out of every package, as P0-03's allow-list rules, so the level keeps P0-03's "No beacons on this level" warning (AC-22) until a beacon is activated (AC-56). Verify: integration (choose a plan on MEZZ, then publish: the package holds only the beacons that were already active; activate one, publish again: it is in the package).

## 4. Edge cases

Each edge case is covered by the criterion named, or has a one-line reason.

**Offline or stale**
- EC-1: The admin page is offline, or the API is down, when Generate, Choose, Activate or Save is pressed. The error shows with a retry, and nothing is written (AC-45, AC-9, AC-27, AC-56, AC-57).
- EC-2: The level is edited after its plans were made. The plans read as stale, with no heat-maps, and a stale plan cannot be chosen (AC-24, AC-28).
- EC-3: A setting (an `rf.*` value, k, a tier spacing, a GDOP band) changes after plans were made. The plans keep the values in their `InputsJson`. The fingerprint (DC-7) covers the planning settings, `rf.*` included, so they read as stale.
- EC-4: The planner works on the draft. A published version older than the draft does not affect it (DC-2). A package carries only active beacons (AC-34).

**Empty and maximum data**
- EC-5: A level with no units. The estimate shows from the level size, with E counting what exists (often 0), so it reads at least 4. Generating is refused (AC-2, AC-20).
- EC-6: A tiny level, such as one 3 × 3 m room with no entrance. The estimate is 4, the minimum (AC-1). The plans place what coverage needs.
- EC-7: A level with walkable area but no walls, pillars or other mount faces. Candidates fall on the walkable area's outer edge, or over the floor when the beacon is ceiling-mounted (AC-14, DC-10).
- EC-8: A level whose walkable area is in two parts that do not touch. Coverage is per cell, so both parts are covered. Reachability is publish's concern, not the planner's.
- EC-9: The largest level: cells beyond `planner.maxCells` are refused with the limit named (AC-52). PRD §7's 100,000 m² per site is measured (AC-52).
- EC-10: A level with many entrances and landings, so the required beacons alone exceed a budget cap. The budget plan holds them and is marked "Over budget" (AC-17, H5).
- EC-11: The identifiers run out (AC-33).
- EC-12: Very long model labels (100 characters) and non-ASCII names (ñ) in the plan card, the confirmation and the beacon list. Covered by widget fixtures (AC-42, AC-46, AC-55).

**Tenant boundary**
- EC-13: A venue, level, plan or beacon id from the other seeded tenant answers 404. A header naming a tenant the user lacks answers 403 (AC-35, AC-38).
- EC-14: A beacon model that is another tenant's own row is refused as a reference (AC-39; Blueprint DC8).
- EC-15: Two tenants' beacons never collide. Each tenant has its own UUID (invariant 9), and the database enforces uniqueness (AC-31).
- EC-16: Demo Malls, which has no namespace, can plan but cannot choose (AC-32, H7). Its beacon lists are empty (AC-55).

**Each role, and permission denied**
- EC-17: MALL_ADMIN uses everything (the stub's default, L154). VIEWER is read-only in the client now and on the server from R2. VENUE_EDITOR has neither module now and gets 403 from R2. SUPERADMIN acting as a tenant is R1/R2. None of these is tested on the server in FF-11 (E-20); each is on the ledger (AC-36, AC-37).
- EC-18: A missing venue grant is R1's check, which the stub does not do. It is on the ledger.

**Concurrent edits and publish in progress**
- EC-19: Two admins generate for one level at once. Both sets are written, and the later one supersedes the earlier (AC-26). Both lock the level row, so the two sets do not interleave (DC-9).
- EC-20: Two admins choose on one level at once. One succeeds and the other gets 409 (AC-27).
- EC-21: One admin generates while another chooses from the previous set. The level lock orders them. If the choice runs first, the new set then supersedes the remaining `PROPOSED` plans. If generation runs first, the choice meets a `SUPERSEDED` plan and gets 409 (AC-26, AC-28).
- EC-22: A publish of the venue is `BUILDING` while plans are generated or chosen, or a beacon is activated or moved. Each proceeds. Publish reads its own snapshot (P0-03 AC-8), so beacon rows written mid-build land in the next version, and inactive rows never land at all (AC-34).
- EC-23: An editor changes the level while plans are being generated. The plans carry the fingerprint of the model they read in one transaction (DC-7), so they read as stale at once if the edit landed first.
- EC-24: Someone tries to delete a level that has plans or beacons. FF-1's delete refuses with 409, naming what uses the level (B4). `BeaconPlan` references the level, so FF-1's foreign-key noun table, built from `schema.sql`, needs a "beacon plans" noun. That is flagged to the plan.
- EC-40: Two admins activate, deactivate or edit one beacon at once. The later save gets 409 from the `UpdatedAt` check (B5; AC-57, AC-59).
- EC-41: An admin activates a beacon from plan A while another chooses plan B on the same level. The choose transaction locks the level row (DC-9), and activation takes the same lock (DC-19), so the order is clear. If the activation runs first, plan B was generated before it, so B is stale and the choice is refused (AC-28). If the choice runs first, the beacon has been removed and activation answers 404.

**Invalid input and bad data**
- EC-25: Malformed ids, tiers, wall types, decimals or dates answer 400 (AC-39).
- EC-26: `CeilingHeightM` is NULL. Generating is refused with the line to set it (AC-12, H3). A ceiling below 3 m mounts on the ceiling, and a ceiling above 4 m mounts at 4 m (AC-12).
- EC-27: The beacon model has no `UnitPrice` (the seed's MOKO H2 has none, N8). The unit price field starts empty. Without a typed price, cost reads "No price set" and the budget plan is not generated (AC-5, AC-9).
- EC-28: An inactive beacon model is chosen for new plans. Refused (AC-39, AC-41).
- EC-29: A level with `NeedsReview` elements (MEZZ, the penthouse). Planning proceeds on the draft, and the screen notes "N elements not yet reviewed: plans may change" (DC-2).
- EC-30: An outdoor level (`IsOutdoor`). No plan and no estimate (AC-53, H8).
- EC-31: A level that already has beacons (the penthouse's five H2s, MEZZ's five). They are fixed inputs (AC-54, H6).
- EC-32: A lift whose stops are on this level. Its stop is a landing (AC-11), and no candidate lies within `planner.liftClearM` of it (AC-14).
- EC-33: Evacuation-only stairs. Their landings count and get beacons (DC-11).
- EC-34: Coordinates and money arrive from `pg` as strings. Geometry is computed from parsed numbers and money with `decimal.js`, never by string comparison and never as floats (AC-4, AC-5).
- EC-37: An existing beacon sits at a connector landing. It counts as that landing's required beacon, and no item is added there (AC-11, DC-18).
- EC-38: A plan is chosen, some of its beacons are activated, then another plan is chosen. The activated beacons stay and were fixed inputs of the new plan. The others are removed (AC-30, H6).
- EC-39: An inactive beacon that has an "Installed on" date. It is neither a fixed input nor removed on a new choice (H6's words: removal is of beacons "not yet installed"). It stays in the list until someone activates it (AC-54, AC-30).
- EC-42: A level whose beacons already use two `Major`s (only possible through data loaded outside the planner). Choosing is refused with the Majors named (AC-33, DC-20).
- EC-43: A position edit outside the venue boundary answers 400. A position inside the boundary but outside the level's walkable area or rectangle is accepted, because beacons mount on outer walls (AC-57).
- EC-44: "Installed on" in the future answers 400. Today and earlier are accepted (AC-39, DC-19).
- EC-45: A cell inside both a corridor unit and an open-area unit needs k = 4 (AC-15, DC-21).
- EC-46: A price is typed but no budget cap. No budget plan; its card says "Enter a budget cap to plan to a budget"; the other two plans generate (AC-9, R16).
- EC-47: An entrance whose outside spot falls outside the venue boundary, which, until FF-10's drawn boundary, is a rectangle over the largest level (P0-01). The outside beacon is left out and the plan warns that the boundary must be drawn around the entrance in the map editor; no item is written beyond the boundary (AC-11, AC-14, R18).
- EC-48: A seeded active beacon with no "Installed on" (every seeded beacon). It shows "Not recorded", stays active and is a fixed input. Its date can be set in an edit (AC-55, AC-57).
- EC-49: Deactivate on a beacon that is already inactive answers 409 "This beacon is not active." and changes nothing (AC-59).
- EC-50: The level's last active beacon is deactivated. The next publish carries no beacon for the level, and P0-03's "No beacons on this level" warning returns (AC-59, AC-34). A seeded beacon deactivated by mistake (no "Installed on") is reactivated through Activate, which then asks for its date (AC-56).

**Rule-specific**
- EC-35: Money (rule 1). `BudgetCap` and `EstimatedCost` are `DECIMAL(18,4)`, with `decimal.js` on the server, decimal strings in JSON and in `InputsJson`, and no `double` on the client (AC-5, AC-10, AC-48).
- EC-36: Option lists (rule 5). `PlanKind`, `Status` and `PlacementReason` are already closed sets with CHECKs (Blueprint invariant 14). The tiers and wall types are two closed sets in the constants file that FF-5 creates (A26), with L95 as their authority. Their numbers are `planner.*` settings (H11).

## 7. Design choices in this spec

DC-1 to DC-16 were accepted on 2026-10-12 (A7, L165). DC-17 to DC-22, new in r2, were accepted on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R17, ruling Q22). Each fills a gap no ruling covers and names an alternative that would also satisfy what is stated.

- **DC-1** The generate request waits until the plans are written, as publish does (P0-03 D3). *Alternative:* a background job with polling, which is better if AC-52's measurements show it takes too long.
- **DC-2** The planner works on the level's draft rows, `NeedsReview` elements included, with a note on screen. *Alternative:* plan only on the latest published version, or refuse while anything is unreviewed. Both would block MEZZ and the penthouse until FF-10's review.
- **DC-3** The walkable area and the walls come from P0-03's own code (AC-24, D4), so the planner and publish never disagree about the floor. *Alternative:* the planner's own derivation, counting store interiors too.
- **DC-4** The planner's cell is the level's `GridCellM`. *Alternative:* a separate `planner.cellM` setting.
- **DC-5** Refinement uses a random generator seeded from `InputsJson`, so the result is repeatable. *Alternative:* unseeded, with only the plans' properties tested.
- **DC-6** Heat-maps are recomputed when a plan is read, not stored. *Alternative:* store each grid as a file. That needs a new `StoredFile.Purpose` value, which means a migration.
- **DC-7** The fingerprint is the SHA-256 of the level model rows (existing beacons included, r2) and the planning settings (`rf.*` included, r2) the planner reads, in a fixed order. *Alternative:* compare `UpdatedAt` timestamps, which misses a deleted row.
- **DC-8** A new set supersedes the level's earlier `PROPOSED` plans. *Alternative:* keep every set `PROPOSED` until one is chosen.
- **DC-9** "One `SELECTED` per level" and the ordering of generate and choose are held by locking the level row (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, as FF-1 locks the venue, §G4). *Alternative:* a partial unique index on `(TenantId, LevelId) WHERE Status = 'SELECTED'`, which needs a migration.
- **DC-10** Candidate mount faces are walls, blocking floor objects and the walkable area's outer edge, sampled at a spacing setting. *Alternative:* a uniform grid of candidates everywhere.
- **DC-11** One required beacon per connector landing, evacuation-only included, and one inside each entrance. *Alternative:* two per landing, the top of L40's "1–2".
- **DC-12** GDOP is the 2D figure from the beacons a cell hears. A cell hearing fewer than three has no fix: it counts against `CoveragePct` and is left out of `P95Gdop`. *Alternative:* count no-fix cells as infinite GDOP, so P95 is undefined whenever more than 5% of cells are short.
- **DC-13** A triple is collinear when its triangle's area is below `planner.collinearMinAreaM2`. *Alternative:* the smallest angle below a set number of degrees.
- **DC-14** Choosing asks for confirmation, because it writes registry rows. *Alternative:* no dialog, as P0-03's publish has none.
- **DC-15** A stale plan cannot be chosen. *Alternative:* allow it with a warning.
- **DC-16** The estimate's default tier and wall type follow L94's reference build (Standard; the wall type from the wall loss, now `rf.wallLossDb` by A18). *Alternative:* no default, so the admin must pick.
- **DC-17** *(new in r2; Accepted, R17)* A corridor junction (H2) is each connected place where the outlines of three or more walkable units touch or overlap, counted once however many units meet there. *Alternative:* count every set of three units that meet, so a cross of four corridors counts as 4. That inflates E the same way H2 meant to avoid.
- **DC-18** *(new in r2; Accepted, R17)* An existing beacon counts as a landing's or an entrance's required beacon when it lies within `planner.candidateSpacingM` of the spot the planner would choose for it. *Alternative:* always add the required beacons, even beside an existing one. That is simpler but places beacons the penthouse and MEZZ do not need.
- **DC-19** *(new in r2; Accepted, R17)* The beacon list's details:
  - Activate needs an "Installed on" date, today or earlier in Philippine time, and an active beacon's date cannot be cleared. Seeded beacons without a date stay as they are.
  - A position edit keeps the level and must lie inside the venue boundary.
  - Activation and edits lock the level row, as choosing does (DC-9).
  - The menu label is "Beacons" until sentinels are built.

  *Alternative:* Activate with no date, positions anywhere with publish's boundary check catching them, and the label "Beacons and sentinels" from the start.
- **DC-20** *(new in r2; Accepted, R17)* Identifiers are allocated from 1, Majors and Minors alike, as the seed numbers them. A level whose beacons already use more than one `Major` cannot take a choice. *Alternative:* allocate from 0 (the schema allows it), and use the level's lowest `Major` when it has several.
- **DC-21** *(new in r2; Accepted, R17)* A cell inside both a corridor unit and an open-area unit needs k = 4, the stricter. *Alternative:* corridor wins, k = 3.
- **DC-22** *(new in r2; Accepted, R17)* Two RF details:
  - The distance is the straight line from the beacon at its mount height to a phone at the phone height, the spike model's form. L94's ranges hold when the two heights are equal (AC-13).
  - An existing beacon's own `MeasuredPower`, when set, is its RSSI at 1 m. Otherwise, and for every new beacon, `rf.rssiAt1mDbm` is used. The live engine reads the package's measured power the same way (P0-13 AC-38).

  *Alternative:* horizontal distance only, which ignores L45's mount heights, and always the default RSSI at 1 m.

## 9. Questions ruled

All twenty were ruled on 2026-10-12, every one as recommended, in `plan/FF-5-11-decisions.md` (L165). The sheet item is named for each.

**Q1. What is the "minimum proximity" plan?** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H1):** (a) the required beacons, then greedy cover until every walkable cell hears at least one beacon. *Not chosen:* (b) the required beacons only; (c) one beacon at each place's door. Applied in AC-16.

**Q2. How are "corridor junctions" counted for E?** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H2):** (a) the places where three or more walkable units meet on the drawn level, with no graph and no publish. *Not chosen:* (b) degree-3 nodes in the published graph after merging; (c) the admin types the number. Applied in AC-3 and AC-51, with DC-17.

**Q3. Where do the RF model's numbers come from?** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (A18):** one shared `rf.*` group, created in FF-9 and read by the live engine and the planner. Its values are the spike's model values as cited numbers, no code (L34), and must reproduce L94's 14.7 m and 11.7 m. This replaces r1's separate `planner.*` RF keys. *Not chosen:* (b) the owner names the numbers; (c) wait for Spike 1; the sheet's alternative of separate `planner.*` and `dot.*` keys. Applied in §2, AC-6, AC-13 and DC-22.

**Q4. Mount height at 3 m or higher, and when the ceiling is not set.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H3):** (a) the lower of `CeilingHeightM` and `planner.maxMountM` (4 m); a NULL ceiling refuses generation until it is set. *Not chosen:* (b) a 3 m default with a warning; (c) always the ceiling height. Applied in AC-12.

**Q5. Where does the unit price come from before R11?** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (A23; §M N8):** (a) a unit-price field in the planner's inputs, prefilled from `BeaconModel.UnitPrice` when it has one, recorded in `InputsJson`; the MOKO H2 gets no seeded price, so the admin types one per plan. *Not chosen:* (b) a seeded price; (c) a maintenance screen pulled forward. Applied in AC-5, AC-43 and EC-27.

**Q6. What does "estimated cost" include?** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H4):** (a) units × unit price only. *Not chosen:* (b) install and yearly battery cost too. Applied in AC-5 and §8.

**Q7. The budget plan when the cap is too small, or there is no price.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H5):** (a) the required beacons stay, marked "Over budget: the required beacons alone cost X"; with no price the budget plan is not generated and says "Enter a unit price to plan to a budget". *Not chosen:* (b) drop required beacons; (c) refuse the whole request. Applied in AC-9 and AC-17. A cap left empty was raised as Q21 and ruled on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R16).

**Q8. Beacons already on the level.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H6):** (a) existing active beacons are fixed inputs; each plan shows "N new + M existing"; choosing writes rows only for the new ones; a previous choice's beacons not yet installed are removed on a new choice. *Not chosen:* (b) plans start from nothing; (c) a level with beacons cannot be planned. Applied in AC-10, AC-30, AC-54 and the level-model term, with DC-18.

**Q9. The state of new registry rows.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (A20):** (a) inactive with no `InstalledOn`, activated when installed. A plain beacon list joins P0-12 in FF-11 to do that, because P0-13 no longer has a fleet screen. *Not chosen:* (b) active at once; (c) no `Beacon` rows. Applied in AC-30, AC-34 and §3.9.

**Q10. The per-floor identifier range, and whether publish checks it.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (A19):** (a) one `Major` per level, unique in the tenant's namespace, `Minor`s within it; publish refuses two levels sharing a `Major` (P0-03 r3, built in FF-11). Changed from r1's option: the live engine does not read floors by `Major` and keeps trusting the package's level. C1's Majors stay until the spike exports arrive. *Not chosen:* (b) no publish check; (c) a `Major` per venue with a block of `Minor`s per level. Applied in §2 and AC-31, with DC-20.

**Q11. Planning on Demo Malls.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H7):** (a) plans can be generated and viewed, and choosing is refused. *Not chosen:* (b) hide the planner; (c) give Demo Malls a namespace. Applied in AC-32.

**Q12. Outdoor levels, and the outside beacon of each entrance.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H8):** (a) no coverage plan outdoors; each entrance's outside beacon is placed just outside its door, as part of the building level's plan. *Not chosen:* (b) plan outdoor levels; (c) inside beacon only. Applied in AC-11 and AC-53. Which level the outside beacon is recorded on, and a spot beyond the venue boundary, were raised as Q23 and ruled on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R18).

**Q13. The GDOP bands.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H9):** (a) provisional settings, up to 2 good, 2 to 5 fair, above 5 poor, "No fix" under three beacons. *Not chosen:* (b) a continuous ramp; (c) the owner names them. Applied in AC-23.

**Q14. L40 step 7's density flag and denser option.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (H10):** (a) a warning per walkable unit, no fourth plan. *Not chosen:* (b) a fourth "dense" plan. Applied in AC-19 and §8.

**Q15. A run-time or memory target.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (A5):** (a) none: measure and record, with no pass or fail, and set `planner.maxCells` from FF-11's measurements. *Not chosen:* (b) a named target. Applied in AC-52 and §6.

**Q16. ## 10. Questions raised in r2 (ruled 2026-10-12)

Each has the recommended option first. All three were ruled by the product owner on 2026-10-12 (`plan/FF-5-11-residual.md`, APPROVED, every item as recommended); the options are kept for the record.

**Q21 [spec-writer] The budget-capped plan when a price is typed but no budget cap.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R16): (a).** Applied in AC-9, AC-17, EC-46. H5 settles a missing price, not a missing cap. AC-9 and EC-46 depend on this.
- (a) The budget plan is not generated, and its card says "Enter a budget cap to plan to a budget". *Recommended:* this mirrors H5's no-price rule, and the other two plans still generate.
- (b) A cap is required whenever a price is typed: generating is refused with 400 until both are given.
- (c) The cap defaults to the estimate's cost (AC-5).

**Q22 [spec-writer] Do you accept the new design choices DC-17 to DC-22 (§7) as written?** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R17): (a), accept all.** Applied in §7. They fill gaps that the rulings opened: junction counting (H2), existing beacons at required spots (H6), the beacon list's details (A20), allocation from 1 (A19), mixed-unit cells (H12), and the RF distance and measured power (A18).
- (a) Accept all. *Recommended.*
- (b) Accept all except the ones you name, with the alternative stated for each.

**Q23 [spec-writer] The outside beacon of each entrance: which level it is recorded on, and what happens when its spot is outside the venue boundary.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R18): (a).** Recorded on the building level; left out with a warning when it falls beyond the boundary; no migration. Applied in AC-11, EC-47, §5 item 1. AC-11 and EC-47 depend on this. r1's Q12 (a), approved through H8, says the outside beacon is placed "on the outdoor level … as part of the building level's plan". In the schema:
- `BeaconPlanItem` has no level of its own, and a chosen item's `Beacon.LevelId` comes from its plan (AC-30), so a building level's plan cannot record a beacon on the outdoor level.
- AC-14 keeps every item inside the venue boundary. Until FF-10's drawn boundary (G4), that boundary is a rectangle over the largest level (P0-01), so a spot just outside a door on the building's edge can fall beyond it.

The options:
- (a) The outside beacon is an item of the building level's plan, recorded on the building level and mounted on the outer face beside the door. If that spot is outside the venue boundary, the beacon is left out, and the plan warns "The outside beacon at ⟨entrance⟩ needs the venue boundary drawn around the entrance (map editor)". *Recommended:* no schema change, and the live engine trusts each beacon's level from the package (A19).
- (b) A migration adds `BeaconPlanItem.LevelId`, so the outside beacon is recorded on the venue's outdoor level when there is one (the Demo Malls venues each have one; the spike venues do not), with the boundary handled as in (a). Better if P0-13's entrance handoff (F7) needs outdoor-level beacons.
- (c) No outside beacon until P0-13's entrance handoff asks for one. Each entrance gets the inside beacon only.

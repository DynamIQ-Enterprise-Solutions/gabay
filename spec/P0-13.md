## 1. What this spec covers

The live position on the phone, for venues whose package carries beacons:

1. **Scanning and sensors** (native-ble, Kotlin): one filtered BLE scan, the accelerometer, the magnetometer and, outdoors, GNSS, all through Gabay's own platform channel (L23, E13). Foreground only. The Swift side is P1-05.
2. **The engine** (engine-coder, in P0-06's worker isolate): steps, the freeze, walkway matching (D4b #6), the plausibility filter (invariant 7), floors at connectors with the recovery check (D4b #8, L106), the re-seed at a hand-set position (A8), the per-device RSSI offset on the shared radio model (D4b #7, L107, A18), the outdoor GNSS dot and the entrance handoff (L31, F7).
3. **The displayed dot** (L106): the jump gate, smoothing and speed cap, the freeze, the walkway line lock, the route snap and the glide, then the look (halo, unsure and grey, cone, "You should be in ⟨store⟩", the in-transit pill, the faded dot on other levels).
4. **The hand-offs:** the confidence state and the takeover rule (A8), the default route origin, Recentre and follow-me (F8), the screen-idle release (A17), and the settings behind every number.

**Coders (A25, ruled).** engine-coder (the engine and display pipeline) and native-ble (the Kotlin channel) on Opus 5.5; flutter-coder (drawing the dot and its prompts) and api-coder (the settings in the mall list and the seed) on Sonnet 5.5 (L164).

**On the test maps (§G ruling 1, L160; F1).** The Demo Malls venues have no beacons and stay on the fallback, so P0-13 does nothing on them (AC-8). The live dot is for real venues: the penthouse (five H2s, internal only, L48) and MEZZ office. Both are refused at publish until FF-10 confirms their doors (D13). Ruled (F1): FF-9 is built and proven with fixtures and a fake channel; FF-10 runs beside FF-9; the on-site check (AC-51) runs once FF-10 has confirmed the penthouse doors (N11).

## 3. Definitions

- **Reading:** one sighting of one beacon identity with its RSSI and time, from the native channel.
- **Update:** one engine step over the readings and motion since the last step. It produces a matched estimate.
- **Matched estimate:** the matcher's position (x, y, level, in the venue frame, E10), its uncertainty in metres, and its travel direction.
- **Unsnapped position (F3, ruled):** the matched estimate, before every display step (jump gate, smoothing, freeze, line lock, route snap, glide). Off-route, arrival and routing read it (L106 ruling 1; P0-06 r4).
- **Displayed position:** where the dot is drawn, after the pipeline of AC-21 to AC-25.
- **States (A8):** the six `PositionSource` states of P0-08 §2. P0-13 produces live-confident and live-unsure, the latter with the grey flag at or above `dot.greyM` (DC-1), and none. P0-08 produces hand-set and approximate, and P0-15 produces guided.
- **Boundary status (A8):** unknown, inside or outside, a separate value beside the state, set by P0-09's check from each reported point (E3).
- **Re-seed (A8; DC-18):** the engine restarting its matcher from a hand-set position, so that a later live-confident fix may take over (AC-53). An approximate position is not re-seeded (DC-19, as R6).
- **Connector zone:** the area near a connector's landing or stop where a level change may be accepted (D4b #8). Its size is an F6 number.
- **Riding:** the matched estimate is on a connector edge between two landings or stops.
- **Per-model seed:** the starting RSSI offset for the phone's model (D4b #7), from Google's Exposure Notifications calibration table; 0 dB for a model not in it (A18).

## 4. Acceptance criteria

### A. The source and the worker

AC-1: The live source is a producer for P0-08's `PositionSource` (Blueprint §2.5, §4.3; A8). Each update reports:
- its state: live-confident, live-unsure (with the grey flag, DC-1), or none;
- the matched estimate (the unsnapped position, F3), from which P0-09 sets the boundary status (E3), and the displayed position;
- the uncertainty, the travel direction, the riding flag with its connector, and the store the shopper is in, if any.

Outside is not a state of this source (A8). Verify: unit (the source against a fake channel; no `outside` value is ever produced).

AC-2: Matching and the display pipeline run in P0-06's long-lived worker isolate. They share one load of the package's graph and R\*Trees with routing (D4b; Blueprint §2.2). The UI isolate only draws, and reaches the engine through asynchronous messages. Verify: unit (one graph load across routing and positioning queries; the matcher is called only in the worker).

### B. Scanning (native-ble; Android now, the Swift side at P1-05)

AC-3: One continuous low-latency scan runs with exactly one hardware `ScanFilter`: Apple company ID `0x004C`, data `02 15` followed by the open venue's tenant UUID, taken from the package's beacons (D4b #1; the unfiltered-scan trap). Where offloaded filtering is not supported, the same filter is applied in software. Verify: unit (a Kotlin JVM test of the filter bytes; the Dart side builds the filter from a fixture package) and review.

AC-4: Scan starts never exceed Android's quota of 5 per 30 s (the scan-quota trap; DC-16 keeps one spare). The scan restarts after `scan.restartS` (about 9 min, D4b #2) at the first freeze that follows. If no freeze comes, it restarts before Android's 10-minute downgrade anyway (DC-3; the deadline is an F6 number). Verify: unit (fake clock; a run of permission flaps and lifecycle changes never makes a 5th start inside 30 s).

AC-5: Readings are batched in native code and sent about every `scan.batchMs` (100 ms) as binary messages. `setReportDelay` is never used (D4b #3). Verify: unit (the Dart decoder of the binary batch) and review (Kotlin).

AC-6: Scanning, the accelerometer, the magnetometer and GNSS run only while the app is in the foreground with a venue's map open. They stop when the app leaves the foreground or the screen locks, and start again on return. No background location, background scan, foreground service or "Always" permission is declared (invariant 5). Verify: unit (lifecycle fake), the `foreground-manifest` lint, and review.

AC-7: On Android 12 and later, the Bluetooth scan permission is asked the first time a venue with beacons is opened, with one line in place saying why (DC-4). It is declared without `neverForLocation` (the spike's finding that the flag filters out some beacons, RECALLED). On Android 11 and earlier, the "while using the app" location permission that P0-04 asks covers scanning (RECALLED). A refusal is not asked again by the app, and leaves the fallback (P0-08) working. Verify: widget (fake permission) and review (manifest).

AC-8: A venue whose package has no beacons starts no scan, asks for no Bluetooth permission and shows no live dot. Every Demo Malls venue is such a venue, and runs on the fallback (§G ruling 1; P0-02 AC-1). Verify: unit and widget (an Aurora-shaped fixture).

### C. Motion

AC-9: Steps and stillness come from the raw accelerometer, which needs no runtime permission on Android (L118's doc check). The step-detector sensor, which needs `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` from Android 10, is not used (DC-2). Verify: unit (synthetic accelerometer traces: walking, standing, a phone put down on a table).

AC-10: **Freeze (D4b #4, L106 ruling 8).** After the accelerometer has been quiet for `dot.freezeQuietS` (about 1 s), the displayed dot holds still. It is released by a step, or by `dot.freezeReleaseUpdates` (5) updates more than `dot.freezeReleaseM` (3 m) away. While frozen, readings keep being checked, so a teleport spoof is still caught (D4b #4). Verify: unit.

AC-11: **Compass (D4b #5).** The heading is a weak matching hint, used only while the measured field strength is inside the gate band. The band is 39–42 µT ±10%, a value marked UNVERIFIED in D4b #5 and provisional here. The gate is on field strength, never dip angle (the Davao dip trap). Outside the band the heading is ignored. The direction of travel comes from the walkway graph. A phone with no magnetometer or gyroscope still positions. Verify: unit (field traces inside and outside the band; a missing sensor).

### D. Matching and plausibility

AC-12: **Matcher (D4b #6).** A sparse Viterbi runs over walkway candidates inside a pruning radius, with a fixed lag. It shows the filtered estimate at once, commits the smoothed one after the lag, and widens the radius after a gap in readings. The radius, lag and gap are F6 numbers: the plan proposes each as a provisional setting with its basis, and the product owner approves them with the plan. Verify: unit (synthetic traces on invented geometry: a straight corridor, an L-turn, two parallel corridors with a wall between them, and a reading gap).

AC-13: **Evidence, not identity (invariant 7).** No single reading places the shopper. A lone beacon, or one strong reading, never moves the matched estimate further than a plausible walk along the graph since the last update. Readings inconsistent with the graph are discounted. Verify: unit (one strong reading of a far beacon leaves the estimate in place).

AC-14: **Clones (the clone trap; D9's checks on the phone).** These are discounted, and the dot does not jump:
- two simultaneous sightings of one identity at strengths that disagree beyond the plausibility limit (an F6 number);
- an identity heard strongly where the package places it implausibly far from the matched estimate.

Verify: unit.

AC-15: Only beacons in the open package count. A reading of the tenant's UUID with a Major and Minor that are not in the package is ignored: another venue of the same tenant (Spike Venues share one UUID), or an unknown device. The engine reads each beacon's level from the package and never infers a level from its `Major` (A19). Verify: unit (MEZZ office's Major 2 heard while a Major 1 fixture venue is open; a fixture whose beacons on two levels share a `Major` still places each on its package level).

AC-16: **Boundary (invariant 10; PRD §6.10; A8, E2, E3).** P0-09's check sets the boundary status from each matched estimate (E3); P0-13 adds no boundary setting or extra hysteresis, and the matcher's smoothing (AC-12) and plausibility filter (AC-13) are what keep one noisy reading from flipping it. While the status is outside, the estimate is never line-locked or route-snapped, no live dot is drawn (E2), and P0-09's out-of-venue card shows within one update. Verify: unit (an estimate walked out through an entrance turns outside within one update; one outlying reading near an entrance does not).

### E. Floors (D4b #8, L106, L113)

AC-17: The matched level changes only inside a connector zone, after the N-window hysteresis of D4b #8 (about 5 s, `floor.changeWindowS`, provisional). A reading of a beacon that the package places on another level is floor evidence only. It never moves x and y on the current level, so signal through the slab is not trusted (L96). Verify: unit (an atrium trace: the upper level's beacons strongest for 20 s away from any connector, and the level does not change).

AC-18: **Recovery check (L106; DC20).** Away from every connector zone, if another level's beacons dominate for `floor.recoveryWindowS` (15 s), the source reports live-unsure and the card asks "Are you on ⟨level⟩?":
- Yes moves the dot to the nearest walkway on that level, reachable without crossing a wall.
- No keeps the level and starts the window again.

This is P0-13's own check, not one of P0-14's (whose Yes never moves a live position, D4). Verify: unit (the window) and widget (the question, both answers).

AC-19: After a ride, P0-14's floor question stands in for the recovery check (L113, DC25). The recovery window does not start until that question has been answered or withdrawn (DC-8). Floor changes without a ride keep the 15 s check. Verify: unit.

AC-20: While riding, the dot carries the demo's in-transit pill, "On the ⟨connector type⟩" (for example "On the escalator"), and the cone is hidden (L106 ruling 6). Verify: widget.

### F. The displayed dot, in order per update (L106; Blueprint §4.3)

AC-21: **Jump gate.** A jump of more than `dot.jumpGateM` (2.5 m) is held until `dot.jumpAgreeFixes` (3) fixes agree within `dot.jumpAgreeM` (1.5 m). Meanwhile the dot moves at most `dot.jumpCreepM` (0.25 m) per fix. Verify: unit.

AC-22: **Smoothing and speed.** Smoothing uses `dot.smoothWalkS` (0.5 s) while walking and `dot.smoothStillS` (4 s) once still. The dot never moves faster than `dot.maxSpeedMps` (1.8 m/s). Verify: unit.

AC-23: **Walkway line lock (L106 ruling 2).** The dot sits on the nearest walkway edge reachable without crossing a wall, at any distance, using the package's graph and its walk-edge and wall-segment R\*Trees (A9). It is released only inside a store or restroom, or outside every room (DC-5). Verify: unit (P0-06 AC-8's vector: a corridor 1 m away through a wall and another 4 m away in the open; the dot locks to the second).

AC-24: **Route snap.** During a route, a position within `dot.routeSnapM` (2 m) of the route is drawn at the shopper's progress point along it. The progress radius is `dot.progressRadiusM` (5 m), and the point advances at most `dot.progressMaxStepM` (10 m) per update. The unsnapped position, which is the matched estimate (F3), is unchanged, and it is what off-route, arrival and routing read (L106 ruling 1; P0-06 r4). Verify: unit (1.5 m off the route: drawn on the route, unsnapped still 1.5 m off; 2.5 m off: not snapped; the line lock never changes the unsnapped position either).

AC-25: **Glide (DC19).** The dot glides to each new point in about `dot.glideMs` (500 ms), at a speed between `dot.glideMinMps` (0.3 m/s) and `dot.glideMaxWalkFactor` (2) times the walking speed (DC-14). It follows the matched path on the walkway graph, and every frame is checked against the wall segments. A frame-driven render with a `dot.chaseMs` (120 ms) chase ticks only while the dot moves or the cone fades. Verify: unit (the glide path follows the graph, and no frame crosses a wall segment) and widget (no frames are scheduled while the dot is still).

AC-26: While beacons are heard, the dot updates at least once per second (PRD §6.9). On site the rate is recorded as a provisional target; a miss is reported with the figure (A5). Verify: unit (fake channel and clock) and manual (on site, AC-51).

### G. The look (L106, the demo's where not ruled otherwise)

AC-27: **Halo.** The halo is the matcher's uncertainty, smoothed over `dot.haloSmoothS` (1 s) and capped at `dot.haloMaxM` (6 m), drawn as a solid soft halo. A live dot never has the dashed ring, which stays for approximate and hand-set positions (P0-08; PRD §6.8). Verify: widget (screenshots, light and dark).

AC-28: **Unsure.** Above `dot.unsureM` (4 m) of uncertainty, the source reports live-unsure and the dot is labelled "Near ⟨place⟩" (DC-7). It returns to live-confident once under `dot.unsureClearM` (3 m) for `dot.unsureClearUpdates` (3) updates. Verify: unit (the hysteresis) and widget (the label).

AC-29: **Grey.** At `dot.greyM` (6 m) the dot is grey (live-unsure with the grey flag), and the app offers "Select where you are", which opens P0-08's "Where are you?" (D4b #11; PRD §6.9). No candidate walkways are highlighted (F5, ruled; a G4 difference from D4b #11). Verify: widget (the grey look has no highlighted edges).

AC-30: **Cone (L106 ruling 5).** The cone has the demo's look (about `dot.coneLengthM` 14 m, `dot.coneHalfAngleDeg` 28° each side). It points along the matched travel direction, never the compass. It shows only after a step within the last `dot.coneStepWindowS` (1.5 s) and at least `dot.coneMinMoveM` (0.5 m) travelled within that time. It fades over `dot.coneFadeMs` (600 ms), and is hidden on arrival and while riding. The turning map does not follow the cone (L111). Verify: unit (the show and hide rule) and widget.

AC-31: Inside an open store (DC-6), the dot carries the line "You should be in ⟨store⟩" (F4, ruled; L113's suggestive form; a G4 difference from L106 and PRD §6.9's "You are in ⟨store⟩"), with a `dot.inStoreFadeMs` (300 ms) fade. Verify: widget.

AC-32: When the shown level is not the dot's level, a faded dot with a floor tag marks where the shopper is. This differs from the demo, by ruling (L106 ruling 6). Verify: widget.

AC-33: Every colour of the dot, halo, grey state, cone, pill and faded dot comes from `gabay_tokens.dart` through the theme, in light and dark (rule 6). Verify: the `colour-literals` lint and screenshots.

AC-34: The map's screen-reader description includes the dot's state ("Near ⟨place⟩", grey, the level, the store line, riding). The recovery question and the "Select where you are" offer have screen-reader names. Nothing overflows at text size ×1.4. Verify: widget (semantics; 320 dp at ×1.4).

### H. States, the handover and the route origin

AC-35: **Takeover (A8; L106 ruling 7; PRD §6.8; R6).** A live-confident fix replaces a hand-set position only when both hold:
- the boundary status of its matched estimate is inside (A8);
- it comes after the engine has re-seeded at the position the shopper set (AC-53).

It replaces an approximate position when the first holds: no re-seed is needed (R6). A live-unsure fix, grey or not, never replaces a held hand-set or approximate position (A8). During a guided walk a live fix is never taken: P0-15 offers "Your position is back. Use it?" only when the live dot turns confident during the walk, once per return, withdrawn if confidence is lost, and after any check card shown at the same moment (E16, L118). Verify: unit (for a hand-set position, each condition alone fails the takeover and both together pass; for an approximate position, an inside fix passes with no re-seed and an outside one fails; a guided walk is never replaced).

AC-36: **Default route origin (D4b, A8).** Go uses the `PositionSource`'s position when its state is live-confident, or live-unsure without the grey flag. A held hand-set or approximate position beats a live-unsure one (AC-35). Grey is never an origin: Go then uses the last place set by hand (D4b), else opens "Where are you?" and starts the route once a position is set (C2). An unsure origin brings P0-14's hedged wording and checks (L99, L113). A position whose boundary status is outside is never an origin (P0-06 EC-11). P0-06 snaps the origin by RG3. Verify: unit (the origin chosen in each state, grey and outside included) and widget.

AC-37: The state is exposed so that the checks follow it (L99, amended by L113). A confident live dot gets detected arrival and no check at the foot of a ride. An unsure or grey dot gets P0-14's checks. Places are worded suggestively either way. P0-13 sets the state; P0-14 and P0-06 r4 own the checks and their tests. Verify: unit (the state stream on synthetic traces).

### I. Calibration (D4b #7, L107, C1, C3, A18; Blueprint §4.3, DC18)

AC-38: When P0-08 reports a calibration pick, the engine takes the readings within `calibration.windowS` (about 2 s) around the pick. A calibration pick is every "I'm near here" pick of a place, by name or by description, typed or spoken (C3), placed at the place's one door or at the door the shopper tapped (C1). The engine computes the median difference between the RSSI heard and the expected value at that door from the shared `rf.*` model (A18): each beacon's measured power and position from the package, the path-loss exponent, and the wall loss for each wall segment crossed between the beacon and the door (the package's wall R\*Tree), so that the live dot and the planner use one radio model (A18's reason). Verify: unit (a synthetic pick with known offsets; a fixture with one wall between beacon and door).

AC-39: A pick counts only if the place is no wider than `calibration.maxPlaceM` (6 m). It is weighted by the beacons heard and by how small the place is (DC18, C3), and clamped to ±`calibration.clampDb` (10 dB) around the per-model seed. It is averaged with earlier picks, saved on the phone with the settings, and applied to every reading after that. Verify: unit (a 4 m place counts; a 7 m place does not; a +25 dB pick is clamped).

AC-40: A pick that contradicts strong beacon evidence beyond the plausibility limit is ignored for calibration (Blueprint §4.3, DESIGN CHOICE as written there). A floor pick and a refining map tap never calibrate: they are not picks of a place (C3; L107). Verify: unit (a pick by name calibrates; a floor pick and a tap do not; a contradicting pick is ignored).

AC-41: The offset is a device value, never sent. A broken or out-of-range saved value falls back to the seed (L99's rule for saved values). The seed is the phone model's value from Google's Exposure Notifications calibration table, shipped as an app asset, and 0 dB for a model not in it (A18; P0-13 Q9 option 1). Verify: unit (corrupted saved values; a known model's seed; an unknown model at 0 dB) and the `position-privacy` lint.

## 10. Design choices (not requirements; each with an alternative)

DC-1 to DC-17 were accepted on 2026-10-12 (A7, L165). DC-18 to DC-21, new in r2, were accepted on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R21, ruling Q17), DC-19 in R6's form.

- **DC-1 Grey is a level of live-unsure**, keeping Blueprint §4.3's state set (confirmed by A8: the grey flag). *Alternative:* a separate "lost" state; better if consumers must treat grey very differently from unsure.
- **DC-2 Steps from the raw accelerometer**, no runtime permission (L118's check). *Alternative:* the step-detector sensor with `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION`; better if raw-sensor step counting proves poor on budget phones.
- **DC-3 Scan restart at the first freeze after `scan.restartS`, else forced before the 10-minute downgrade.** *Alternative:* a fixed 9-minute restart whatever the motion; simpler, but restarts mid-walk.
- **DC-4 The Bluetooth permission is asked on first opening a beacon venue**, with one line saying why. *Alternative:* at first launch with location; one prompt fewer later, but asked of Demo Malls users who never need it.
- **DC-5 The line lock is released inside any unit holding an occupant or an amenity** (a store or a restroom by L106's words), and outside every unit. *Alternative:* a flag per amenity type; finer, but a new column.
- **DC-6 "Open store" means the occupant's status is routable** (`StoreStatus.IsRoutable`, never a code string). *Alternative:* any occupant unit whatever its status.
- **DC-7 "Near ⟨place⟩" names the nearest named place on the dot's level by walking distance**, from the cached whole-graph Dijkstra (Blueprint §4.7, DC12). *Alternative:* the straight-line nearest door; cheaper, but it can name a place behind a wall.
- **DC-8 After a ride, the recovery window waits until P0-14's floor question is answered or withdrawn.** *Alternative:* a fixed quiet period after stepping off.
- **DC-9 A live takeover during a route does not work the route out again;** progress continues and off-route applies. *Alternative:* recompute at takeover (PRD §6.8 does so for a new hand-set position).
- **DC-10 With animations off, the dot moves in one step and fades are instant**, as P0-04 AC-42 does for the camera. *Alternative:* keep the glide, which carries meaning.
- **DC-11 On return to the foreground, the last position shows as live-unsure until the first new fix.** *Alternative:* hide the dot until then.
- **DC-12 A beacon with no measured power is left out of ranging.** *Alternative:* a default from a setting.
- **DC-13 With Bluetooth or location services off, a one-line hint to turn it on**, plus the fallback offer. *Alternative:* no hint, the fallback only.
- **DC-14 The glide's "walking speed" is the shopper's current speed from steps.** *Alternative:* 1.2 m/s × the Walking pace factor.
- **DC-15 When the worker is busy, the newest batch supersedes waiting ones.** *Alternative:* queue every batch; it keeps all evidence but can lag behind.
- **DC-16 At most 4 scan starts in any 30 s**, one below the quota. *Alternative:* the full 5.
- **DC-17 An unanswered recovery question is withdrawn once the evidence returns to the current level for the window.** *Alternative:* it stays until answered.
- **DC-18 (Accepted, R21) The re-seed at a hand-set position** (A8 and P0-08 Q6 leave its definition to P0-13): the matcher restarts from the walkway point nearest the set spot, reachable without crossing a wall, with candidates within the pruning radius; contradicting readings widen the search as after a gap (AC-53). *Alternative:* a hold time after a hand-set position before any live fix may take over (P0-08 Q6 option 2); simpler, but a wrong live dot would retake the position the moment the hold ends.
- **DC-19 (Accepted as R6, R21) An approximate position (a floor pick) does not re-seed the engine**: P0-08 emits no re-seed request for it, and the first live-confident fix inside the boundary takes over (R6). This replaces the r2 draft's level-only re-seed (the matcher restarted with candidates on every walkway of the picked level), which was not taken; nor was a re-seed at the approximate point with P0-08's 25 m ring as the radius (the approximate point is a level centre, not where the shopper said they are).
- **DC-20 (Accepted, R21) The internal "Test position" tool stays after FF-9** (A16 leaves the call to P0-13), internal builds only, with the live producer's values not taken while it runs (AC-56). Reason: the Demo Malls venues have no beacons (§G ruling 1), so it remains the only way to show movement there on localhost. *Alternative:* remove it in FF-9 once the live dot exists; better if an internal producer beside the live one is judged a risk.
- **DC-21 (Accepted, R21) After the idle release, the screen-on is set again at the next step while the route is active**, and idle means no step from the accelerometer, on any venue (AC-44). *Alternative:* it stays released until the shopper touches the screen; simpler, but the screen may then go dark mid-walk after a long wait at a check.

**Q17 [spec-writer] The design choices new in r2 (DC-18 to DC-21, §10).** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R21): option 1, with DC-19 as R6** (an approximate position needs no re-seed; the first confident live fix inside the boundary takes over, not a level-only re-seed). **Q18 [spec-writer] The negative `rf.*` figures and the positive-number rule.** **Ruled 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R22): option 2, as worded on the sheet.** Each key gets the validity rule its own spec states, so radio values are stored as the real dBm the beacon datasheets use (−55 dBm, −95 dBm); A3's reading is narrowed in P0-04 AC-60.

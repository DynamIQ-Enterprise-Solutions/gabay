## 2. Definitions

- **Active way:** the way the shopper is following on an active trip (P0-04 trip-active; P0-06's lead way or the one they tapped).
- **Guided walk:** the mode started by "Guide me step by step" (or a tap on the hint) and ended by the trip's end, by "Stop guiding" (E11), or by a redo that finds no route (E10).
- **Guided source:** the `PositionSource` producer (P0-08 AC-1) that reports the guided dot's point with the confidence state `guided`, one of P0-08's six states (A8). File: `lib/core/positioning/guided_source.dart` (FEATURE_PIPELINE P0-15).
- **Guided dot:** the shopper's dot while the guided source is the position.
- **Checkpoint:** a point on the active way where the guided dot stops and waits for an answer (E7): every turn, every named landmark the directions use, the foot of every ride, the destination's door, and P0-14's along-the-way checkpoints on approaches over 40 m, by the demo's rules as named constants (D10: at least 22 m apart, none within 25 m of the turn, every 35 m where nothing is named). Turns the voice says together stay separate checkpoints.
- **Stretch:** the part of the active way between two consecutive checkpoints. "Never more than one stretch ahead" (L118) means the guided dot is never past the next unanswered checkpoint.
- **Starting pace:** `guide.paceMps` ÷ the Me → Walking pace factor (AC-6).
- **Pace steps:** a whole number saved on the phone: how many Slower (negative) or Faster (positive) taps the shopper's pace sits from the starting pace (E12).
- **Guided pace:** starting pace + pace steps × `guide.speedStep`, held within `guide.minPaceMps` and `guide.maxPaceMps`, in metres per second along the way.
- **Ghost dot:** Preview's dot, drawn distinctly from the shopper's dot; it is not a position and no `PositionSource` reports it.
- **Real metres:** the walked length along the way, without weights (P0-06 §2).

## 3. What this spec takes from other specs (requirements on them)

1. **P0-06 r4 (FF-6): the directions.** Each direction of a way carries its kind (turn left or right, a named landmark passed, the start and end of a ride, arrival), its side, its point on the way as real metres from the way's start, its level, the landmark's name (and `SpokenName`, P0-14) where there is one, and its plain instruction (L165 §L). P0-15 derives its checkpoints from this list and P0-14's along-the-way rule (AC-10).
2. **P0-06 r2 AC-27:** the way's points in order with their levels, and its legs with real metres. The guided and ghost dots move along these points.
3. **P0-06 r4: off route, the wrong-way notice and browsing** read the position's confidence state and are off while it is `guided` (AC-17; L165 §L). **Re-routing** (a way pill, a changed option, "Find another way", a new position) starts from the guided dot's point when the state is `guided` (E20). P0-06 r4 also draws the direction card and "I've arrived" (A14).
4. **P0-08 (FF-6): the `PositionSource`** with its six states (`none`, `approximate`, `handSet`, `liveConfident`, `liveUnsure` with a grey flag, `guided`) and the boundary status kept apart from them (A8); its takeover rule, under which a live fix during a guided walk is offered, never taken (P0-08 AC-4, EC-39; A8); and "Where are you?" opened with a reason from a check's No, which redoes an active route from the chosen place (P0-08 §"opened with a reason"; PRD §6.8).
5. **P0-14 (FF-7): the check cards and their wording** ("Are you here?" on pause, P0-14 AC-34; "Are you at ⟨connector⟩?" at the foot of a ride; L113's floor question, P0-14 AC-36; the arrival line), shown as a card over the map; Pause and Resume; the voice queue and its priorities; the buzz before turns; the wrong-way sound on a check's No; Walking pace (×1.0, ×1.3, ×1.7) in Me; the along-the-way checkpoint rule and its constants (D10), shared with P0-15, not copied (C2); the Tagalog review sheet (A2). Three guided-only rules P0-14 must allow: the floor question holds the guided dot until answered (E9; P0-14 AC-36 otherwise never blocks guidance); no "now" cue for a guided dot (E17); and L111's "face my way" tip moves to the trip after the guided-walk tip (E18).
6. **P0-04 (r2, and r3 in FF-7):** the route sheet container (where "Guide me step by step", "Stop guiding", Preview, "Stop preview" and the pace buttons sit), the trip-active state, the map's drawing of dots above the scene, the camera's follow-me and Recentre, and **the turning map** (L111), built by P0-04 r3 in FF-7 (A15). P0-04 r3 also carries the `guide.*` keys in the mall list from FF-8, and keeps the last values received per venue on the phone (A6).
7. **P0-13 (FF-9): the live source** and its states (Bluetooth off or denied, no beacons heard, unsure, grey, confident). Until FF-9, a fake live source in tests stands in (A16).
8. **P0-09 (FF-8, the same slice): the boundary status** (unknown, inside, outside; A8) for AC-3, and the internal "Put me here" tool (A16), which P0-15 does not need for its own tests.

## 4. Acceptance criteria

### 4.1 Starting and stopping the guided walk

AC-1: During an active trip the route sheet shows "Guide me step by step"; tapping it starts the guided walk. A tap on the hint (AC-23) also starts it (E18: a tap is by hand). Nothing else starts one: not a live dot that is unsure, grey or lost, not a venue without beacons, not the end of a trip, not a package swap, not the hint appearing (L118 ruling 3, "by hand only"). Verify: widget (the button only with a trip; the hint's tap starts a walk; a fake live source taken through every untrusted state never starts a walk).

AC-2: On start, the guided dot appears at the shopper's current point on the active way (the snapped origin of a hand-set or approximate position; the progress point of a live dot), and the position becomes the guided source's, with the confidence state `guided` (Blueprint §4.3; A8). Checkpoints at or behind that point are skipped (§9 DC-3). Verify: unit.

AC-3: "Guide me step by step" is not offered when there is no route (no origin, "already here", no route with its reason; P0-06 AC-31, AC-35, EC-8), and not while the boundary status is outside (P0-09, PRD §6.10; A8). Verify: widget.

AC-4: Ending the trip (✕, "I've arrived", or confirming "End your route?" when changing malls, P0-04 AC-59) ends the guided walk and removes the guided dot. While a guided walk runs, "Guide me step by step" becomes "Stop guiding" (E11): tapping it ends the walk, the dot stays where it is as a hand-set position (state `handSet`, the dashed ring), and the trip continues under the hand-set rules (P0-14's checks; P0-08's takeover rule, A8). Verify: widget (each way of ending; after "Stop guiding" the state is `handSet` at the dot's point and the trip is still active).

### 4.2 How the guided dot moves

AC-5: While the guided walk runs and the dot is not waiting, the dot advances along the active way's points at the guided pace, counted in real metres (§9 DC-1) against elapsed time from a monotonic clock (§9 DC-2), changing level only at the way's rides (AC-14). The camera follows it under follow-me, until a pan, pinch or twist pauses follow-me and Recentre resumes it (L111). Verify: unit (fake clock: after t seconds the dot is pace × t real metres along, up to the next checkpoint); widget.

AC-6: The starting pace is `guide.paceMps` divided by the Me → Walking pace factor (PRD §6.23, L118 ruling 2): 1.2, 1.2 ÷ 1.3 ≈ 0.923 and 1.2 ÷ 1.7 ≈ 0.706 m/s at the defaults. Verify: unit (each factor).

AC-7: Slower and Faster on the card change the pace steps by one, each step `guide.speedStep` (default 0.1 m/s; Blueprint §4.4). A button is disabled when one more step would take the pace below `guide.minPaceMps` (0.3) or above `guide.maxPaceMps` (2.4) (E12). The pace steps are saved on the phone, never sent, and used by the next guided walk (L118 ruling 2). Because the saved value is steps from the starting pace, a change of Walking pace in Me moves the guided pace with it, during a walk too (E12); the result is held within the bounds. A saved value that is not a whole number is ignored and the steps are 0 (PRD §6.4). Verify: unit (a step; each bound; saved and restored; a Me change before and during a walk; a result held at a bound; a corrupted saved value); widget.

AC-8: The dot stops at each checkpoint and moves on only after the shopper's Yes. It is never past the next unanswered checkpoint, however long it waits (L118 ruling 1, "never more than one stretch ahead"). Verify: unit (fake clock run for ten times the stretch's walking time: the dot sits on the checkpoint).

AC-9: Pause holds the guided dot where it is; Resume moves it on from there. A guided position is not live-confident, so pausing asks P0-14's "Are you here? …" check (P0-14 AC-34; Blueprint §4.3); its Yes keeps the dot where it is (D4), and its "No, I'm somewhere else" behaves as a checkpoint's No (AC-13). Verify: widget.

### 4.3 Checkpoints

AC-10: The checkpoints of a way are derived by one pure function from its directions (§3 item 1) and P0-14's along-the-way rule (§3 item 5): every turn, every named landmark the directions use, the foot of every ride, the destination's door, and the along-the-way checkpoints on approaches over 40 m, in order along the way (E7, D10). Turns the voice says together stay separate checkpoints. The list is derived again for every new route result. Verify: unit (vectors: a straight walk to a door under 40 m; a straight approach over 40 m with and without named places; one turn; two turns within 25 m; a landmark passed; a ride; a store crossing; a lift spanning several levels).

AC-11: At a checkpoint other than a ride or the door, a card over the map (never hidden in a swiped-down sheet, PRD §6.6) asks its check, with Yes and "No, I'm somewhere else" (E8; PRD §6.6):
- a turn at a named landmark: "Are you at ⟨landmark⟩? ⟨instruction⟩." ("Are you at Zen Gifts? Turn left here.");
- a named landmark that is not a turn: "Are you passing ⟨landmark⟩? Keep going straight.";
- a checkpoint with no landmark to name (an unnamed turn, or an unnamed along-the-way checkpoint): "You should be about N m past ⟨last confirmed point⟩ now. ⟨instruction⟩." ("… Turn left here." at a turn; "… Keep going straight." elsewhere), N being the real metres from that point. ⟨last confirmed point⟩ is the last confirmed checkpoint that has a name, with N counted from it ("about 60 m past Zen Gifts"); before any named checkpoint is confirmed, it is the start: its place name when the start was picked by name in "Where are you?", else "where you started" (R24).
Places are worded suggestively and the instruction plainly, with left and right as the shopper walks (L113). Verify: widget (each checkpoint kind); unit (the sentence for each kind; ⟨last confirmed point⟩ after a named checkpoint, after only unnamed ones, and before any, with a start picked by name and with one set by a floor pick or a tap).

AC-12: Yes closes the card and the dot moves on towards the next checkpoint at the current guided pace. Verify: unit; widget.

AC-13: "No, I'm somewhere else" plays the wrong-way sound first when that setting is on (P0-14; PRD §6.6), then opens "Where are you?" with its reason (P0-08). A place chosen there redoes the route from it (PRD §6.8) and the guided walk carries on along the redone route from that place, heading for its first checkpoint (E10). If "Where are you?" is closed without a choice, the dot keeps waiting at the checkpoint and its card shows again (E10). If the redo finds no route, the guided walk ends and the reason shows (E10; P0-06 AC-31). Verify: widget (fake router and fake sound channel: each of the three outcomes).

AC-14: At the foot of a ride the dot stops and the card asks P0-14's "Are you at ⟨connector⟩?" once per ride. Yes moves the dot at once to the landing on the exit level, the shown level follows, and the card asks L113's floor question, "Are you on ⟨level⟩? You should see ⟨A⟩ on your ⟨side⟩ …" (P0-14 AC-36); the dot waits for its Yes before walking on (E9; L118 ruling 6). No to either behaves as AC-13. Verify: unit (the dot at the landing, waiting); widget.

AC-15: At the destination the dot stops at the door and the card says "You should be at ⟨place⟩ now. Tap I've arrived if you are." (PRD §6.6). Arrival (the double vibration, the confetti) follows only that tap; arrival is never detected during a guided walk. Verify: unit (no arrival event from the guided source); widget.

AC-16: Checks are answered by tap only (L112, D9). With a screen reader on, the check card is a live region that says its sentence, and its buttons are named (PRD §6.23). Verify: widget (semantics).

### 4.4 What the guided state changes

AC-17: Off route and the wrong-way notice (L80, L110), the falling-progress notice and browsing (L110) are not evaluated on guided positions: a guided dot, whatever its pace against the shopper's, never confirms a deviation, re-routes or shows the browsing card (Blueprint DC26; L165 §L). A wrong turn is caught only by the shopper's No. Verify: unit (P0-06 r4's detectors fed a guided source report nothing; the same track from a live fake does).

AC-18: The guidance reads `guided` as not live-confident (Blueprint §4.3; L118 ruling 6; P0-14 AC-40): wording about places is suggestive, the pause check, the foot-of-ride check, the floor question and the arrival tap all apply. Verify: unit (the guidance's check gate for each of the six states).

AC-19: With voice guidance on, "Coming up" (20 m) and the buzz before each turn (20 m) follow the guided dot as they follow a live one; the "now" cue (5 m) is not given for a guided dot, because the dot stops there and the check says the instruction (E17). With "Map faces the way I walk" on, the map turns with the guided dot's route segment (L118 ruling 6; PRD §6.6; built in FF-7, A15). Verify: unit (a fake voice channel and fake haptics receive "Coming up" and the buzz at the guided dot's 20 m, and no "now" cue).

AC-20: Guided and preview movement is never recorded or sent: no guided or ghost position is written to any trace, event, log or request (Blueprint DC27; PRD §6.19; invariant 4). FF-8 has no trace recorder (P1-04 is blocked on L42); the test asserts no network request during a guided walk and a preview, and that every guided position carries the `guided` state that P1-04's recorder must exclude. Verify: unit (a failing HTTP client is never called); the `position-privacy` lint; the review privacy pass.

AC-21: The guided walk reads no sensor and asks for no permission: no motion, activity-recognition, Bluetooth or location permission is requested for it (L118: a timed dot, not counted steps). It works in full with Bluetooth and location denied. Verify: unit; the `foreground-manifest` lint (no new permission declared); manual (permissions denied).

### 4.5 The tip and the hint

AC-22: The first trip on the phone that shows no other one-time tip shows the guided-walk tip pointing at "Guide me step by step": "No dot, or a wrong one? Tap Guide me step by step and Gabay walks the route with you, stopping at each turn." (L118 ruling 3; PRD §6.6; E18). L111's "face my way" tip shows on the next trip, so at most one tip shows per trip (E18). Once dismissed the guided-walk tip never shows again, after a restart included (a flag saved on the phone, never sent). Verify: widget (first trip, second trip, after a restart, a corrupted flag, a phone that already saw the "face my way" tip).

AC-23: During a trip without a guided walk, the direction card shows the quiet hint "Can't find you? Try Guide me step by step" once either has lasted `guide.hintAfterS` (default 10 s; Blueprint §4.4) without a break: the live position untrusted (Bluetooth off or denied, no beacons heard, a grey dot, a phone without Bluetooth LE; PRD §6.6), or the open venue's package listing no beacons, which counts as "no beacons heard" on every trip there (E6). It never starts the walk by itself; a tap on it does (AC-1, E18). It goes when the live position is trusted again or a guided walk starts. Verify: unit (fake live source and fake clock: shown at 10 s, not at 9.9 s, reset by a trusted fix; a no-beacon package: shown at 10 s on every trip); widget (the tap starts a walk).

### 4.6 The live dot returning

AC-24: During a guided walk, when the live source turns live-confident (it was not live-confident when the walk started, or has lost confidence since), the guided walk carries on and a card asks "Your position is back. Use it?" with Yes and "Keep guiding" (L118 ruling 4; PRD §6.8; A8; E16). The live position is never taken without Yes. Yes ends the guided walk and the live source becomes the position, with its own checks, off route and arrival (P0-06 r4, P0-13); "Keep guiding" keeps the guided dot. The offer is made at most once per return: after "Keep guiding" it is made again only after the live dot has lost confidence and regained it. If the live dot loses confidence while the offer is open, the offer is withdrawn. A check card open at the same moment comes first; the offer follows its answer if the live dot is still confident (E16). Verify: unit and widget (fake live source: each of these sequences). Proven on a device with the penthouse's beacons in FF-9 (A16).

### 4.7 Preview the route

AC-25: During a trip without a guided walk, the route sheet shows Preview (E14). Tapping it plays a ghost dot along the active way from its start to the destination at `guide.previewSpeedX` × `guide.paceMps` (3 × 1.2 m/s at the defaults), whatever the shopper's guided pace or Walking pace (L118 ruling 5; E13). The map turns with the ghost dot's route segment even when "Map faces the way I walk" is off, and is put back as it was afterwards (E13). At a ride the ghost dot holds at the foot for a named constant of about 1 s, then the shown level changes to the landing and it carries on (E13). There is no voice: no cue, check, buzz or wrong-way sound (P0-14 AC-28). It stops at no checkpoint. At the destination the ghost dot disappears and the camera returns to where it was before the preview (Blueprint §4.4; E13). Verify: unit (ghost progress on a fake clock; the ride hold; no voice-channel or haptics call; camera and turning restored); widget.

AC-26: Preview never touches the position: the `PositionSource`, its confidence state, the shopper's dot, the route's origin and progress, the off-route state and any guided walk's state are the same after a preview as before it (Blueprint §4.4). Verify: unit (state compared before and after).

AC-27: The ghost dot is hollow and translucent, without halo or cone, drawn distinctly from the shopper's dot (Blueprint §4.4; E19) and above the scene with the route (P0-04 AC-39). The guided dot has the hand-set look, the dashed ring (PRD §6.9), with a small walking mark (E19). Verify: widget (screenshots); unit (paint order).

AC-36: While Preview plays, the sheet shows "Stop preview"; tapping it, or any pan, pinch or twist on the map, ends the preview and the ghost dot goes (E14). The camera springs back only after "Stop preview" or the preview's natural end (E13); after a pan, pinch or twist, the camera stays where the shopper moved it, follow-me is paused until Recentre (L111), and the turning setting is back to its own state (R26). Tapping "Guide me step by step" during a preview ends the preview and starts the walk (E14). Verify: widget (each way of ending).

### 4.8 Settings

AC-28: Six platform `GlobalSetting` rows are seeded: `guide.paceMps` (1.2), `guide.speedStep` (0.1), `guide.hintAfterS` (10), `guide.minPaceMps` (0.3), `guide.maxPaceMps` (2.4) and `guide.previewSpeedX` (3) (FEATURE_PIPELINE P0-15 DB; Blueprint §4.4; A6, E12, E13). They reach the phone in the public mall list's shopper settings, resolved for each venue's tenant (the tenant's override, else the platform value; round-1 A3, P0-04 AC-60). The phone keeps the last values received for each venue and applies them offline (A6; P0-04 r3); before any are received, the defaults apply. A value that is not a positive number is ignored and the default used (P0-04 §2). When the two pace bounds received for a venue put `guide.minPaceMps` at or above `guide.maxPaceMps`, both received bounds are ignored and the platform's bounds apply (the defaults, 0.3 and 2.4); every other `guide.*` value applies (R25). These are the validity rules this spec states for the `guide.*` keys, each key's spec stating its own (R22). Verify: seed test (the six rows); API (the list carries the keys, a tenant with an override and one without); unit (defaults; values kept per venue and used offline; invalid values; a crossed pair of bounds, and an equal pair, fall back to the platform's bounds while the other four values apply).

## 9. Design choices (not requirements; each with an alternative)

DC-1 to DC-4 were accepted by the product owner on 2026-10-12 (L165, A7).

- **DC-1 The guided dot walks real metres.** A store crossing takes its real length at the guided pace. *Alternative:* the way's metre cost, as the demo's `progress` over `total`; it would slow every store crossing by 1.25× and make a ride take its metre cost at walking pace (not taken: E9 moves the dot across a ride at the Yes).
- **DC-2 Progress is timed on a monotonic elapsed clock**, as L111's clock-timed glide and P0-04 EC-26. *Alternative:* per-frame increments, as the demo's `onTick` (capped at 0.1 s per frame); frame-rate dependent, so a slow phone walks slower (the reason L111 did not copy the demo's per-frame turn).
- **DC-3 Checkpoints at or behind the guided start are skipped.** *Alternative:* ask the check at the start point at once; better if a hand-set start is often wrong (the shopper would confirm it first). The pause check (AC-9) already lets them confirm.
- **DC-4 The checkpoint list is one pure function of a route result**, derived again for every new route. *Alternative:* derived lazily ahead of the dot; no gain at the stated scale (§5), and harder to test.

**Readings of the rulings, stated so a reviewer can check them (not new choices):**
- AC-7 "held within the bounds": E12 gives bounds and steps-from-start; when a Me change puts the start plus the saved steps past a bound, the pace sits at the bound and the saved steps are kept, so a later Me change can bring it back.
- AC-25 "3 × 1.2 m/s": E13's 1.2 m/s is `guide.paceMps`'s default and A6 makes the 3 `guide.previewSpeedX`; Preview's speed is their product, independent of Walking pace and pace steps (Q10 option 1, "whatever the shopper's guided pace").
- AC-25's ride hold of "about 1 s" is a named constant, not a setting: A6 lists the six `guide.*` settings and the hold is not one of them (C8).
- AC-11's unnamed along-the-way checkpoint uses E8's no-landmark form with the plain instruction "Keep going straight".

**RECALLED, to verify by a doc check in the plan:** Dart's `Stopwatch` measures monotonic elapsed time unaffected by a wall-clock change (DC-2, EC-37).

## 3. Definitions

- **Confidence state:** the `PositionSource`'s state, exposed by P0-08, one of six (A8): none, approximate, hand-set, live-confident, live-unsure (grey is live-unsure with a grey flag), guided. **Not live-confident** means every state but live-confident; a guided position is not live-confident (L118). Being outside the venue is a separate boundary status, not a state (A8).
- **Voice on / off:** the one saved switch, Me → Voice guidance, shared with the rail speaker button (L112 ruling 10). Off by default (PRD §6.4).
- **Muted (D2):** a third state, set when an earphone disconnect is broadcast during an active route (AC-6), cleared when the shopper turns the voice on again from the rail or Me. While muted the saved switch stays on (DC-28); muted is saved on the phone and lasts until cleared (DC-29).
- **Item:** one thing for the voice: a sentence, or the wrong-way clip in the voice's turn (Blueprint §4.4).
- **Priority (L112 with D3, high to low):** emergency, a closure on the route, the wrong-way notice, arrival, answers, checks, directions. "Directions" covers every cue along the way: a direction when it becomes next, "Coming up", "now", checkpoints, closeness cues, landings, the ride-end line, the resume line, the line on return.
- **Urgent:** emergency, a closure on the route, the wrong-way notice (D3), arrival, answers (PRD §6.6: "only alerts, the emergency, arriving and answers interrupt").
- **Progress:** the shopper's distance in metres along the active route, from P0-06 r4 (the metres that "count down as the shopper walks", PRD §6.6).
- **Side:** left, right, ahead or behind, relative to the direction of travel along the route segment at that point (L113), never the screen.
- **Check:** a question on a card over the map with Yes and "No, I'm somewhere else" (PRD §6.6).

## 4. What P0-14 reads from other entries

P0-14 fixes no data structure of another entry. It needs, through their interfaces:

1. **From P0-06 r4 (FF-6), per direction of the active route:** its kind (turn left or right, slight or sharp; a ride up or down with its connector, its from and to levels and each level it passes with whether it stops there; entering or leaving a store; walking through a store; a bridge; going outside; arriving); its landmark and the landmark's side; the places passed on its approach; its approach metres; its position along the route in real metres; whether it is outdoors; the checkpoints along its approach (metres along the route, place, side). Also: the progress (§3), the heading of the route segment at a point, the off-route, browsing and arrival events, and the "Go this way" event of Find another way. §L of the decision sheet lists these as r4's to carry.
2. **From P0-08 (FF-6):** the confidence state and its changes (A8); opening "Where are you?"; its sheet's top results for the read-back (A13).
3. **From the package (P0-03), through the package reader:** each place's door position and level, each connector's stop positions, and the names and `SpokenName`s of occupants, levels and connectors (AC-45 of P0-03), for "you should see" lines.
4. **From P0-04:** the mall list's shopper settings and their last received values per venue (A6, P0-04 r3), the route sheet container, the map's rail and the map's own description (P0-04 r2 AC-43).
5. **From P0-05 (FF-5):** the microphone's listening start and stop (P0-05's search and P0-08's sheet alike), and the search's top results for the read-back (A13).

If P0-06 r4 or P0-08 settles a different shape, this spec's next revision follows it; the behaviour below does not change.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

### 5.1 The platform channels (native-ble; Android now, iOS at P1-05)

AC-1: Speech goes through Gabay's own channel `gabay/voice` with `speak` (text, language tag `en-PH` or `fil-PH`, utterance id, flush), `stop`, and a `done` callback from the platform for each utterance that finishes, is stopped or fails. No text-to-speech package is in `pubspec.yaml` (L99). Verify: unit (the Dart side against a fake channel); review (`pubspec.yaml`, the Kotlin side).

AC-2: Vibration goes through `gabay/haptics` with `buzz` (left, right, ride, alert) and `arrive`, with no package. Verify: unit (fake channel); manual (felt on a phone).

AC-3: The channel answers, on request, whether a text-to-speech engine exists and whether an `en-PH` and a `fil-PH` voice are installed. Verify: unit (fake channel); manual (the emulator and the product owner's phone).

AC-4: Before each item the Android side asks for transient audio focus that lets other audio duck (`AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK`, checked L117), and gives it up when the item ends. Music playing on the phone is lowered during a sentence and restored after. Verify: review (Kotlin); manual (music playing, a route with the voice on).

AC-5: Speech plays on the media stream at the phone's own speech rate; Gabay sets no rate (L112 ruling 3). It plays with the ringer on silent (L112 ruling 4). Verify: review; manual (ringer on silent).

AC-6: When the platform broadcasts `ACTION_AUDIO_BECOMING_NOISY` (earphones unplugged or Bluetooth audio disconnected, checked L117) during an active route with the voice on, the voice becomes muted (§3, D2) and stays muted until the shopper turns it on again from the rail or Me (DC-28, DC-29). Outside an active route the broadcast changes nothing (PRD §6.23 "during a route"). Verify: unit (a fake event, with and without a route); manual (unplug earphones mid-route).

AC-7: Where the channel is missing (an iOS build before P1-05, a test host), the app runs visual only: no error is shown, the queue keeps moving, buzzes are skipped. Verify: unit (missing-plugin path).

### 5.2 The queue (Blueprint §4.4: "a one-deep queue that never cuts a sentence off")

AC-8: While an item is playing, a new non-urgent item waits in the one slot and the playing item is never cut off by it. When the playing item ends, the waiting one plays. Verify: unit (fake channel and clock).

AC-9: The slot holds one item. A new item replaces the waiting one when its priority is equal or higher. A new item of lower priority than the waiting one is dropped (DC-2). Verify: unit.

AC-10: An urgent item stops a playing item of lower priority, clears the slot and plays at once. It never stops a playing item of higher priority; it waits in the slot instead (DC-1). Verify: unit (each pair of priorities, the wrong-way notice in D3's place included).

AC-11: A waiting "Coming up" item is dropped once it has waited longer than `voice.staleComingUpS` (6 s), and a waiting "now" item after `voice.staleNowS` (4 s) (Blueprint §4.4). Checkpoints and closeness cues use the limits of DC-3. A dropped cue is never said late. Verify: unit (fake clock, at the limit and just past it).

AC-12: When the platform never reports an item's end, a fallback timer frees the queue (DC-5). Verify: unit.

AC-13: The emergency priority plays with the voice off or muted and buzzes three times (L112 ruling 9; D2). Nothing in P0 raises it (the emergency button is hidden until P3-02, L117); it is proven with a fake source. Verify: unit.

AC-14: An answer (the spoken read-back of P0-05's search or P0-08's sheet, AC-75) plays with the voice off, and not when muted (L112 ruling 9; D2). Every other item except the emergency follows the switch and is silent when muted. Verify: unit (on, off, muted).

AC-15: Turning the voice off stops what is playing at once and clears the slot (DC-13). Verify: unit.

### 5.3 What is said, and when (Simple wording)

AC-16: When a direction becomes the next one, its sentence is said: the approach first, naming a landmark ("Walk straight towards Zen Gifts", "Walk past the ATM towards the escalator"), then the action ("then turn left", "then take it up to level 2"). Metres are said only when there is nothing to name ("Walk straight for about 120 m"). An approach under 15 m gets no clause of its own (DC-21). Verify: unit (sentence vectors from fixture directions).

AC-17: A direction within 25 m of the one before is said in the same sentence, joined by "then" (PRD §6.6). Verify: unit (at 25 m and just over).

AC-18: "Coming up: ⟨direction⟩" is said when the progress reaches `voice.comingUpM` (20 m) before a turn or ride, only when that direction was first said more than `voice.comingUpGapM` (40 m) before it. "⟨Turn left⟩ now" is said at `voice.nowM` (5 m) before it. Each is said once. Verify: unit (a fake progress stream, at the thresholds).

AC-19: Checkpoints follow the demo's rules as named constants (D10; Blueprint §4.4): only on an approach over 40 m; checkpoints at least 22 m apart; none within 25 m of the turn; each names a place passed ("You should be passing Lumi Salon on your left about now. Keep straight; next look for …"); where nothing is named, a line every 35 m says how far the target is. Each is said once. PRD §6.6's "every 25–40 m" is reworded to match in FF-7's L-row (G4). FF-8's guided checkpoints use the same rules (D10, E7). Verify: unit (an approach of 40 m and just over; a named stretch; an unnamed stretch; a place within 25 m of the turn).

AC-20: Near the destination: "Getting close now, about 50 m" at `voice.arriveFarM` (50 m, on an approach over 70 m), "Almost there" with the arrival clause at 20 m (DC-4), and "This should be it" at `voice.arriveNearM` (5 m), each once (PRD §6.6; Blueprint §4.4). Verify: unit.

AC-21: On stairs and escalators each landing is said ("Level 2. You should see Lumi Salon; keep going up."); an express escalator says only the levels it stops at; a lift says nothing between its ends (PRD §6.6). Verify: unit (a stairs ride over three levels, an express escalator, a lift).

AC-22: Stepping off a ride, the floor question is asked (AC-36). With the voice on, the same sentence is spoken, followed by the next direction. Verify: unit.

AC-23: Tapping the direction card says the current direction again, worked out for the current progress (L112 ruling 2; DC-24). It follows the switch. Verify: widget.

AC-24: During an active route the rail shows a speaker button. It is the same saved value as Me → Voice guidance: changing either changes both at once. Turning it on during a route, or turning it on again while muted (DC-28), says the direction ahead at once (L112 ruling 10). Verify: widget.

AC-25: Browsing the ways around (Find another way, P0-06 r4) says nothing. "Go this way" says the new first direction (PRD §6.6). Verify: unit (with a fake P0-06 r4 event).

AC-26: While the shopper is browsing in a store (P0-06 r4), no cue is said and no buzz is given. Walking back towards the route says "Back on the way to ⟨destination⟩" once (PRD §6.6). Verify: unit.

AC-27: With Technical wording, the voice says the direction card's short English line, and no checkpoints or closeness cues, as the demo does (`narration.dart`; L112 ruling 2 "everything the demo says"). Verify: unit.

AC-28: During "Preview the route" (P0-15) nothing is said (L118). In FF-7 this is proven with a fake preview state. Verify: unit.

### 5.4 Wording (L113, L112)

AC-29: Every phrase about where the shopper is or what they will see is suggestive, whatever the confidence state ("You should be on Level 2 now, with Jollibee on your left"). Every instruction is plain ("Turn left at Jollibee"). The same route gives the same suggestive clauses for a live-confident and a hand-set position. Verify: unit (sentences compared across states); manual (the product owner's line-by-line review of the phrase sheet, AC-61).

AC-30: Left and right are relative to the direction of travel along the route segment, with a landmark where there is one; never "up" or "down" the screen, and the same whether or not the map turns (L113 ruling 3), P0-04 r3's turning map included (A15). Ahead and behind follow DC-21. Verify: unit (one place, the map rotated to several angles, the side unchanged); widget (the turning map on and off, the same sentence).

AC-31: The voice says a name's `SpokenName` where the package has one (occupants, levels, connectors), else the name as written. The screen always shows the written name. Verify: unit.

AC-32: Shop names and level names are never translated: in Tagalog they are spoken and shown as the mall wrote them (PRD §6.23). Verify: unit (a name passes through every Tagalog template unchanged).

### 5.5 The checks

AC-33: A check opens as a card over the map and is never hidden in a swiped-down sheet. It is answered by tap only (L112 ruling 8; D9: answering by voice would be a new pipeline entry). One card shows at a time (DC-10). Verify: widget (the sheet swiped down, the card still shown).

AC-34: A full-width Pause / Resume sits under the direction card, and a pause square on the rail while the sheet is down (PRD §6.6; P0-14's, A14). When the position is not live-confident, Pause asks "Are you here? You should be on ⟨level⟩, with ⟨one landmark⟩ ⟨side⟩. If that's right, ⟨the one next thing to do⟩." It names one landmark and the one next action, never the directions after it. When live-confident, Pause only pauses. Verify: widget; unit (the sentence).

AC-35: When the position is not live-confident and reaches a lift, escalator or stairs on the route, the card asks "Are you at ⟨connector⟩? …" once per ride. When live-confident, there is no ride-foot check (L99 ruling 2). Verify: unit (each confidence state).

AC-36: After every ride, whatever the confidence state, the card asks "Are you on ⟨level⟩? You should see ⟨A⟩ on your ⟨side⟩ …" with Yes and "No, I'm somewhere else" (L113 ruling 2). With nothing within 15 m (DC-21), it asks only "Are you on ⟨level⟩?". It stays on the card while guidance continues and never blocks it (DC-11). Verify: widget; unit (live-confident, hand-set, guided).

AC-37: When the position is not live-confident and reaches the destination, the card says "You should be at ⟨place⟩ now. Tap I've arrived if you are." The arrival (P0-06 r4: confetti, "You have arrived") waits for that tap. When live-confident, arrival is detected by P0-06 r4 and "I've arrived" is still offered (L113 ruling 1). Verify: unit; widget.

AC-38: "No, I'm somewhere else" on any check opens "Where are you?" (P0-08) and gives the wrong-way notice (AC-45). Verify: widget.

AC-39: What "Yes" does to the position (D4): Yes on a ride-foot check moves a hand-set or approximate position to that connector's foot; Yes on the floor question moves it to the connector's exit on the new level, as the floor recovery check does (L106; Blueprint §4.3). Yes on a pause check keeps the position where it is. A live position (live-confident or live-unsure) is never moved by a Yes. A guided position follows P0-15 (E9). Verify: unit (each check × hand-set, approximate, live-confident, live-unsure).

AC-40: The checks read the confidence state from the `PositionSource` (P0-08, A8); a guided position counts as not live-confident (L118). A card already open stays when the state changes; new checks follow the new state (DC-12). Verify: unit (each state; a change while a card is open).

### 5.6 Vibration (PRD §6.6)

AC-41: With "Vibrate before each turn" on (the default), each turn and each ride gets its pattern once, at `voice.comingUpM` before it (DC-7): one short buzz for left, two for right, one long before a lift, escalator or stairs. A turn that becomes next already inside that distance buzzes at once. Pattern lengths are named constants (DC-6). Verify: unit (fake channel and progress); manual (felt).

AC-42: The wrong-way notice gives the alert pattern (three buzzes) (DC-9). The arrival event (P0-06 r4) gives the short double vibration through `arrive`; every buzz goes through P0-14's channel (A14). Verify: unit.

AC-43: With the setting off, turns, rides and the wrong-way notice give no buzz. The arrival and the emergency still buzz (DC-8). Verify: unit.

## 8. Design choices (not requirements; each with an alternative)

DC-1 to DC-27 were accepted by the product owner on 2026-10-12 (A7, L165). DC-28 to DC-30, new in r2, were accepted on 2026-10-12 (residual sheet R23).

- **DC-1 Urgent items of equal priority:** the newer interrupts the older (two answers: the newer question is the one asked). *Alternative:* the newer waits; better if answers were long and often overlapped.
- **DC-2 A lower item never replaces a higher waiting one;** it is dropped. *Alternative:* a second slot per priority; better if lower items were often lost behind checks.
- **DC-3 Stale limits for checkpoints and closeness cues:** checkpoints use `voice.staleComingUpS`, closeness cues `voice.staleNowS` (the demo gives them 6 s and 4 s). *Alternative:* their own settings; better if the field shows they need different values.
- **DC-4 "Almost there" at 20 m:** a named constant, the demo's value. *Alternative:* a `voice.*` setting; better if malls tune it.
- **DC-5 Fallback timer:** about 600 ms plus 70 ms per character, named constants (the demo's estimate). *Alternative:* no fallback; worse when an engine never reports an end.
- **DC-6 Pattern lengths:** named constants from the demo's behaviour (left 80 ms; right two of 60 ms; a ride 220 ms; alert three of 70 ms; arrival 90 ms and 160 ms). *Alternative:* settings; better if shoppers report they cannot tell them apart.
- **DC-7 The buzz comes at `voice.comingUpM`,** so the buzz and "Coming up" coincide (PRD §6.6 says "about 20 m"). *Alternative:* its own setting.
- **DC-8 Arrival and the emergency vibrate whatever the Vibrate setting;** the setting names turns only. *Alternative:* all buzzes follow it.
- **DC-9 The wrong-way notice uses the alert pattern.** PRD §6.6's browsing rule lists a vibration among the wrong-way notice's effects. *Alternative:* no buzz.
- **DC-10 One check card at a time;** a newer check replaces an unanswered older one. *Alternative:* a queue of cards; slower to clear.
- **DC-11 An unanswered floor question stays** until answered, replaced by a newer check, or the trip ends. *Alternative:* it clears at the next direction.
- **DC-12 An open card stays when the confidence state changes.** *Alternative:* close it at once; risks closing under the shopper's thumb.
- **DC-13 Turning the voice off stops at once.** *Alternative:* finish the sentence.
- **DC-14 A language change waits for the playing sentence to end.** *Alternative:* stop and repeat in the new language.
- **DC-15 With a screen reader on, answers go through it and the wrong-way clip still plays** (a sound, not a second voice). *Alternative:* the clip silent too.
- **DC-16 A screen reader switched off mid-route re-announces the direction ahead,** as turning the voice on does (L112 ruling 10). *Alternative:* wait for the next direction.
- **DC-17 Voice availability is checked at each route start and on return to the foreground.** *Alternative:* only when Me opens.
- **DC-18 No engine:** Me says so, and the switch stays. *Alternative:* hide the switch.
- **DC-19 Technical lines use the `en-PH` voice** in either language, since they are English. *Alternative:* the `fil-PH` voice reading English.
- **DC-20 An English-by-design list** in the test, reviewed with the Tagalog. *Alternative:* a separate ARB file for English-only keys; needs a second localisation class.
- **DC-21 "You should see" counts places and connectors within 15 m; ahead is within 25° of the heading, behind beyond 135°; an approach under 15 m gets no clause.** Named constants, the demo's values. *Alternative:* settings.
- **DC-22 The screen is kept on through Gabay's own channel** (native-ble), with no package, during a route only, released at trip end or when the app leaves the foreground (A17). *Alternative:* a wake-lock package with a pin and an EXCEPTIONS entry.
- **DC-23 An inconsistent `voice.*` pair falls back** to the last valid pair kept for the venue, else both defaults (A6). *Alternative:* the admin settings screen (R9) refuses it on save, and the phone trusts what it gets.
- **DC-24 A repeat is worked out for the current progress** (the metres updated). *Alternative:* replay the last sentence as said.
- **DC-25 "On screen" means visible:** the app lifecycle resumed or inactive; paused and hidden stop guidance. *Alternative:* resumed only, which silences split screen.
- **DC-26 An item that cannot get audio focus is dropped.** *Alternative:* retry once focus returns; risks a late cue.
- **DC-27 P0-14 stays `IN PROGRESS` until FF-9** proves the live-dot cases (plain wording, no ride-foot check, detected arrival) on a phone (confirmed by A16). *Alternative:* `DONE` after FF-7 with fakes, and FF-9 re-tests under P0-13.
- **DC-28 (new in r2; Accepted, R23) How muted shows:** while muted, the saved switch stays on; the rail speaker button shows a muted icon and Me → Voice guidance reads "Muted: earphones disconnected"; one tap on either clears muted and says the direction ahead (AC-24). This keeps D2's rule that answers speak when off but not when muted, which a muted state stored as "off" would break. *Alternative:* a separate "Unmute" button on the rail and in Me; better if shoppers confuse the muted icon with off.
- **DC-29 (new in r2; Accepted, R23) How long muted lasts:** it is saved on the phone and lasts until the shopper turns the voice on again, across trips and restarts (PRD §6.23: "the voice mutes until turned on again"). *Alternative:* muted ends with the trip, so the next trip speaks again; better if shoppers forget they were muted, at the cost of speaking aloud through the phone's speaker after earphones were removed.
- **DC-30 (new in r2; Accepted, R23) The voice-search answer in Me (B5):** one row per recognition language with a remembered answer, showing "Use Google/Apple: Yes" or "Type instead", with a change and a "Ask me again" that clears it. *Alternative:* one switch per language, always shown; simpler, but it would show a choice for a language the shopper has never used.

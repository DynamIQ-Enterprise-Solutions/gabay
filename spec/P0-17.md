## 0. How to read this revision

- **Stage tags.** Each criterion carries its sub-slice (DC-1):
  - **[OA-5a]** the engine: a pure library with no network and no model. Measure, tidy, calibrate, build elements, validate.
  - **[OA-5b]** the server: the port and its one adapter, the three routes, the guards, the cost, the failure behaviour, the audit entry.
  - **[OA-5c]** the editor: the crops, the grid, the notice, the proposal view, Add to floor.
  - **[every sub-slice]** cross-cutting items.
- **Terms.**
  - "The tenant" is `req.tenantCompanyID` and nothing else (rule 2). "The venue" is the `:venueId` in the path, read through `parseIdParam`.
  - "The draft" is `VenueMap.DraftJson`; "the stored draft" is the draft as the server last saved it.
  - "A proposal" is a set of walls, openings and units, with fresh ids, laid over one level. It lives only in the browser until it is added.
  - "The gridded copy" is the picture sent to the model (the request field `file`). "The clean copy" is the picture the server measures and never sends on (the field `plan`). "The crop" is the part of the stored backdrop image both copies show, as four fractions.
  - "The validator" is OA-1's `validateMap`; "the context" is what OA-2's `loadMapContext` reads for the tenant.
  - "Refused" is an HTTP 4xx or 5xx in the standard shape `{ error }`, with nothing written.
- **The editor's hooks, as P0-11 r3 §1 gives them.** The view model has one `replaceDocument(document, { oneUndoStep })` entry; the `review` flag, its filter and its highlight exist (AC-92 to AC-94); a level can hold a backdrop with a calibration (AC-99 to AC-107). The button that starts a conversion appears only when OA-5 ships and `ai.planConversion` is on. If the shipped hook differs, the plan stops and asks (spec and plan disagree).
- **The flow, in one paragraph.** The admin has a level with a calibrated backdrop. They save the draft, drag a rectangle over the part of the plan to read, and open the conversion dialog. The dialog shows the part's real size, the model, an upper bound on the cost, and a notice that the grid copy will be sent to Claude; the admin confirms the plan is a test plan. The browser builds the two copies and posts them. The server checks them, reads the level's calibration from the stored draft, measures the walls from the clean copy, asks the model for the rest, tidies the geometry, validates the result as part of the whole map, and returns a proposal with the cost. The editor draws it over the floor. **Add to floor** inserts it as one undo step, every element flagged `review`; **Discard** drops it.

## 3. Acceptance criteria

### 3.1 OA-5a: the engine (a pure library; no network, no model, no database)

The library reads picture bytes and numbers and returns data. It reads no environment value, no clock and no random source except through injected functions (the id source, AC-9). Tests build every picture in memory from code, with exact ground truth (DC-23).

- AC-1 [OA-5a] **Wall measurement (office §10.2).** From the clean copy and the real width of the crop in metres, it returns wall segments in picture space, as the midlines of pairs of long parallel ink lines:
  - ink is dark pixels (the red grid is not dark);
  - the picture is min-pooled to at most `MEASURE_PX_PER_M`, never upsampled, so a one-pixel line survives;
  - runs are scanned along 0° and 90° at full detail and in `WALL_ANGLED_STEP_DEG` steps at half detail, keeping ink runs of at least `WALL_RUN_MIN_M` and bridging gaps of at most `WALL_RUN_BRIDGE_M`;
  - same-direction runs `WALL_PAIR_GAP_MIN_M` to `WALL_PAIR_GAP_MAX_M` apart, overlapping at least `WALL_PAIR_OVERLAP_MIN_M`, make a wall, whose `t` is the gap;
  - adjacent-row runs cluster on any overlap; the angled pass tests the ungrown mask; collinear pieces merge along the group's mean direction.

  On an invented plan generated with known walls (axis-aligned, and one angled at 35° built from 34° and 36° pieces), every known wall is found as one segment whose ends and midline are within the test tolerance (DC-23). Verify: unit.
- AC-2 [OA-5a] **What it must not find.** On invented pictures, each yields no wall: a single one-pixel line; two lines closer than `WALL_PAIR_GAP_MIN_M` (a glass board); two lines farther apart than `WALL_PAIR_GAP_MAX_M` (a dimension line pair); short strokes (text-like marks); rows of text; a curved shape. Single-line partitions and glass are left to the model, as office §10.2 says. Verify: unit.
- AC-3 [OA-5a] **A picture that is not a readable plan.** When the measured walls total less than `READABLE_MIN_WALL_M` or fewer than `READABLE_MIN_SEGMENTS` segments, it returns `readable: false` and the walls and rooms of the model are used instead, with a note saying so (AC-27's `wallsFrom: 'model'`). A white picture, a photograph-like noise picture and a plan too coarse for its crop all return `readable: false` without error. Verify: unit.
- AC-4 [OA-5a] **Rooms from measured walls (office §10.2).** The measured walls are rasterised at `ROOM_CELL_M` and the enclosed spaces flood-filled; the space reachable from the picture's margin is the outside and is never a unit. Loose wall ends (no other wall within `LOOSE_END_CLEAR_M`) are bridged up to `LOOSE_END_BRIDGE_ROOM_M` for rooms and `LOOSE_END_BRIDGE_OUTSIDE_M` for the outside test, and nothing is closed wider than that. On invented plans: (a) two walls that both stop short of an outside corner (the L-gap) leave the building's rooms enclosed and not read as outside; (b) a 0.9 m corridor between two rooms is not erased; (c) a plan with two rooms and a gap-door gives two spaces. Verify: unit.
- AC-5 [OA-5a] **The model's rooms attach by share, not centroid (office §10.2).** Each space first takes the model's room (a "seed") that covers most of it; a leftover seed goes to an unclaimed space holding at least `SEED_MATCH_MIN_SHARE` of its outline and within `SEED_MATCH_MAX_SIZE_RATIO` times its size; otherwise it shares its best space. A space with no seed becomes no unit (nothing is invented; the notes count them). On an invented plan where a small room's centroid falls in the neighbouring space, the small room still gets its own name. Verify: unit.
- AC-6 [OA-5a] **Tidy-up of walls, in picture space first (office §10.4).** In this order:
  - walls shorter than `TIDY_DROP_WALL_M` are dropped;
  - a wall within `TIDY_STRAIGHTEN_DEG` of horizontal or vertical becomes exactly so, near lines are shared (weighted by length), and corners and T-junctions within `TIDY_JOIN_M` close by moving ends along their own line, so two walls share the exact corner point and nothing drifts;
  - diagonal walls join to the nearest end, else the nearest wall body, within `TIDY_JOIN_M`;
  - overlapping copies of one wall merge.

  Each rule has its own fixture. A fixture picture turned on the floor (rotated 90° and 35°) tidies the same walls in picture space. Verify: unit.
- AC-7 [OA-5a] **From the picture to level metres (office §10.4; OA-1 §2.5.9; P0-11 AC-101, AC-102).** A function maps a picture point to a venue-frame point through the level's backdrop calibration (`cx`, `cz`, `mpp`, `rot`, clockwise degrees, 0 facing up the plan) and the crop. Then everything is clipped to the level's rectangle: a wall or room partly outside is cut at the edge, one wholly outside is dropped and counted. Test vectors: the image centre maps to (`cx`, `cz`); a point 100 px right of centre at `mpp` 0.0083333 and `rot` 0 is 0.83333 m east; the same at `rot` 90 is 0.83333 m south; a crop that is not the whole image maps its own corner to the right place; a backdrop that hangs over the level edge is cut at the edge. The function is the only picture-to-metres code on the server. Verify: unit (vectors), and a manual check that a proposal lies over the same walls the editor draws (AC-46).
- AC-8 [OA-5a] **Doors (office §10.4).** A model door snaps to the nearest wall within `DOOR_SNAP_M`; a door with no wall that near is dropped and counted. Its width is clamped to `DOOR_WIDTH_MIN_M` to `DOOR_WIDTH_MAX_M`, 0.9 m (`DOOR_WIDTH_DEFAULT_M`) when absent, and never more than its wall's length (a wall shorter than `OPENING_WIDTH_MIN_M` takes no door). A collinear gap of `GAP_DOOR_MIN_M` to `GAP_DOOR_MAX_M` between two measured walls is a door at the gap's middle. Every door is `kind: door`; it never sets `entrance`, `clearW` or a `name` (those are the admin's). A door is listed in a unit's `doors` for every unit whose boundary holds the door's wall, taken from the room raster (no distance constant). Verify: unit.
- AC-9 [OA-5a] **Elements built to the document (OA-1 §2.5, DC-17).** Each proposal element has exactly the document's fields for its kind and carries `review: true` (L170 Q3):
  - wall: `id`, `a`, `b`, `t`, `kind` (`solid` for measured walls; `glass` or `partition` as the model says), `review`;
  - opening: `id`, `wall`, `at`, `w`, `kind: 'door'`, `review`;
  - unit: `id`, `name`, `category`, `poly`, `doors`, `review`, with `walkable` left for the validator to fill and `passThrough`, `parent`, `floor` and `name`-less units never produced.

  Names are trimmed single-line text clipped to the document's name cap; a category outside the document's unit category set becomes `room` and is counted in the notes (DC-8). A polygon is simplified to at most `MAX_POLY_POINTS` points at `ROOM_SIMPLIFY_M`. A room under `ROOM_MIN_AREA_M2` is dropped. Ids are `ai-` plus a short kind letter and a counter, matching `ELEMENT_KEY`, unique inside the proposal and against every id already in the draft; the id source is injected (DC-22). The proposal contains no store, amenity, transit point, anchor, connector, item, correction, backdrop or desk (DC-6). Verify: unit (each kind, each omitted field, the fallback category, the id uniqueness against a draft that already holds `ai-` ids).
- AC-10 [OA-5a] **No dangling references (office §10.4's real bug).** The tidy-up drops and merges walls after openings are built. After it, every opening names a wall that exists in the proposal, or is re-attached to the surviving wall it lay on, or is dropped and counted; no unit lists a dropped opening; no unit names a missing opening. Verify: unit (a door on a wall that merges; a door on a dropped short wall).
- AC-11 [OA-5a] **Validated before it is returned (office §10.4).** The engine builds the candidate document, the stored draft with the proposal's elements added to the chosen level, and validates it with the one validator and the tenant's context (OA-1 AC-29; never a copy). A candidate with any problem returns `{ ok: false, problems, total }` and no proposal. The candidate is also held to `MAX_MAP_BYTES`, and the level's caps count what is already on it plus the proposal. A draft that already holds a since-deactivated lookup code still validates (OA-1 AC-20; FEATURE_PIPELINE §5 item 15). Verify: unit (a valid case; an over-cap case; a proposal wall outside the level; a draft with an inactive code); integration (the context read from the real local database, inside a rolled-back transaction, L151).
- AC-12 [OA-5a] **Pure and deterministic.** The same inputs and the same injected id source give a byte-identical proposal. A test runs the library with the network, the clock and the environment stubbed to throw. Verify: unit.
- AC-13 [OA-5a] **Measured, not gated (A5; as OA-1 AC-33).** On invented plans at the largest size the clean copy allows (`PLAN_CLEAN_MAX_SIDE_PX`), the library's time and peak heap, and the longest time the API's event loop is blocked while it runs, are measured on this machine and pasted in the pull request. The API answers `/health` while a measurement runs, and the longest wait is recorded. No target is stated, so none is a gate (§7). Verify: integration (measured), by `npm run measure:plan-conversion` (DC-17).

## 4. Edge cases

Each is covered by the criterion named, or has a one-line reason.

**Offline or stale package**
- EC-1: The proposal is never in a package. A conversion never publishes; publish strips backdrops (OA-2 AC-26) and the flagged elements publish as warnings only (P0-03's review finding); the shopper app's package and any phone's copy are untouched (AC-18, AC-29).
- EC-2: The admin is offline, or the request fails in transit: the editor shows the error, nothing changed, the slot frees when the server sees the disconnect (AC-23, AC-41).
- EC-3: The stored draft moved on since the editor saved (another admin, another tab): 409 before anything is sent to the provider, and the editor's conflict stop applies (AC-18).
- EC-4: The stored original is gone (for example after `setup-db`): 409 "The floor-plan image is missing", before any provider call (AC-18; P0-11 EC-6).

**Empty and maximum data**
- EC-5: A level with no backdrop: no control, and the route answers 409 (AC-18, AC-32).
- EC-6: A blank, white or featureless crop: no measured walls; the model says `isFloorPlan` false, or returns nothing; 422 or an empty proposal with notes; the cost is shown (AC-3, AC-26).
- EC-7: A crop at the limits, the clean copy at `PLAN_CLEAN_MAX_SIDE_PX` and the gridded copy at `PLAN_FILE_MAX_SIDE_PX`: accepted; one pixel over: 400 (AC-16).
- EC-8: A mall-scale crop (hundreds of metres across) whose clean copy has far fewer pixels per metre than the office's 110: the walls may not be found; the library falls back to the model's walls and says so; the dialog warned (AC-3, AC-33; §8).
- EC-9: A proposal that would push a level past a cap, or the candidate past `MAX_MAP_BYTES`: 502 with the validator's problems, nothing offered (AC-11, AC-26).
- EC-10: An answer with the maximum number of walls and rooms the model can produce: arrays beyond the caps are a 502, never truncated; at the caps, accepted (AC-26).
- EC-11: A level at its cap already: the editor refuses Add to floor with the number over (AC-38).

**Tenant boundary**
- EC-12: Another tenant's venue answers 404 on all three routes; no image, draft or setting of it is read (AC-15).
- EC-13: The flag on for tenant A and off for tenant B: B's conversion is 403 (AC-14).
- EC-14: The image is read by the key in the stored draft, bound to the tenant and venue; a key of another tenant cannot be reached (AC-18).
- EC-15: The venue's tenant is `req.tenantCompanyID`, never the token's user object (rule 2; AC-15).

**Each role**
- EC-16: Under E-20 no role is checked on the server (`Waived: 3`, `Waived: 4`); the client hides the control by the role the switcher chose, per Q3 (a): MALL_ADMIN and SUPERADMIN only (AC-32, AC-44).
- EC-17: A SUPERADMIN with no tenant gets 400 on all three routes (AC-15).
- EC-18: A VIEWER and a VENUE_EDITOR never see the control; until R2 the server would still accept their request, which is the E-20 gap R2 closes (AC-32, AC-44).

**Permission denied**
- EC-19: An `X-Tenant-Id` the user does not hold answers 403 (AC-15). The flag off, the venue blocked, and the key missing each answer their own message, never a way round (AC-14, AC-15, AC-19).

**Concurrent edits**
- EC-20: Two conversions on one venue: the second is 409 while the first runs (AC-23).
- EC-21: An autosave, a restore or a clear while a conversion runs: the conversion only reads the stored draft at its start; the proposal is validated against the version it read; Add to floor then follows AC-39, and the next save follows OA-2's 409 rules (AC-18, AC-27, AC-39).
- EC-22: The admin moves or re-scales the backdrop, or deletes the level, while converting: Add to floor is refused (AC-39).
- EC-23: A second proposal while one is pending: replaced only by the admin's choice (AC-37).
- EC-24: The conversion and an unrelated save by the same admin: allowed; the save does not carry the proposal (AC-37).

**Publish in progress**
- EC-25: A conversion while a publish of the venue is `BUILDING` succeeds: it reads the draft and writes nothing, so the version being built cannot change (AC-18; OA-2 EC-24).
- EC-26: A proposal added after a publish is a draft change like any other; the published layout is frozen (invariant 3).

**Invalid input**
- EC-27: Missing or extra multipart parts, a repeated `file`, a `plan` that is not PNG, a `file` that is a PDF or renamed text, a body over the limit: 400, 413 or 415, never a 500 (AC-15, AC-16).
- EC-28: `crop` as text, `NaN`, reversed, out of 0 to 1, or with extra values; `level` missing, not a string, or unknown; `baseVersion` as `"3"`, `3.5`, `-1` or `null`; `attest` as `"yes"`: 400 naming the field (AC-15, AC-17, AC-18).
- EC-29: Pictures that are the right type but not copies of the plan, or of the wrong shape, or each off in a different direction: 400 (AC-17).
- EC-30: A PNG with a forged header, an oversized claimed size or a truncated stream: 400 with no large allocation (AC-16).
- EC-31: A model answer with `NaN`, strings for numbers, coordinates outside 0 to 1000, polygons with under three points, a self-crossing polygon, duplicate walls, `__proto__` keys, or a category outside the closed set: repaired as AC-6 to AC-9 say, or a 502; never a 500 and never a prototype change (AC-26).
- EC-32: A model name or note containing markup, control characters, a URL, or text that reads like an instruction to the model or the admin: kept as trimmed plain text only, shown as text, never used as a key, a path, a prompt or a link (AC-9, AC-37). The plan's own text is untrusted data; the schema and the validator are the only gate.
- EC-33: A photograph, a selfie or a document uploaded as a backdrop: it would be sent if attested; the model says `isFloorPlan` false and 422 follows. The pre-send checks cannot tell; see AC-19's residual risk.
- EC-34: A plan with several storeys on one sheet, a plan turned on the floor, a backdrop with `rot` not 0, or a backdrop that hangs over the level's edge: the crop is one part; the transform handles rotation; elements are cut at the level's edge (AC-6, AC-7).
- EC-35: A door at a wall's end, a door on a wall that merges, a room under 1 m², a unit with no name, a model room with no matching space: handled and counted (AC-5, AC-8, AC-10).

**Provider and server failures**
- EC-36: The key is wrong, revoked or out of credit: 503 "not set up", no detail, no retry (AC-26).
- EC-37: A rate limit, an overload, a time-out, a cut-off answer, a refusal: AC-26's statuses; the slot frees; the cost is reported where billed (AC-23, AC-28).
- EC-38: The server restarts mid-conversion: the browser's request fails (EC-2); the in-memory slot dies with the process, so a fresh process starts clean (DC-11).
- EC-39: The request outlives the Functions emulator's limit: step 0 measures it, and a shorter limit stops the slice for a job design (AC-31).
- EC-40: The provider adds a model or changes a price: the model list and prices are a constants entry with a date; a price older than the owner's choice shows in `pricedAsOf`; nothing sends an unlisted model (AC-22).
- EC-41: A second committed source, the penthouse, or an owner's spike export offered to the live script: the script has no path argument and reads only the one MEZZ path, so none of them can be given to it; in the editor, a Spike Venues venue stays off because its tenant is never turned on, and a `PENTHOUSE` venue is blocked by code wherever it is (AC-19, AC-46).

## 9. Design choices

Each is a DESIGN CHOICE, not a requirement, with an alternative that would also satisfy what is stated. They are listed for sign-off, not asked (L174).

- **DC-1 Three sub-slices (§1).** About 45 criteria is far past L174's "about 20 a stage", and the three pieces have three coders and three review surfaces. *Alternative:* one slice, which holds a pixel-measurement library, a paid network call and a canvas client in one review.
- **DC-2 The convert route is one request that stays open** (office §10.1: 2 to 3 minutes), not a job. *Alternative:* start the work and poll for it, which survives a short request limit but needs somewhere to keep the job (a table, so a migration and R13 changes) and work after the response, which a single-instance function may throttle (RECALLED). If step 0 finds the limit too short, this comes to the owner as a revision.
- **DC-3 The proposal is not stored on the server.** It lives in the browser until Add or Discard (AC-40 guards it). *Alternative:* keep it for a time so a lost tab does not lose a paid answer, which needs storage, an expiry and a tenant-bound read route.
- **DC-4 The penthouse exclusion is layers, not one rule** (AC-19), because the server cannot recognise a drawing. *Alternative:* an allow-list of venue codes that may convert (fail closed on the venue, not the tenant), which is stricter but means a setting edit for every new test venue.
- **DC-5 The browser builds both copies and the server verifies them** (office §10.1). *Alternative:* the server crops the stored original and draws the grid itself, which removes the aspect guard but needs JPEG and WebP decoders and text drawing that E-28 (PNG only, RECALLED) does not give.
- **DC-6 The proposal holds walls, doors and named units only.** Office-only fields (desks and the rest) are left out (re-plan §1.2); stores are relational rows (OA-1 AC-43) and are never created; amenities, connectors, items, corrections and anchors are not asked of the model (office §10 does not ask). *Alternative:* also ask for stairs and lifts, which is new design work with no office precedent.
- **DC-7 The model's room `category` is the document's own IMDF set**, read from `closed-sets.json` at call time, so there is no second list. *Alternative:* a handful of coarse types and a mapping table in code, which is a list the closed set already covers; it becomes the fallback if the provider refuses a 63-value enum.
- **DC-8 An unknown category becomes `room`** and is counted. *Alternative:* refuse the whole answer, which wastes a paid call on one bad label.
- **DC-9 The cost shown first is a ceiling** (a maximum, from named limits), not a typical figure. *Alternative:* a typical estimate from measured runs, which is better once AC-46 has data and can replace it by a revision.
- **DC-10 Cost in US dollars only, prices in a dated constants entry.** The provider charges in dollars; no exchange-rate source exists in the repository. *Alternative:* show pesos, which needs a rate source nobody has named; or prices as per-tenant settings, which is an option list a mall could mis-set.
- **DC-11 One conversion in flight per venue, counted in memory.** `maxInstances` is 1 (L98), and a database lock held for minutes would be wrong. *Alternative:* a row or an advisory lock, which is needed only once more than one instance runs.
- **DC-12 No automatic retry, one billed attempt.** *Alternative:* the SDK's default retries, which can bill twice for one click.
- **DC-13 CPU work in a worker thread** (or an equivalent) so the one instance keeps answering other requests while a plan is measured. *Alternative:* inline, with the blocking time measured by AC-13 and accepted or not by the owner.
- **DC-14 The route returns the elements, not the whole candidate document.** The candidate is built and validated on the server and thrown away. *Alternative:* return the candidate, up to `MAX_MAP_BYTES` per answer, and have the editor replace its document.
- **DC-15 Add to floor is refused when the backdrop's calibration or the level changed**, not re-projected. *Alternative:* keep the proposal's picture coordinates and re-project through the new calibration, which would need those coordinates in the answer and a second transform.
- **DC-16 The constants live in one new config file with their sources,** not in `venue-map-schema.json` (they are not the document's) and not in `closed-sets.json` (they are not closed sets). `ROOM_SIMPLIFY_M` and the other "set at step 0" values are not the office's and not invented here. *Alternative:* two files, one for limits and one for models.
- **DC-17 A new npm script, `measure:plan-conversion`,** for the engine's timing and the live run, taking no file path, like `measure:venue-map` (run by hand, not a verify check). *Alternative:* a test file skipped unless an environment value is set, with no new command.
- **DC-18 Three routes; the client reads its limits from the status route.** *Alternative:* add `ai.planConversion` to `PUBLIC_FLAGS` and copy the limits and the price table into Dart, which puts a second copy of every number in the client.
- **DC-19 Until R3, one structured log line per conversion carries the audit entry's fields** (no content). *Alternative:* nothing, as OA-2 does for saves; but a conversion spends money and sends data out, so a record is worth the line.
- **DC-20 The tenant setting turns the module on; a venue block list turns one venue off.** The flag is per tenant because a tenant is the contracting unit and settings already override per tenant. *Alternative:* a per-venue flag, which needs a column or a document field.
- **DC-21 The grid colour is a token** though it is image data, so the colour lint stays unbent. *Alternative:* an `ignore` for one line, which the lint's own rule discourages.
- **DC-22 Element ids are `ai-` plus a kind letter and a counter**, with an injected source, regenerated on collision at Add. *Alternative:* random ids, which cannot be tested byte for byte.
- **DC-23 Measurement tests use pictures the tests draw, with exact truth, and a tolerance named in the plan** (a small multiple of the measurement's cell size). The tolerance is a test constant, not a product target. *Alternative:* committed fixture images, which raise a question about their provenance that generated pictures do not.

- **DC-24 MEZZ is rendered by repository code from the committed source, at one fixed path, in memory** (Q2 (b)). The picture is a drawing of MEZZ's own rooms, doors and obstacles, so the source is also the ground truth for AC-46. *Alternative:* the owner supplies a MEZZ plan image, which would need a path argument and so open the live script to any file.
